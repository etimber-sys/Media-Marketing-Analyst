# Content Strategy Synthesis

## Core Thesis

Paramount+'s content strategy centers on leveraging a deep catalogue of owned IP — CBS network programming, Paramount Pictures films, Nickelodeon/MTV/BET originals, and the Star Trek franchise — while aggressively building new tentpole original franchises through Taylor Sheridan's neo-Western universe, all at a price point ($8.99–$13.99/mo) designed to undercut Netflix. Sports rights (NFL, UFC, UEFA Champions League) serve as the primary subscription acquisition lever for audiences that cannot be won by narrative content alone.

## The Taylor Sheridan Universe

Taylor Sheridan is Paramount+'s single most important original content asset. His output spans a universe of interconnected and standalone prestige dramas rooted in American frontier mythology — neo-Westerns, crime thrillers, espionage — that appeal to a broad, politically diverse US audience.

**Yellowstone** (Paramount Network, 2018–2024) was the anchor property. Though Yellowstone itself aired on the Paramount Network cable channel (not on Paramount+), it became the most-watched cable drama in the United States during its peak years. Season 4 drew 12.7 million premiere viewers; Season 5 drew 12.1 million. Kevin Costner won the 2023 Golden Globe for Best Actor in a Drama Series. The show's success was so unexpected that Paramount had sold US streaming rights to NBCUniversal's Peacock before the show broke out — a deal Paramount CEO Bob Bakish later called "unfortunate."

The spinoffs and follow-on series were all built for Paramount+ directly, capitalizing on Yellowstone's cable audience and funneling them into the subscription service:
- **1883** (Paramount+, 2021–2022): 10-episode Dutton family origin story; helped drive the December 2021 subscriber count to 32.8 million.
- **1923** (Paramount+, 2022–2025): Second Yellowstone prequel, two seasons; Harrison Ford and Helen Mirren in lead roles.
- **Mayor of Kingstown** (Paramount+, 2021–present): Prison-town crime drama; ongoing.
- **Tulsa King** (Paramount+, 2022–present): Mob comedy-drama with Sylvester Stallone; renewed through Season 4. A spinoff, Frisco King (starring Samuel L. Jackson), is in development.
- **Landman** (Paramount+, 2024–present): Oil industry drama with Billy Bob Thornton.
- **Lioness** (Paramount+, 2023–present): CIA espionage thriller.
- **The Madison** (Paramount+, 2026): Sheridan's biggest debut season launch with 8 million global views.

The strategic risk: Sheridan signed a five-year overall deal with NBCUniversal in October 2025, effective after his Paramount TV deal expires in 2028. Paramount+ has a limited window (through 2028) to consolidate the Sheridan franchise and build franchises that can outlast his departure.

## Sports as a Differentiator

Live sports is the second major pillar of Paramount+'s subscriber acquisition strategy, particularly for demographic segments (sports fans, cable cord-cutters) who may not engage with prestige drama.

**NFL on CBS** is the most valuable property. CBS has broadcast NFL games for decades, and Paramount+ holds streaming rights to those games through 2033. Sunday afternoon AFC games, playoff games, Super Bowls, and the AFC Championship Game all stream on Paramount+. NFL drives the single largest subscriber spikes of any content type — Star Trek: Discovery's premiere in 2017 drove the largest single-day increase in CBS All Access history, but NFL games drove comparable weekend-level growth.

**UEFA Champions League and Europa League** (exclusive US streaming rights through 2030) serve a different audience — soccer fans, particularly international-leaning urban demographics — complementing the NFL's heartland audience.

**UFC** (beginning 2026, $7.7 billion 7-year deal through 2033): Paramount+ became the exclusive US streamer of all UFC events in 2026, with select events on CBS. This is one of the largest sports rights deals in streaming history and signals Paramount's intent to use combat sports as a major subscriber driver. UFC 328: Chimaev vs. Strickland was promoted prominently on the Paramount+ homepage as of the May 2026 scrape date.

**Rugby World Cup** (through 2029), **NCAA March Madness**, **The Masters and PGA Championship**, and international soccer (NWSL, Premier League in Mexico/Central America, Copa Libertadores in Brazil) round out a diverse sports portfolio.

Sports rights are expensive but create a different kind of engagement than on-demand drama: live viewing, appointment TV, and low churn (subscribers maintain the service through sports seasons even if they are not consuming drama content).

## IP-Driven Strategy

Unlike Netflix, which built its library primarily by licensing third-party content and commissioning expensive standalone originals, Paramount+ follows an IP-leverage strategy: use existing owned brands to produce lower-risk extensions and revivals.

**Star Trek** is the clearest example. The franchise was dormant on television from 2005 (end of Enterprise) until 2017 when Star Trek: Discovery launched on CBS All Access. Discovery's premiere produced the largest single-day subscriber increase in the platform's history at the time. Since then, multiple series have been produced under the Trek umbrella: Picard, Lower Decks (animated), Strange New Worlds, Prodigy. Each new season functions as a marketing moment for the subscription service with an established, loyal fan base.

**NCIS franchise** (NCIS: Tony & Ziva being a current example): CBS's most-watched procedural franchise generates spinoffs that convert CBS broadcast viewers into Paramount+ subscribers.

**Nickelodeon IP**: SpongeBob SquarePants, PAW Patrol, Avatar: The Last Airbender, The Loud House. Family and children's content reduces churn among household subscribers (once a family subscribes for kids' content, they are less likely to cancel month-to-month). The SpongeBob Movie: Sponge on the Run and Kamp Koral spinoff were early Paramount+ launch titles in March 2021.

**South Park** ($1.5B deal, 2025): Brings the long-running Comedy Central franchise exclusively to Paramount+ after a bidding war that included Netflix and HBO Max. South Park movies had been produced for Paramount+ since 2021 under an earlier deal.

**Dexter revival** (Dexter: Resurrection): Leverages the original Showtime IP for a prestige drama that draws both SHOWTIME subscribers and CBS/general audiences. Season 2 was in production as of April 2026.

**The Offer** (making of The Godfather) and other Paramount Pictures-adjacent projects: Monetizes the film studio's heritage IP on the streaming service.

IP-driven strategy reduces the risk per project (established audiences, known franchise equity) but requires ongoing investment in franchise quality to avoid diminishing returns.

## Free Tier: Pluto TV

Pluto TV is the AVOD (ad-supported video on demand) / FAST (free ad-supported streaming television) complement to Paramount+. Acquired by Viacom for $340 million in January 2019, Pluto TV has grown to 80 million monthly active users as of April 2023 — larger than Paramount+'s 79 million paid subscribers.

Pluto operates as a free linear-channel service (emulating traditional TV with commercial breaks) plus an on-demand library. Since the Viacom acquisition, it carries branded channels from Nickelodeon, MTV, Comedy Central, BET, Paramount Pictures, and other Viacom/Paramount properties.

Strategically, Pluto TV serves multiple functions:
1. **Funnel to paid subscription**: Pluto is used to upsell viewers to Paramount+ by exposing them to Paramount content for free.
2. **Reach beyond subscribers**: The free tier captures viewers who are unwilling to pay for a subscription, generating ad revenue.
3. **Broader market coverage**: Together, Pluto TV (free) and Paramount+ (paid) give Paramount Skydance a two-tier streaming stack that mirrors the traditional broadcast/premium cable model.

CEO David Ellison indicated in August 2025 that a "soft merger" of Pluto TV and Paramount+ — unifying technical infrastructure but keeping separate front-end identities — is planned for completion by late 2026.

## SHOWTIME Integration

The integration of SHOWTIME into Paramount+ is both a content strategy and a consolidation strategy. Showtime Anytime was a separate DTC streaming service before being bundled with Paramount+ in September 2021. The full integration came on June 27, 2023, when Showtime's DTC service was merged into the Paramount+ ad-free Premium tier (now called "Paramount+ with SHOWTIME" at $13.99/mo).

This move:
1. Added a premium content layer (Yellowjackets, The Chi, Dexter franchise, Cold Water, The Curse) to attract subscribers who wanted more than CBS/Nickelodeon
2. Discontinued the Showtime Anytime standalone app (December 2023), consolidating all premium subscribers onto Paramount+
3. Bundled SHOWTIME's prestige drama reputation with Paramount's mass-market sports and franchise content

The $13.99 Premium tier positions Paramount+ against HBO Max's premium tier while undercutting Netflix's pricing.

## Relevance to This Analysis

The TMDB dataset covers movies and TV shows scored by popularity (vote count proxy for reach) and ratings (vote average proxy for quality perception). Paramount+'s content strategy provides direct hypotheses to test against this data:

- **Genre bet**: Paramount+ invests heavily in drama (neo-Western, crime, prestige TV) and sci-fi (Star Trek). Do these genres consistently score high popularity and ratings in the broader market, or does Paramount+ occupy a niche?
- **TV originals vs. movies**: Paramount+ is primarily a TV original-driven service (Sheridan universe, Star Trek) using Paramount Pictures movies as a secondary draw. Does TV content outperform movies on popularity and ratings in the TMDB data? Or vice versa?
- **Franchise IP vs. originals**: Paramount+'s IP-leverage strategy (Star Trek revivals, Dexter revival, NCIS spinoffs) mirrors the broader streaming industry preference for known IP over risky original content. If high-IP content correlates with higher TMDB ratings/popularity, that validates the strategy.
- **Recency**: Paramount+ targets both new releases (Sheridan originals) and catalog depth (CBS library, Paramount Pictures). Analyzing whether newer content outperforms catalog — or whether classic IP holds its own — has direct implications for content acquisition strategy.
- **Vote count as reach proxy**: Titles with high vote counts in TMDB are those that drove broad audience engagement. If Paramount+'s content bets (drama/sci-fi, franchise TV) cluster in the high-vote-count high-rating quadrant, the platform's strategy is validated by market data.
