**CBS Broadcasting Inc.**, commonly shortened to **CBS** (an abbreviation of its original name, the **Columbia Broadcasting System**), is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_the_United_States "Television in the United States") [commercial broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_broadcasting "Commercial broadcasting") [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_broadcaster "Television broadcaster") and [radio network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_network "Radio network") and the [flagship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship_(broadcasting) "Flagship (broadcasting)") property of the [CBS Entertainment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_assets_owned_by_Paramount_Skydance#CBS_Entertainment_Group "List of assets owned by Paramount Skydance") division of [Paramount Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Skydance "Paramount Skydance"). It is one of Paramount Skydance's three flagship subsidiaries, along with [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") and [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV").

Founded in 1927, headquartered at the [CBS Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Building "CBS Building") in New York City and being part of the " [Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_(American_television) "Big Three (American television)")" television networks, CBS has major production facilities and operations at the [CBS Broadcast Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Broadcast_Center "CBS Broadcast Center") and [One Astor Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Astor_Plaza "One Astor Plaza") in New York City, and [Television City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_City "Television City") and the [CBS Studio Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Studio_Center "CBS Studio Center") in Los Angeles. It is sometimes referred to as the **Eye Network**, after the company's trademark symbol of an eye, which has been in use since October 20, 1951,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-1) and also the **Tiffany Network**, which alludes to the perceived high quality of its programming during the tenure of [William S. Paley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Paley "William S. Paley") (and can also refer to some of CBS's first demonstrations of [color television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_television "Color television"), which were held in the former [Tiffany and Company Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_and_Company_Building "Tiffany and Company Building") in New York City in 1950).[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-3)

## History

Main article: [History of CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_CBS "History of CBS")

The network has its origins in **United Independent Broadcasters, Inc.**, a radio network founded in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") by New York City [talent agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talent_agent "Talent agent") [Arthur Judson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Judson "Arthur Judson") on January 27, 1927. In April of that year, the Columbia Phonograph Company, parent of [Columbia Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records")' record label, invested in the network, resulting in its rebranding as the **Columbia Phonographic Broadcasting System** ( **CPBS**).[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-bartow-4) In early 1928, Judson and Columbia sold the network to Isaac and Leon Levy, two brothers who owned [WCAU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WPHT "WPHT"), the network's Philadelphia affiliate, as well as their partner Jerome Louchheim. They installed William S. Paley, an in-law of the Levys, as president of the network. With the Columbia record label out of ownership, Paley rebranded the network as the **Columbia Broadcasting System**.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-5)

By September 1928, Paley became the network's majority owner with 51 percent of the business.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-berg-6) [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") then acquired the other 49 percent of CBS in 1929, but the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression "Great Depression") eventually forced the studio to sell its shares back to the network in 1932.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-bartow-4) CBS would then remain primarily an independent company throughout the next 63 years. Under Paley's guidance, CBS would first become one of the largest radio networks in the United States and eventually one of the Big Three American broadcast television networks. CBS ventured and expanded its horizons through television starting in the 1940s, spinning off its broadcast syndication division [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932005) "Viacom (1952–2005)") to a separate company in 1971. In 1974, CBS dropped its original full name and became known simply as **CBS, Inc.**

The company was listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange "New York Stock Exchange") under the ticker symbol "CBS". The [Westinghouse Electric Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Electric_Corporation "Westinghouse Electric Corporation") acquired the network in 1994, renaming its legal name to the current CBS Broadcasting Inc. two years later, and in 1997 adopted the name of the company it had acquired to become [CBS Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation_(1997%E2%80%932000) "CBS Corporation (1997–2000)"). In 1999, CBS came under the control of [the original incarnation of Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932006) "Viacom (1952–2006)"), which was formed as a spin-off of CBS in 1971. In 2005, Viacom split itself into two separate companies and re-established [CBS Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation_(2005-2019) "CBS Corporation (2005-2019)") through the [spin-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off "Corporate spin-off") of its broadcast television, radio and select [cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television "Cable television") and non-broadcasting assets, with the CBS network at its core.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-9) CBS Corporation was controlled by [Sumner Redstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumner_Redstone "Sumner Redstone") through [National Amusements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Amusements "National Amusements"), which also controlled [the second incarnation of Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(2005%E2%80%932019) "Viacom (2005–2019)") until December 4, 2019, when the two separated companies [agreed to re-merge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_merger_of_CBS_and_Viacom "2019 merger of CBS and Viacom") to become ViacomCBS (later Paramount Global and now known as Paramount Skydance Corporation). Following the sale, CBS and its other broadcasting and entertainment assets were reorganized into a new division, CBS Entertainment Group.

CBS operated the [CBS Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Radio "CBS Radio") network until 2017 when it sold its radio division to Entercom (now known as [Audacy, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audacy,_Inc. "Audacy, Inc.") since 2021).[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-10) Before this, CBS Radio mainly provided news and feature content for its portfolio of [owned-and-operated radio stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owned-and-operated_station "Owned-and-operated station") in large and mid-sized markets, as well as its [affiliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_affiliate "Network affiliate") radio stations in various other markets. While CBS Corporation common shareholders (i.e. not the multiple-voting shares held by National Amusements) were given a 72% stake in the combined Entercom,[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-11) CBS no longer owns or operates any radio stations directly; however, it still provides radio news broadcasts to its radio affiliates and the new owners of its former radio stations, and licenses the rights to use CBS trademarks under a long-term contract. The television network has over 240 owned-and-operated (O&O) and affiliated television stations throughout the United States, some also available in Canada via pay-television providers or [border areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border "Canada–United States border") over-the-air.

## Programming

Main articles: [List of programs broadcast by CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_programs_broadcast_by_CBS "List of programs broadcast by CBS"), [List of programs previously broadcast by CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_programs_previously_broadcast_by_CBS "List of programs previously broadcast by CBS"), [CBS News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News "CBS News"), and [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports "CBS Sports")

As of 2026[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=CBS&action=edit), CBS provides 87+1⁄2 hours of regularly scheduled network programming each week. The network provides 22 hours of primetime programming to affiliated stations Monday through Saturday from 8:00p.m. to 11:00p.m. and Sunday from 7:00p.m. to 11:00p.m. Eastern and Pacific time (7:00p.m. to 10:00p.m. Monday through Saturday and 6:00p.m. to 10:00p.m. on Sunday in Central/Mountain time).

The network also provides daytime programming from 11:00a.m. to 4:00p.m. Eastern and Pacific weekdays (subtract 1 hour for all other time zones), including a half-hour break for local news and features game shows _[The Price Is Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Price_Is_Right "The Price Is Right")_ and _[Let's Make a Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Make_a_Deal "Let's Make a Deal") and_ [soap operas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_opera "Soap opera") _[The Young and the Restless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Young_and_the_Restless "The Young and the Restless"),_ _[The Bold and the Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bold_and_the_Beautiful "The Bold and the Beautiful")_, and _[Beyond the Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_the_Gates_(TV_series) "Beyond the Gates (TV series)")._

[CBS News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News "CBS News") programming includes _[CBS Mornings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Mornings "CBS Mornings")_ from 7:00a.m. to 9:00a.m. weekdays and _[CBS Saturday Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Saturday_Morning "CBS Saturday Morning")_ in the same period on Saturdays; nightly editions of _[CBS Evening News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Evening_News "CBS Evening News")_; the Sunday political talk show _[Face the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Nation "Face the Nation")_; early morning news program _[CBS News Mornings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_Mornings "CBS News Mornings")_; and the newsmagazines _[60 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Minutes "60 Minutes")_, _[CBS News Sunday Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_Sunday_Morning "CBS News Sunday Morning")_, and _[48 Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48_Hours_(TV_series) "48 Hours (TV series)")_. On weeknights, CBS airs the talk show _[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")_ and reruns of [Comics Unleashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comics_Unleashed "Comics Unleashed") until May 21, 2026, after that, reruns of Comics Unleashed and [Funny You Should Ask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funny_You_Should_Ask_(2017_game_show) "Funny You Should Ask (2017 game show)") will air in the 11:35 and 12:37 am ET/PT slots respectively.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-12)

[CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports "CBS Sports") programming is also provided most weekend afternoons. Due to the unpredictable length of sporting events, CBS occasionally delays scheduled primetime programs to allow the programs to air in their entirety, a practice most commonly seen with the [NFL on CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_CBS "NFL on CBS"). In addition to rights to sports events from major sports organizations such as the [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League"), [PGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Golfers'_Association_of_America "Professional Golfers' Association of America"), and [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association "National Collegiate Athletic Association"), CBS broadcasts the _[CBS Sports Spectacular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports_Spectacular "CBS Sports Spectacular")_, a sports [anthology series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series "Anthology series") that fills certain weekend afternoon time slots before (or in some cases, in place of) a major sporting event.

### Daytime

Main article: [CBS Daytime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Daytime "CBS Daytime")

CBS's daytime schedule is the longest among the major networks at 4+1⁄2 hours. It is the home of the long-running game show _[The Price Is Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Price_Is_Right "The Price Is Right")_, which began production in 1972 and is the longest continuously running daytime game show on network television. After being hosted by [Bob Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Barker "Bob Barker") for 35 years, the show has been hosted since 2007 by actor and comedian [Drew Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Carey "Drew Carey"). The network is also home to the current incarnation of _[Let's Make a Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Make_a_Deal "Let's Make a Deal")_, hosted by singer and comedian [Wayne Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Brady "Wayne Brady").

CBS is the only commercial broadcast network that continues to broadcast daytime game shows. Notable game shows that once aired as part of the network's daytime lineup include _[Match Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_Game "Match Game")_, _[Tattletales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattletales "Tattletales")_, _[The $10/25,000 Pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_(franchise) "Pyramid (franchise)")_, _[Press Your Luck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Your_Luck "Press Your Luck")_, _[Card Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Card_Sharks "Card Sharks")_, _[Family Feud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Feud "Family Feud")_, and _[Wheel of Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Fortune_(American_game_show) "Wheel of Fortune (American game show)")_. Past game shows that have had both daytime and prime time runs on the network include _[Beat the Clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_the_Clock "Beat the Clock")_ and _[To Tell the Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Tell_the_Truth "To Tell the Truth")_. Two long-running primetime-only games were the panel shows _[What's My Line?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What's_My_Line%3F "What's My Line?")_ and _[I've Got a Secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I've_Got_a_Secret "I've Got a Secret")_.

The network was also home to _[The Talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Talk_(talk_show) "The Talk (talk show)")_, a panel talk show similar in format to ABC's _[The View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_View_(talk_show) "The View (talk show)")_. It debuted in October 2010. The panel featured [Sheryl Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheryl_Underwood "Sheryl Underwood"), [Amanda Kloots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Kloots "Amanda Kloots"), [Jerry O'Connell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_O'Connell "Jerry O'Connell"), [Akbar Gbajabiamila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Gbajabiamila "Akbar Gbajabiamila"), and [Natalie Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Morales_(journalist) "Natalie Morales (journalist)") who served as moderator. _The Talk_ officially ended its run on December 20, 2024.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-13)

CBS Daytime airs three daytime soap operas each weekday: the hour-long series _[The Young and the Restless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Young_and_the_Restless "The Young and the Restless")_, which debuted in 1973, and the half-hour series _[The Bold and the Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bold_and_the_Beautiful "The Bold and the Beautiful")_, which debuted in 1987 and hour-long series _[Beyond the Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_the_Gates_(TV_series) "Beyond the Gates (TV series)")_ which debuted in 2025. CBS has long aired the most soap operas out of the [Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_(American_television) "Big Three (American television)") networks, carrying 3+1⁄2 hours of soaps on its daytime lineup from 1977 to 2009, and still retains the longest daily schedule. Other than _[Guiding Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiding_Light "Guiding Light")_, notable daytime soap operas that once aired on CBS include _[As the World Turns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_the_World_Turns "As the World Turns")_, _[Love of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_of_Life "Love of Life")_, _[Search for Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_for_Tomorrow "Search for Tomorrow")_, _[The Secret Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_Storm "The Secret Storm")_, _[The Edge of Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Edge_of_Night "The Edge of Night")_, and _[Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_(TV_series) "Capitol (TV series)")_.

### Children's programming

Main article: [Children's programming on CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_programming_on_CBS "Children's programming on CBS")

CBS broadcast the live-action series _[Captain Kangaroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Kangaroo "Captain Kangaroo")_ on weekday mornings from 1955 to 1982, and on Saturdays until 1984. From 1971 to 1986, CBS News produced a series of one-minute segments titled _[In the News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_News "In the News")_, which aired between other Saturday morning programs. Otherwise, CBS's children's programming has mostly focused on animated series such as reruns of _[Mighty Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mighty_Mouse "Mighty Mouse")_, _[Looney Tunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looney_Tunes "Looney Tunes")_, and _[Tom and Jerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_and_Jerry "Tom and Jerry")_ cartoons, as well as _[Scooby-Doo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooby-Doo "Scooby-Doo")_, _[Fat Albert and the Cosby Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_Albert_and_the_Cosby_Kids "Fat Albert and the Cosby Kids")_, _[Jim Henson's Muppet Babies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Henson's_Muppet_Babies "Jim Henson's Muppet Babies")_, _[Garfield and Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfield_and_Friends "Garfield and Friends")_, and _[Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles_(1987_TV_series) "Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles (1987 TV series)")_. In 1997, CBS premiered _[Wheel 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_2000 "Wheel 2000")_, a children's version of the syndicated game show _[Wheel of Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_of_Fortune_(American_game_show) "Wheel of Fortune (American game show)")_ which aired simultaneously on the [Game Show Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Show_Network "Game Show Network").

In September 1998, CBS began contracting the time out to other companies to provide programming and material for its Saturday morning schedule. The first of these outsourced blocks was the _[CBS Kidshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Kidshow "CBS Kidshow")_, which ran until 2000 and featured programming from Canadian studio [Nelvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelvana "Nelvana")[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-Nick_Jr._on_CBS-14) such as _[Anatole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatole_(TV_series) "Anatole (TV series)")_, _[Mythic Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythic_Warriors "Mythic Warriors")_, _[Rescue Heroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_Heroes "Rescue Heroes")_, and _[Flying Rhino Junior High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_Rhino_Junior_High "Flying Rhino Junior High")_.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-15)

After its agreement with Nelvana ended, the network then entered into a deal with [Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon "Nickelodeon") to air programming from its [Nick Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Jr._(block) "Nick Jr. (block)") block beginning in September 2000, under the banner [Nick Jr. on CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_on_CBS "Nickelodeon on CBS").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-Nick_Jr._on_CBS-14) By the time of the deal, Nickelodeon and CBS were corporate sisters through the latter's then parent company Viacom as a result of its 2000 merger with CBS Corporation. From 2002 to 2005, live-action and animated Nickelodeon series aimed at older children also aired as part of the block under the name [Nick on CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_on_CBS "Nickelodeon on CBS").

Following the Viacom-CBS split, the network decided to discontinue the Nickelodeon content deal. In March 2006, CBS entered into a three-year agreement with [DIC Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DIC_Entertainment "DIC Entertainment"), which was acquired later that year by the [Cookie Jar Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Jar_Group "Cookie Jar Group"), to program the Saturday morning time slot as part of a deal that included distribution of select [tape-delayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_delay "Broadcast delay") [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One "Formula One") auto races.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-16)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-19) The _KOL Secret Slumber Party on CBS_ replaced _Nick Jr. on CBS_ that September, with the inaugural lineup featuring two new first-run live-action programs, one animated series that originally aired in syndication in 2005, and three shows produced before 2006. In mid-2007, KOL, the children's service of [AOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOL "AOL"), withdrew sponsorship from CBS's Saturday morning block, which was subsequently renamed KEWLopolis. Complementing CBS's 2007 lineup were _[Care Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Care_Bears "Care Bears")_, _[Strawberry Shortcake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry_Shortcake "Strawberry Shortcake")_, and _[Sushi Pack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sushi_Pack "Sushi Pack")_. On February 24, 2009, it was announced that CBS would renew its contract with Cookie Jar for another three seasons through 2012.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-21) On September 19, 2009, KEWLopolis was renamed [Cookie Jar TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Jar_TV "Cookie Jar TV").[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-22)

On July 24, 2013, CBS agreed with [Litton Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litton_Entertainment "Litton Entertainment"), which already programmed a syndicated Saturday morning block exclusive to ABC stations and later produced a [block for CBS's sister network The CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Magnificent_Morning "One Magnificent Morning") that received its debut the following year, to launch a new Saturday morning block featuring live-action reality-based lifestyle, wildlife, and sports series. The Litton-produced [CBS Dream Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_WKND "CBS WKND") block, aimed at teenagers 13 to 16 years old, began broadcasting on September 28, 2013, replacing Cookie Jar TV.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-23) The block was renamed CBS WKND in 2023.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-24)

### Specials

#### Animated primetime holiday specials

CBS was the original broadcast network home of the [animated primetime specials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanuts_filmography#Specials "Peanuts filmography") based on the _[Peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanuts "Peanuts")_ comic strip, beginning with _[A Charlie Brown Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Charlie_Brown_Christmas "A Charlie Brown Christmas")_ in 1965. Over 30 Peanuts specials (many for a specific holiday such as [Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween "Halloween")) were broadcast on CBS until 2000 when the broadcast rights were acquired by ABC. CBS also aired several primetime animated specials based on the works of [Dr. Seuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Seuss "Dr. Seuss") (Theodor Geisel), beginning with _[How the Grinch Stole Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_the_Grinch_Stole_Christmas!_(TV_special) "How the Grinch Stole Christmas! (TV special)")_ in 1966, as well as several specials based on the _[Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfield "Garfield")_ comic strip during the 1980s (which led to Garfield getting his [Saturday-morning cartoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday-morning_cartoon "Saturday-morning cartoon") on the network, _Garfield and Friends_, which ran from 1988 to 1995). _[Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolph_the_Red-Nosed_Reindeer_(TV_special) "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (TV special)")_, produced in [stop motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion "Stop motion") by [Rankin/Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rankin/Bass "Rankin/Bass"), has been another annual holiday staple of CBS; however, that special first aired on NBC in 1964. As of 2011[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=CBS&action=edit), _Rudolph_ and _[Frosty the Snowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frosty_the_Snowman_(film) "Frosty the Snowman (film)")_ was the only two pre-1990 animated specials remaining on CBS; the broadcast rights to the _Charlie Brown_ specials are now held by Apple,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-25) _The Grinch_ rights by NBC,[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-27) and the rights to the _Garfield_ specials by [Boomerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomerang_(TV_network) "Boomerang (TV network)").[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-28)\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

All of these animated specials, from 1973 to 1990, began with a fondly remembered seven-second animated opening sequence, in which the words "A CBS Special Presentation" were displayed in colorful lettering (the [ITC Avant Garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITC_Avant_Garde "ITC Avant Garde") typeface, widely used in the 1970s, was used for the title logo). The word "SPECIAL", in [all caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_caps "All caps") and repeated multiple times in multiple colors, slowly zoomed out from the frame in a spinning counterclockwise motion against a black background, and rapidly zoomed back into frame as a single word, in white, at the end; the sequence was accompanied by a jazzy though majestic up-tempo fanfare with dramatic horns and percussion (which was edited incidental music from the CBS crime drama _[Hawaii Five-O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Five-O_(1968_TV_series) "Hawaii Five-O (1968 TV series)")_, titled "Call to Danger" on the [Capitol Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records "Capitol Records") soundtrack LP). This opening sequence appeared immediately before all CBS specials of the period (such as the [Miss USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_USA "Miss USA") pageants and the annual presentation of the [Kennedy Center Honors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honors "Kennedy Center Honors")), in addition to animated specials.

#### Classical music specials

CBS was also responsible for airing the series of _[Young People's Concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_People's_Concerts "Young People's Concerts")_, conducted by [Leonard Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Bernstein "Leonard Bernstein"). Telecast every few months between 1958 and 1972, first in black-and-white and then in color beginning in 1966, these programs introduced millions of children to [classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music") through the eloquent commentaries of Bernstein. The specials were nominated for several [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award"), including two wins in 1961 and later in 1966,[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-29) and were among the first programs ever broadcast from the [Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts "Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts").

Over the years, CBS has broadcast three different productions of Tchaikovsky's ballet _[The Nutcracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nutcracker "The Nutcracker")_ – two live telecasts of the George Balanchine [New York City Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Ballet "New York City Ballet") production in 1957[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-30) and 1958 respectively,[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-31) a little-known German-American filmed production in 1965 (which was subsequently repeated three times and starred [Edward Villella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Villella "Edward Villella"), [Patricia McBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_McBride "Patricia McBride") and [Melissa Hayden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Hayden_(actress) "Melissa Hayden (actress)")), and beginning in 1977, the [Mikhail Baryshnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Baryshnikov "Mikhail Baryshnikov") staging of the ballet, starring the Russian dancer along with [Gelsey Kirkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelsey_Kirkland "Gelsey Kirkland") – a version that would become a television classic, and remains so today (the broadcast of this production later moved to PBS).\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

In April 1986, CBS presented a slightly abbreviated version of _Horowitz in Moscow_, a live piano recital by pianist [Vladimir Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Horowitz "Vladimir Horowitz"), which marked his return to Russia after over 60 years. The recital was televised as an episode of _CBS News Sunday Morning_ (televised at 9:00a.m. Eastern Time in the U.S., as the recital was performed simultaneously at 4:00p.m. in Russia). It was so successful that CBS repeated it a mere two months later by popular demand, this time on videotape, rather than live. In later years, the program was shown as a standalone special on [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS "PBS"); the current DVD of the telecast omits the commentary by [Charles Kuralt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Kuralt "Charles Kuralt") but includes additional selections not heard on the CBS telecast.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-32)

In 1986, CBS telecast _Carnegie Hall: The Grand Reopening_ in primetime, in what was then a rare move for a commercial broadcast network, since most primetime classical music specials were relegated to PBS and [A&E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A&E_(TV_network) "A&E (TV network)") by this time. The program was a concert commemorating the re-opening of [Carnegie Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Hall "Carnegie Hall") after its complete renovation. A range of artists were featured, from classical conductor Leonard Bernstein to popular music singer [Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra "Frank Sinatra").

#### _Cinderella_

To compete with NBC, which produced the televised version of the [Mary Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Martin "Mary Martin") Broadway production of _[Peter Pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pan_(1954_musical) "Peter Pan (1954 musical)")_, CBS responded with a musical production of _[Cinderella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella_(Rodgers_and_Hammerstein_musical) "Cinderella (Rodgers and Hammerstein musical)")_, with music by [Richard Rodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Rodgers "Richard Rodgers") and lyrics by [Oscar Hammerstein II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Hammerstein_II "Oscar Hammerstein II"). Based upon the [classic Charles Perrault fairy tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella "Cinderella"), it is the only Rodgers and Hammerstein musical to have been written for television. It was originally broadcast live in color on CBS on March 31, 1957, as a vehicle for [Julie Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Andrews "Julie Andrews"), who played the title role; that broadcast was seen by over 100 million people. It was subsequently remade by CBS in 1965, with Lesley Ann Warren, Stuart Damon, [Ginger Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginger_Rogers "Ginger Rogers"), and [Walter Pidgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Pidgeon "Walter Pidgeon") among its stars; the remake also included the new song "Loneliness of Evening", which was originally composed in 1949 for _[South Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pacific_(musical) "South Pacific (musical)")_ but was not performed in that musical.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-recreates-33)[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-34) This version was rebroadcast several times on CBS into the early 1970s, and is occasionally broadcast on various cable networks to this day; both versions are available on DVD.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

#### National Geographic

CBS was also the original broadcast home for the primetime specials produced by the [National Geographic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Society "National Geographic Society"). The Geographic series in the U.S. started on CBS in 1964, before moving to ABC in 1973 (the specials subsequently moved to PBS – under the production of Pittsburgh member station WQED – in 1975 and NBC in 1995, before returning to PBS in 2000). The specials have featured stories on many scientific figures such as [Louis Leakey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Leakey "Louis Leakey"), [Jacques Cousteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Cousteau "Jacques Cousteau"), and [Jane Goodall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Goodall "Jane Goodall"), that not only featured their work but helped make them internationally known and accessible to millions. A majority of the specials were narrated by various actors, notably [Alexander Scourby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Scourby "Alexander Scourby") during the CBS run. The success of the specials led in part to the creation of the [National Geographic Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Channel "National Geographic Channel"), a cable channel launched in January 2001 as a joint venture between the National Geographic Society and [Fox Cable Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Cable_Networks "Fox Cable Networks"). The specials' distinctive theme music, by [Elmer Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Bernstein "Elmer Bernstein"), was also adopted by the National Geographic Channel.

#### Other notable specials

From 1949 to 2002, the [Pillsbury Bake-Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillsbury_Bake-Off "Pillsbury Bake-Off"), an annual national cooking contest, was broadcast on CBS as a special. Hosts for the broadcast included [Arthur Godfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Godfrey "Arthur Godfrey"), [Art Linkletter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Linkletter "Art Linkletter"), [Bob Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Barker "Bob Barker"), [Gary Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Collins_(actor) "Gary Collins (actor)"), [Willard Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Scott "Willard Scott") (although under contract with CBS's rival NBC), and [Alex Trebek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Trebek "Alex Trebek").[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-35)

The [Miss USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_USA "Miss USA") [beauty pageant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_pageant "Beauty pageant") aired on CBS from 1963 to 2002, during a large portion of that period, the telecast was often emceed by the host of one of CBS's game shows including Bob Barker from 1967 to 1987 (at which point Barker, an [animal rights activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights_activist "Animal rights activist") who eventually convinced producers of _The Price Is Right_ to cease offering [fur coats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_coat "Fur coat") as prizes on the program, quit in a dispute over their use), succeed by [Alan Thicke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Thicke "Alan Thicke") in 1988, [Dick Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark "Dick Clark") from 1989 to 1993, and [Bob Goen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Goen "Bob Goen") from 1994 to 1996. The pageant's highest viewership was recorded in the early 1980s when it regularly topped the Nielsen ratings on the week of its broadcast.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-36)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-37)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-38) Viewership dropped sharply throughout the 1990s and 2000s, from an estimated viewership of 20 million to an average of 7 million from 2000 to 2001.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-39) In 2002, [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") (owner of the Miss USA pageant's governing body, the [Miss Universe Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Universe "Miss Universe")) brokered a new deal with NBC, giving it half-ownership of the Miss USA, Miss Universe and [Miss Teen USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Teen_USA "Miss Teen USA") pageants and moving them to that network as part of an initial five-year contract,[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-40) which began in 2003 and ended in 2015 after 12 years amid Trump's controversial remarks about Mexican immigrants during the launch of his [2016 campaign for the Republican presidential nomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Donald_Trump_presidential_campaign "2016 Donald Trump presidential campaign").[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-41)

On June 1, 1977, it was announced that [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley "Elvis Presley") had signed a deal with CBS to appear in a new television special. Under the agreement, CBS would videotape Presley's concerts during the summer of 1977; the special was filmed during Presley's final tour at stops in [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska "Omaha, Nebraska") (on June 19) and [Rapid City, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_City,_South_Dakota "Rapid City, South Dakota") (on June 21 of that year). CBS aired the special, _[Elvis in Concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_in_Concert "Elvis in Concert")_, on October 3, 1977,[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-42) nearly two months after Presley died in his [Graceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graceland "Graceland") mansion on August 16.

Since its inception in 1978, CBS has been the sole broadcaster of [The Kennedy Center Honors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kennedy_Center_Honors "The Kennedy Center Honors"), a two-hour performing arts tribute typically taped and edited in December for later broadcast during the holiday season.

## Stations

Main articles: [List of CBS television affiliates (table)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_CBS_television_affiliates_(table) "List of CBS television affiliates (table)"), [List of CBS television affiliates (by U.S. state)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_CBS_television_affiliates_(by_U.S._state) "List of CBS television affiliates (by U.S. state)"), and [CBS News and Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_and_Stations "CBS News and Stations")

CBS has 15 owned-and-operated stations, and current and pending affiliation agreements with 228 additional television stations encompassing 50 states, the District of Columbia, two U.S. possessions (Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands) and [Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda "Bermuda") and [St. Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vincent_and_the_Grenadines "St. Vincent and the Grenadines").[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-re-43)[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-StationIndex-44) The network has a national reach of 95.96% of all households in the United States (or 299,861,665 Americans with at least one television set). Currently, [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey"), [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire "New Hampshire") and [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware "Delaware") are the only U.S. states where CBS does not have a locally licensed affiliate (New Jersey is served by New York City O&O WCBS-TV and Philadelphia O&O KYW-TV; Delaware is served by KYW and [Salisbury, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury,_Maryland "Salisbury, Maryland"), affiliate [WBOC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBOC-TV "WBOC-TV"); and New Hampshire is served by [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston") O&O [WBZ-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBZ-TV "WBZ-TV") and [Burlington, Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington,_Vermont "Burlington, Vermont"), affiliate [WCAX-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCAX-TV "WCAX-TV")).

CBS maintains affiliations with low-power stations (broadcasting either in analog or digital) in a few markets, such as [Harrisonburg, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisonburg,_Virginia "Harrisonburg, Virginia") ( [WSVF-CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WSVF-CD "WSVF-CD")), [Palm Springs, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Springs,_California "Palm Springs, California") ( [KPSP-CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPSP-CD "KPSP-CD")), and [Parkersburg, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkersburg,_West_Virginia "Parkersburg, West Virginia") ( [WIYE-LD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WIYE-LD "WIYE-LD")). In some markets, including both of those mentioned, these stations also maintain digital simulcasts on a subchannel of a co-owned/co-managed full-power television station. CBS also maintains a sizeable number of subchannel-only affiliations, the majority of which are with stations in cities located outside of the 50 largest Nielsen-designated markets; the largest CBS subchannel affiliate by market size is [KOGG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHNL "KHNL") in [Wailuku, Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wailuku,_Hawaii "Wailuku, Hawaii"), which serves as a repeater of [Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu "Honolulu") affiliate [KGMB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGMB "KGMB") (the sister station of KOGG parent KHNL).

[Nexstar Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nexstar_Media_Group "Nexstar Media Group") is the largest operator of CBS stations by numerical total, owning 49 CBS affiliates (counting satellites); [Tegna Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegna,_Inc. "Tegna, Inc.") is the largest operator of CBS stations in terms of overall market reach, owning 15 CBS-affiliated stations (including affiliates in the larger markets in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHOU_(TV) "KHOU (TV)"), [Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTSP "WTSP") and [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WUSA_(TV) "WUSA (TV)")) that reach 8.9% of the country.

## Related services

### Video-on-demand services

CBS provides [video-on-demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_on_demand "Video on demand") access for delayed viewing of the network's programming through various means, including via its website at CBS.com; the network's [apps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_app "Mobile app") for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_(Apple) "IOS (Apple)"), [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_(operating_system) "Android (operating system)"), and newer version [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows "Microsoft Windows") devices; a traditional VOD service called CBS on Demand available on most traditional cable and IPTV providers; and through content deals with [Amazon Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Video "Amazon Video") (which holds exclusive streaming rights to the CBS drama series _[Extant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extant_(TV_series) "Extant (TV series)")_ and _[Under the Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_the_Dome_(TV_series) "Under the Dome (TV series)")_) and [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix").[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-45)[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-46)[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-47)[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-48) Notably, however, CBS is the only major broadcast network that does not provide recent episodes of its programming on [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu "Hulu") (sister network The CW does offer its programming on the [streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media "Streaming media") service, albeit on a one-week delay after becoming available on the network's website on Hulu's free service, with users of its subscription service being granted access to newer episodes of CW series eight hours after their initial broadcast), due to concerns over cannibalizing viewership of some of the network's most prominent programs; however, episode back catalogs of certain past and present CBS series are available on the service through an agreement with CBS Television Distribution.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-49)[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-50)[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-51)

Upon the release of the app in March 2013, CBS restricted streaming of the most recent episode of any of the network's programs on its streaming app for Apple [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS") devices until eight days after their initial broadcast to encourage live or same-week (via both [DVR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video_recorder "Digital video recorder") and cable on demand) viewing; programming selections on the app were limited until the release of its [Google Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Play "Google Play") and [Windows 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_8 "Windows 8") apps in October 2013, expanded the selections to include full episodes of all CBS series to which the network does not license the streaming rights to other services.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-52)

#### Paramount+ (formerly CBS All Access)

On October 28, 2014, CBS launched [CBS All Access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+ "Paramount+"), an [over-the-top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-top_media_service "Over-the-top media service") subscription streaming service – priced at $5.99 per month ($9.99 with the no commercials option) – which allows users to view past and present episodes of CBS shows.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-NewWayToWatchCBS-53)[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-54)[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-55) Announced on October 16, 2014 (one day after HBO announced the launch of its over-the-top service [HBO Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Now "HBO Now")) as the first OTT offering by a USA broadcast television network, the service initially encompassed the network's existing streaming portal at CBS.com and its mobile app for [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone "Smartphone") and [tablet computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablet_computer "Tablet computer"); CBS All Access became available on [Roku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roku "Roku") on April 7, 2015, and on [Chromecast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromecast "Chromecast") on May 14, 2015.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-56)[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-57) In addition to providing full-length episodes of CBS programs, the service allows live programming streams of local CBS affiliates in 124 markets reaching 75% of the United States.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-58)[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-59)[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-60)[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-61)[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-62)

CBS All Access offered the most recent episodes of the network's shows the day after their original broadcast, as well as complete back catalogs of most of its current series and a wide selection of episodes of classic series from the [CBS Television Distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Television_Distribution "CBS Television Distribution") and [ViacomCBS Domestic Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ViacomCBS_Domestic_Media_Networks "ViacomCBS Domestic Media Networks") program library to subscribers of the service. CBS All Access also carried behind-the-scenes features from CBS programs and special events.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-NewWayToWatchCBS-53)

Original programs aired on CBS All Access included _[Star Trek: Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek:_Discovery "Star Trek: Discovery")_, _[The Good Fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Fight "The Good Fight")_, and _[Big Brother: Over the Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brother:_Over_the_Top "Big Brother: Over the Top")_.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-63)[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-64)[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-65)

In December 2018, the service was launched in Australia under the name **10 All Access**, due to its affiliation with CBS-owned free-to-air broadcaster [Network 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_10 "Network 10"). Due to local programming rights, not all content is shared with its U.S. counterpart, whilst the Australian version also features numerous full seasons of local Network 10 shows, all commercial-free.

It was announced in September 2020 that the service would be rebranded as [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+ "Paramount+") in early 2021, and would feature content from the wider ViacomCBS library following the re-merger between CBS and Viacom. The name was also extended to international markets and services such as 10 All Access.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-ParamountPlus-66) The rebrand to Paramount+ took place on March 4, 2021.

### CBS HD

CBS's master feed is transmitted in [1080i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1080i "1080i") [high definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television "High-definition television"), the native resolution format for CBS Corporation's television properties. However, seven of its affiliates transmit the network's programming in [720p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/720p "720p") HD, while seven others carry the network feed in [480i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/480i "480i") [standard definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_definition "Standard definition")[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-re-43) either due to technical considerations for affiliates of other major networks that carry CBS programming on a digital subchannel or because a primary feed CBS affiliate has not yet upgraded their transmission equipment to allow content to be presented in HD. A small number of CBS stations and affiliates are also currently broadcasting at [1080p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1080p "1080p") via an [ATSC 3.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATSC_3.0 "ATSC 3.0") multiplex station to simulcast a station's programming such as [WNCN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNCN "WNCN") through [WRDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WRDC "WRDC") in [Durham, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina "Durham, North Carolina"), [WTVF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTVF "WTVF") through [WUXP-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WUXP-TV "WUXP-TV") in [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee"), and [KLAS-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLAS-TV "KLAS-TV") through [KVCW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVCW "KVCW") in [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas "Las Vegas"), [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada "Nevada").

CBS began its conversion to high definition with the launch of its simulcast feed CBS HD in September 1998, at the start of the [1998–99 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_United_States_network_television_schedule "1998–99 United States network television schedule"). That year, CBS aired the first NFL game broadcast in high-definition, with the telecast of the [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_New_York_Jets_season "1998 New York Jets season")– [Buffalo Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Buffalo_Bills_season "1998 Buffalo Bills season") game on November 8. CBS gradually converted much of its existing programming from standard definition to high definition beginning with the [2000–01 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_United_States_network_television_schedule "2000–01 United States network television schedule"), with select shows among that season's slate of freshmen scripted series being broadcast in HD starting with their debuts. _The Young and the Restless_ became the first daytime soap opera to broadcast in HD on June 27, 2001.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-HDTV-67)

CBS's 14-year conversion to an entirely high-definition schedule ended in 2014, with _[Big Brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Brother_(American_TV_series) "Big Brother (American TV series)")_ and _[Let's Make a Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Make_a_Deal "Let's Make a Deal")_ becoming the final two series to convert from [4:3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4:3 "4:3") standard definition to HD (in contrast, NBC, Fox, and The CW were already airing their entire programming schedules – outside of Saturday mornings – in high definition by the 2010–11 season, while ABC was broadcasting its entire schedule in HD by the 2011–12 midseason). All of the network's programming has been presented in full HD since then (except for certain [holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_holiday_season "Christmas holiday season") specials produced before 2005 – such as the Rankin-Bass specials – which continue to be presented in 4:3 SD, although some have been remastered for HD broadcast).

On September 1, 2016, when ABC converted to a [16:9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16:9 "16:9") widescreen presentation, CBS and The CW were the only remaining networks that framed their promotions and on-screen graphical elements for a [4:3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4:3 "4:3") presentation, though with CBS Sports' _de facto_ 16:9 conversion with [Super Bowl 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_50 "Super Bowl 50") and their new graphical presentation designed for 16:9 framing, in practice, most CBS affiliates ask pay-TV providers to pass down a 16:9 widescreen presentation by default over their standard definition channels. This continued for CBS until September 24, 2018, when the network converted its on-screen graphical elements to a 16:9 widescreen presentation for all non-news and sports programs. Litton Entertainment continues to frame the graphical elements in their programs for _Dream Team_ within a 4:3 frame due to them being positioned for future syndicated sales, though all of its programming has been in high definition.

## Branding

### Logos

"CBS eye" redirects here. For the animation studio, see [CBS Eye Animation Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Eye_Animation_Productions "CBS Eye Animation Productions").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/CBS_Eyemark.svg/500px-CBS_Eyemark.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:CBS_Eyemark.svg) CBS _Eye_ logo used since 1951

The CBS television network's initial logo, used from the 1940s to 1951, consisted of an oval spotlight which shone on the block letters "CBS".[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-68) The present-day _Eye_ device was conceived by William Golden, based on a [Pennsylvania Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch "Pennsylvania Dutch") [hex sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hex_sign "Hex sign") and a [Shaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakers "Shakers") drawing. While the logo is commonly attributed to Golden, some design work may have been done by CBS staff designer [Georg Olden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Olden_(graphic_designer) "Georg Olden (graphic designer)"), one of the first African-Americans to attract some attention in the postwar graphic design field.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-69) The Eye device made its broadcast debut on October 20, 1951. The following season, as Golden prepared a new "ident", CBS President Frank Stanton insisted on keeping the Eye device and using it as much as possible. Golden died unexpectedly in 1959, and was replaced by [Lou Dorfsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Dorfsman "Lou Dorfsman"), one of his top assistants, who would go on to oversee all print and on-air graphics for CBS for the next 30 years.

The CBS eye has since become a widely recognized symbol. While the wordmark has been changed, the Eye device itself has never been redesigned.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-70) As part of a then-new graphical identity created by Trollbäck + Company that was used by the network during the 2006–2007 network television season, the eye was placed in a "trademark" position on show titles, days of the week and descriptive words, an approach highly respecting the value of the design. The logo is alternatively known as the "Eyemark", a branding used for CBS's [domestic television syndication division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Broadcasting#End_of_Group_W_Productions "Westinghouse Broadcasting"), under the Eyemark Entertainment name, in the mid-to-late 1990s after Westinghouse Electric bought CBS, but before the King World acquisition (which Eyemark was folded into), and subsequent merger with Viacom; Eyemark Entertainment was the result of the merger of MaXaM Entertainment (an independent television syndication firm which Westinghouse acquired shortly after its merger with CBS in 1996), Group W Productions (Westinghouse Broadcasting's own syndication division), & CBS Enterprises (CBS's syndication arm from the late 1960s to the early 1970s).

The eye logo has served as inspiration for the logos of [Associated Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Television "Associated Television") (ATV) in the United Kingdom, [Canal 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_4_(Salvadoran_TV_channel) "Canal 4 (Salvadoran TV channel)") in El Salvador, [Televisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televisa "Televisa") in Mexico, [France 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_3 "France 3"), [Latina Televisión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latina_Televisi%C3%B3n "Latina Televisión") in Peru, [Fuji Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Television "Fuji Television") in Japan, [Rede Bandeirantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rede_Bandeirantes "Rede Bandeirantes") and [TV Globo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Globo "TV Globo") in Brazil, and [Canal 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_10_(Uruguay) "Canal 10 (Uruguay)") in Uruguay.

In October 2011, the network celebrated the 60th anniversary of the introduction of the Eye logo, featuring special IDs of logo versions from previous CBS image campaigns being shown during the network's primetime lineup.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-71)

CBS historically used a specially-commissioned variant of [Didot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didot_(typeface) "Didot (typeface)"), a close relative to [Bodoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodoni "Bodoni"), as its corporate font until 2021.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-72)

### Image campaigns

#### 1980s

CBS has developed several notable image campaigns, and several of the network's most well-known slogans were introduced in the 1980s. The "Reach for the Stars" campaign used during the [1981–82 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_United_States_network_television_schedule "1981–82 United States network television schedule") features a space theme to capitalize on both CBS's stellar improvement in the ratings and the historic launch of the space shuttle [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_(space_shuttle) "Columbia (space shuttle)"). 1982's "Great Moments" juxtaposed scenes from classic CBS programs such as _I Love Lucy_ with scenes from the network's then-current classics such as _Dallas_ and _M\*A\*S\*H_. From 1983 to 1986, CBS (by now firmly atop the ratings) featured a campaign based on the slogan "We've Got the Touch". Vocals for the campaign's jingle were contributed by [Richie Havens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richie_Havens "Richie Havens") (1983–84; one occasion in 1984–85) and [Kenny Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Rogers "Kenny Rogers") (1985–86).

The [1986–87 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_United_States_network_television_schedule "1986–87 United States network television schedule") ushered in the "Share the Spirit of CBS" campaign, the network's first to completely use computer graphics and [digital video effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video_effect "Digital video effect"). Unlike most network campaign promos, the full-length version of "Share the Spirit" not only showed a brief clip preview of each new fall series but also utilized CGI effects to map out the entire fall schedule by night. The success of that campaign led to the 1987–88 "CBS Spirit" (or "CBSPIRIT") campaign. Like its predecessor, most "CBSpirit" promos utilized a procession of clips from the network's programs. However, the new graphic motif was a swirling (or "swishing") blue line that was used to represent "the spirit". The full-length promo, like the previous year, had a special portion that identified new fall shows, but the mapped-out fall schedule shot was abandoned.

For the [1988–89 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_United_States_network_television_schedule "1988–89 United States network television schedule"), CBS unveiled a new image campaign officially known as "Television You Can Feel", but more commonly identified as "You Can Feel It On CBS". The goal was to convey a more sensual, new-age image through distinguished, advanced-looking computer graphics and soothing music, backgrounding images, and clips of emotionally powerful scenes and characters. However, it was this season in which CBS saw its ratings freefall, the deepest in the network's history. CBS ended the decade with "Get Ready for CBS", introduced with the [1989–90 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_United_States_network_television_schedule "1989–90 United States network television schedule"). The initial version was an ambitious campaign that attempted to elevate CBS out of last place (among the major networks); the motif centered around network stars interacting with each other in a remote studio set, getting ready for photo and television shoots, as well as for the new season on CBS. The high-energy promo song and the campaign's practices saw many customized variations by all of CBS's owned-and-operated stations and affiliates, which participated in the campaign per a network mandate. In addition, for the first time in history, CBS became the first broadcast network to partner with a national retailer (in this case, [Kmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kmart_(United_States) "Kmart (United States)")) to encourage viewership, with the "CBS/Kmart Get Ready Giveaway".

#### 1990s

For the [1990–91 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_United_States_network_television_schedule "1990–91 United States network television schedule"), the campaign featured a new jingle performed by [the Temptations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Temptations "The Temptations"), which featured an altered version of their hit " [Get Ready](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Ready_(The_Temptations_song) "Get Ready (The Temptations song)")". The early 1990s featured less-than-memorable campaigns, with simplified taglines such as "This is CBS" (1992) and "You're on CBS" (1995). Eventually, the promotions department gained momentum again late in the decade with "Welcome Home to a CBS Night" (1996–1997), shortened to "Welcome Home" (1997–1999), and succeeded by the spin-off campaign "The Address is CBS" (1999–2000), whose history can be traced back to a CBS slogan from the radio era of the 1940s, "The Stars' Address is CBS". During the 1992 season for the end-of-show network identification sequence, a four-note sound mark was introduced, which was eventually adapted into the network's IDs and production company vanity cards following the closing credits of most of its programs during the "Welcome Home" era.

#### 2000s

Throughout the 2000s, CBS's rating resurgence was backed by the network's "It's All Here" campaign (which introduced updated versions of the 1992 sound mark used during certain promotions and production company vanity cards during the closing credits of programs); in 2005 campaign introduced the slogan "Everybody's Watching", the network's strategy led to the proclamation that it was "America's Most Watched Network". The network's 2006 campaign introduced the slogan "We Are CBS", with [Don LaFontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_LaFontaine "Don LaFontaine") providing the voiceover for the IDs (as well as certain network promos) during this period. In 2009, the network introduced a campaign entitled "Only CBS", in which network promotions proclaim several unique qualities it has (the slogan was also used in program promotions following the announcement of the timeslot of a particular program). The "America's Most Watched Network" was re-introduced by CBS in 2011, used alongside the "Only CBS" slogan.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-73)

#### 2020s

In October 2020, CBS announced that it would begin to employ a more unified branding between the network and its divisions to strengthen [brand awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_awareness "Brand awareness") across platforms. The two main components of the rebranding are a "deconstructed eye" motif using the individual shapes of the eyemark (such as an animated station ID), and a five-note [sound trademark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_trademark "Sound trademark") developed by the audio design agency [Antfood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antfood "Antfood"), phonetically resembling the "This is CBS" slogan.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:2-74)[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:0-75)[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:1-76)

Alongside the rebranding, CBS Television Studios was renamed CBS Studios, and CBS Television Distribution was renamed CBS Media Ventures. The network also dropped the "America's Most Watched Network" and "Only CBS" taglines, with chief marketing officer Michael Benson explaining that they aimed to "be something where people feel like they are part of the family. It's tough to unify if you're bragging about yourself."[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:0-75)[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:1-76) Due to its programming being licensed to third-party streaming services, CBS programming began to carry a CBS Studios [production logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_logo "Production logo") based on the ident when applicable, and are billed with "CBS Original" or "CBS Presents" (specials) bylines in promotional material.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:0-75)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-77)[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:1-76)

As part of the rebranding, CBS News and CBS Sports also introduced new logos and imaging incorporating the deconstructed eye motif and sonic branding, with CBS News initially using it for coverage of the [2020 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election "2020 United States presidential election"), and CBS Sports launching its rebrand ahead of [Super Bowl LV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LV "Super Bowl LV") in 2021.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:0-75)[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:1-76)[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-78)[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-79) In December 2022, [CBS News and Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_and_Stations "CBS News and Stations") began to deploy the branding on the local news operations of CBS's owned-and-operated stations, with most now being branded as "CBS News (region)" to align themselves with CBS News and its chain of [local streaming news channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_(streaming_service)#CBS_News_Local "CBS News (streaming service)") (with some exceptions in markets with heritage station brands, such as [KPIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPIX-TV "KPIX-TV")) and adopting new graphics and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_news_music "Television news music") incorporating the eye motif and sound mark (replacing [Frank Gari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Gari "Frank Gari")'s "Enforcer" music package, which was based on a theme historically used by [WBBM-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBBM-TV "WBBM-TV")).[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-:2-74)[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-80)

## Controversies

### "Gay Power, Gay Politics"

On April 26, 1980, CBS aired " [Gay Power, Gay Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_Power,_Gay_Politics "Gay Power, Gay Politics")", an episode of the _[CBS Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Reports "CBS Reports")_ documentary series which purported to document the growing political significance of the [LGBTQ community in San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_culture_in_San_Francisco "LGBTQ culture in San Francisco"). The documentary was accused of purposeful misrepresentation and propagating [misinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misinformation "Misinformation"), including in its coverage of the [1979 mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_San_Francisco_mayoral_election "1979 San Francisco mayoral election").[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-91) In spite of the documentary's premise, the episode focused largely on gay sexual practices and among other claims, alleged that 10% of deaths in San Francisco were related to [BDSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BDSM "BDSM"). Journalist [George Crile III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Crile_III "George Crile III") also attempted to initiate an interview with then mayor [Dianne Feinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Feinstein "Dianne Feinstein") by asking her "how does it feel to be the mayor of [Sodom and Gomorrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodom_and_Gomorrah "Sodom and Gomorrah")?" The documentary was aired at a time when the [Christian right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_right "Christian right") in the United States sought to use homophobic sentiment as a political tactic.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-92) Six months after the episode aired, the [National News Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_News_Council "National News Council"), in response to a complaint by journalist Randy Alfred, found that CBS had violated journalistic standards in airing the program.[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-93)[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-94)

### Brown & Williamson interview

In 1995, CBS refused to air a _[60 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Minutes "60 Minutes")_ segment that featured an interview with a former president of research and development for [Brown & Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_&_Williamson "Brown & Williamson"), the U.S.'s third largest tobacco company. The controversy raised questions about the legal roles in decision-making and whether journalistic standards should be compromised despite legal pressures and threats. The decision nevertheless sent shockwaves throughout the television industry, the journalism community, and the country.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-95) This incident was the basis for the 1999 [Michael Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mann_(director) "Michael Mann (director)")-directed drama film, _[The Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Insider_(film) "The Insider (film)")_.

### Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show controversy

Main article: [Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVIII_halftime_show_controversy "Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show controversy")

In 2004, the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission "Federal Communications Commission") (FCC) imposed a record $550,000 fine, the largest fine ever for a violation of federal decency laws, against CBS for an incident during its broadcast of [Super Bowl XXXVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVIII "Super Bowl XXXVIII") in which singer [Janet Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Jackson "Janet Jackson")'s right breast (which was partially covered by a piece of nipple jewelry) was briefly and accidentally exposed by guest performer [Justin Timberlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Timberlake "Justin Timberlake") at the end of a duet performance of Timberlake's 2003 single " [Rock Your Body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Your_Body "Rock Your Body")" during the [halftime show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halftime_show "Halftime show") (produced by then sister cable network [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV")).[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-96) Following the incident, CBS apologized to its viewers and denied foreknowledge of the incident, which was televised live. The incident resulted in a period of increased regulation of broadcast television and radio outlets (including self-imposed content regulation by networks and syndicators), which raised concerns surrounding [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship "Censorship") and [freedom of speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_speech "Freedom of speech"),[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-97) and resulted in the FCC voting to increase its maximum fine for indecency violations from US$27,500 to US$325,000.[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-Washington_Post_FCC_Final_Fine-98) In 2008, a Philadelphia federal court annulled the fine imposed on CBS, labeling it "arbitrary and capricious".[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-99)

### Killian documents controversy

Main article: [Killian documents controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killian_documents_controversy "Killian documents controversy")

On September 8, 2004, less than two months before the [Presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election "2004 United States presidential election") in which he defeated [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") candidate [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry "John Kerry"), CBS aired a controversial episode of _[60 Minutes Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Minutes_II "60 Minutes II")_, which questioned then-President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush")'s service in the [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard "Air National Guard") in 1972 and 1973.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-100) Following allegations of forgery, CBS News admitted that four of the documents used in the story had not been properly authenticated and admitted that their source, Bill Burkett, had admitted to having "deliberately misled" a CBS News producer who worked on the report, about the documents' origins out of a confidentiality promise to the actual source.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-101)[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-102) The following January, CBS fired four people connected to the preparation of the segment.[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-103) Former CBS news anchor Dan Rather filed a $70million lawsuit against CBS and former corporate parent Viacom in September 2007, contending the story, and his termination (he resigned as CBS News chief anchor in 2005), were mishandled.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-104)[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-105) Parts of the suit were dismissed in 2008;[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-106) subsequently in 2010, the entire suit was dismissed and Rather's motion to appeal was denied.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-107)

### Hopper controversy

See also: [CNET §Hopper controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNET#Hopper_controversy "CNET")

In January 2013, [CNET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNET "CNET") named [Dish Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dish_Network "Dish Network")'s "Hopper with [Sling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slingbox "Slingbox")" digital video recorder as a nominee for the [CES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_CES "International CES") "Best in Show" award (which is decided by CNET on behalf of its organizers, the [Consumer Electronics Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Electronics_Association "Consumer Electronics Association")), and named it the winner in a vote by the site's staff. However, CBS division CBS Interactive disqualified the Hopper and vetoed the results as CBS was in active litigation with Dish Network over its [AutoHop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AutoHop "AutoHop") technology (which allows users to skip commercial advertisements during recorded programs).[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-108) CNET announced that it would no longer review any product or service provided by companies that CBS Corporation was in litigation with. The "Best in Show" award was instead given to the [Razer Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razer_USA "Razer USA") tablet.[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-wsj-hopper-109)[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-pcmag-cnethopper-110)[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-verge-cescbs-111) On January 14, 2013, CNET editor-in-chief Lindsey Turrentine said in a statement that its staff was in an "impossible" situation due to the [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest "Conflict of interest") posed by the lawsuit, and promised to prevent a similar incident from occurring again. The conflict also prompted the resignation of CNET senior writer Greg Sandoval.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-pcmag-cnethopper-110) As a result of the controversy, the CEA announced on January 31, 2013, that CNET will no longer decide the CES Best in Show award winner due to the interference of CBS (with the position being offered to other technology publications), and the "Best in Show" award was jointly awarded to both the Hopper with Sling and Razer Edge.[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-verge-cescbs-111)[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-112)

### Harassment allegations

In July 2018, an article by [Ronan Farrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronan_Farrow "Ronan Farrow") in _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_ claimed that thirty "current and former CBS employees described harassment, gender discrimination, or retaliation" at CBS and six women accused Les Moonves of harassment and intimidation.[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-113) Following these allegations, it was reported on September 6, 2018, that CBS board members were negotiating Les Moonves's departure from the company.[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-114)

On September 9, 2018, _The New Yorker_ reported that six additional women (in addition to the six original women reported in July) had raised accusations against Moonves, going back to the 1980s.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-115) Following this, Moonves resigned the same day as chief executive of CBS.[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-116)

### Second Trump administration

In 2025, some accused CBS of capitulating to the second [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") administration, allegedly in part to gain federal government approval for the [Skydance–Paramount merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Skydance_Media_and_Paramount_Global "Merger of Skydance Media and Paramount Global").

In April, in response to a lawsuit filed by Trump, _[60 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Minutes "60 Minutes")_ editor Bill Owens resigned, saying "he no longer has control of the show."[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-117)

On July 17, [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert "Stephen Colbert") announced that his show, _[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")_ would be canceled effective May 2026 at the conclusion of his current contract, and that the entire _Late Show_ franchise would be retired at that time. While CBS stated that the decision was "purely financial", many were skeptical, since the decision came shortly after Colbert had publicly called CBS's $16 million settlement to Trump "a big fat bribe", the pending Skydance merger was approved soon after by the [FCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission "Federal Communications Commission"), and Trump publicly celebrated the show's cancellation. Other late night hosts spoke out in support of Colbert, with several appearing in cameo roles on the July 20 episode of _The Late Show_ to offer their support.[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-118)

On January 14, 2026, CBS reported that Jonathan Ross, the [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement") agent who shot [Renee Nicole Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Ren%C3%A9e_Good "Killing of Renée Good") in Minneapolis, suffered internal bleeding to the torso after the incident.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-119) A CBS staffer who was unauthorized to speak publicly said, "It was viewed as a thinly veiled, anonymous leak by \[the Trump administration\] to someone who'd carry it online."[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-120) _Forbes_, _The Guardian_ and [Mediaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediaite "Mediaite") reported that there was "huge internal concern" at CBS News over running that news report.[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-121)[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-122)[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS#cite_note-123)
