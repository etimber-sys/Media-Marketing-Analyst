Nickelodeon

**Nickelodeon** (commonly shortened to **Nick**) is an American [pay television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_television "Pay television") [channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_channel "Television channel") and the flagship property of [Nickelodeon Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Group "Nickelodeon Group"), a sub-division of the [Paramount Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks "Paramount Media Networks") division of [Paramount Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Skydance "Paramount Skydance"). Launched on April 1, 1979, as the first cable channel for children, it is primarily aimed at children and adolescents aged 2 to 17,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-2) along with a broader family audience through its [programming blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_programming "Block programming").

The channel began as a test broadcast on December 1, 1977,[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-3) as part of [QUBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qube_(cable_television) "Qube (cable television)"),[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200415%E2%80%9316-4) an early [cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television "Cable television") system broadcast locally in [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-:0-5) On April 1, 1979, the channel was renamed Nickelodeon and launched to a new nationwide audience,[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-6) with _[Pinwheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinwheel_(TV_series) "Pinwheel (TV series)")_ as its inaugural program.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-:0-5) The network was initially commercial-free and remained without advertising until 1984. Nickelodeon gained a rebranding in programming and image that year, and its ensuing success led to it and its sister networks [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV") and [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1") being sold to [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932005) "Viacom (1952–2005)") in 1985.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-MilwaukeeSentinel-7)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200421%E2%80%9322-8)

Nickelodeon began expanding as a franchise model with the addition of [sister channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_channel "Sister channel") and program blocks. [Nick Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Jr. "Nick Jr.") launched as preschool morning block on January 4, 1988, and was eventually spun-off into the [Nick Jr. Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Jr._Channel "Nick Jr. Channel") in 2009. [Nicktoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicktoons_(American_TV_channel) "Nicktoons (American TV channel)"), based on [the flagship brand for Nickelodeon original animated series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicktoons "Nicktoons"), launched as a standalone channel in 2002. [Noggin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noggin_(brand) "Noggin (brand)"), an interactive educational brand created in partnership with [Sesame Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Workshop "Sesame Workshop"), existed as a television channel from 1999 to 2009, and a mobile streaming service from 2015 to 2024. Two blocks aimed at teenage audiences, Nickelodeon's [TEENick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TEENick "TEENick") and Noggin's [The N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_N "The N"), were merged to form the [TeenNick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeenNick "TeenNick") channel in 2009.

As of December2023[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Nickelodeon&action=edit), Nickelodeon was available to approximately 70 million pay television households in the United States, down from its peak of 101 million households in 2011.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-9) Until August 2025, the channel's content aired on the children's channel [YTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YTV_(Canadian_TV_channel) "YTV (Canadian TV channel)"). Nickelodeon content stopped airing on YTV on September 1, 2025, the same date that the [Canadian counterpart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_(Canadian_TV_channel) "Nickelodeon (Canadian TV channel)") of the channel shut down completely.

## History

Main article: [History of Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nickelodeon "History of Nickelodeon")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fa/Nickelodeon_logo_2009.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nickelodeon_logo_2009.png) Nickelodeon "Splat" logo used in 2009. The text in this logo has been in use from 1984 to 2009. This text is known as the typeface, [Balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon_(typeface) "Balloon (typeface)").

The channel's name comes from the first [five-cent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel_(United_States_coin) "Nickel (United States coin)") movie theaters called [nickelodeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_(movie_theater) "Nickelodeon (movie theater)"). Its history dates back to December 1, 1977, when Warner Cable Communications (later known as [Time Warner Cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Warner_Cable "Time Warner Cable")) launched the first 2-way interactive cable system, [QUBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qube_(cable_television) "Qube (cable television)"),[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200415%E2%80%9316-4) in [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio"). The C-3 cable channel carried _[Pinwheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinwheel_(TV_series) "Pinwheel (TV series)")_ daily from 7:00a.m. to 9:00p.m. Eastern Time,[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200415%E2%80%9316-4)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-10) and the channel was labelled "Pinwheel" on remote controllers, as it was the only program broadcast. Initially scheduled for a February 1979 launch,[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-11) Nickelodeon launched on April 1, 1979, initially distributed to Warner Cable systems via satellite on the [RCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Corporation "RCA Corporation") [Satcom-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satcom_(satellite) "Satcom (satellite)") transponder.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-cablecenter.org-12) Originally commercial-free, advertising was introduced in January 1984.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-MilwaukeeSentinel-7)

## Programming

Main article: [List of programs broadcast by Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_programs_broadcast_by_Nickelodeon "List of programs broadcast by Nickelodeon")

Programming seen on Nickelodeon includes animated series (such as _[SpongeBob SquarePants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpongeBob_SquarePants "SpongeBob SquarePants")_, _[The Loud House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Loud_House "The Loud House")_, _[The Patrick Star Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Patrick_Star_Show "The Patrick Star Show")_ and _[The Smurfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smurfs_(2021_TV_series) "The Smurfs (2021 TV series)")_), live-action, scripted series (such as _[The Thundermans: Undercover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thundermans:_Undercover "The Thundermans: Undercover")_), and original [television films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_film "Television film"), while the network's daytime schedule is dedicated to shows targeting preschoolers (such as _[Bubble Guppies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_Guppies "Bubble Guppies")_, _[Paw Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paw_Patrol "Paw Patrol")_, and _[Blue's Clues & You!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue's_Clues_&_You! "Blue's Clues & You!")_).

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7a/Nickelodeon_2009_logo.svg/960px-Nickelodeon_2009_logo.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nickelodeon_2009_logo.svg) Logo used since September 2009. Concurrently used with the 2023 logo since March 2023

A recurring program was bi-monthly special editions of _[Nick News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_News "Nick News")_,[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200454-13) a news magazine series aimed at children hosted by [Linda Ellerbee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ellerbee "Linda Ellerbee"); it premiered in 1992 as a weekly series and ended in 2015.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-14) In June 2020, Nickelodeon announced that they would revive _Nick News_ in a series of hour-long specials. The first installment, _**Kids, Race and Unity: A Nick News Special**_ premiered on June 29, 2020, and was hosted by R&B musician [Alicia Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicia_Keys "Alicia Keys").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-15)

Since 2021, Nickelodeon has aired at least one live [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") game a year, produced by corporate sibling [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports "CBS Sports") and incorporating elements unique to Nickelodeon into the broadcast such as green slime in the end zone and [SpongeBob SquarePants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpongeBob_SquarePants_(character) "SpongeBob SquarePants (character)")' face superimposed on the netting of the goalposts. Nickelodeon also carries the weekly shoulder program _[NFL Slimetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Slimetime "NFL Slimetime")_ during the season which includes similar graphics.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-16) Nickelodeon offered the first alternate broadcast of a [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl "Super Bowl") in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVIII "Super Bowl LVIII") when it aired a _[SpongeBob SquarePants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpongeBob_SquarePants "SpongeBob SquarePants")_-themed simulcast of CBS' coverage.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-17)

### Nicktoons

Main article: [Nicktoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicktoons "Nicktoons")

**Nicktoons** is the branding for Nickelodeon's original [animated series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animated_series "Animated series").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200487-18)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlickstein2013-19) Until 1991, the animated series that aired on Nickelodeon were largely imported from foreign countries, with some original animated specials that were also featured on the channel up to that point.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot2004104%E2%80%93105-20)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-21) Though the Nicktoons branding has infrequently been used by the network itself since the 2002 launch of the channel [of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicktoons_(American_TV_channel) "Nicktoons (American TV channel)"), original animated series continue to take a substantial portion of Nickelodeon's lineup.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlickstein2013-19) Roughly, six to seven hours of these programs are seen on the weekday schedule, and around nine hours on weekends, including a dedicated weekend morning animation [block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_programming "Block programming").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot2004104%E2%80%93105-20)

In 2006, the channel struck a deal with [DreamWorks Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation "DreamWorks Animation") to develop the studio's animated films into television series (such as _[The Penguins of Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Penguins_of_Madagascar "The Penguins of Madagascar")_).[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-22) Since the early 2010s, [Nickelodeon Animation Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Animation_Studio "Nickelodeon Animation Studio") has also produced series based on preexisting [IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property "Intellectual property") purchased by Paramount's predecessor, Viacom, such as _[Winx Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winx_Club "Winx Club")_ and _[Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles_(2012_TV_series) "Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles (2012 TV series)")_.

### Movies

Main article: [List of Nickelodeon original films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nickelodeon_original_films "List of Nickelodeon original films")

Nickelodeon has produced a variety of original made-for-television movies, which usually premiere in weekend evening timeslots or on [school holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_holiday "School holiday"). Nickelodeon also periodically acquires theatrically released feature films for broadcast on the channel.

The channel occasionally airs feature films produced by the network's [Nickelodeon Movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Movies "Nickelodeon Movies") film production division (whose films are distributed by sister company [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures")). Although the film division has the Nickelodeon brand name, the channel does not have access to most of the movies produced by its film unit. The majority of the live-action feature films produced under the Nickelodeon Movies banner are licensed for broadcast by various free-to-air and pay television outlets within the United States other than Nickelodeon (although the network has aired a few live-action Nickelodeon Movies releases such as _[Angus, Thongs and Perfect Snogging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus,_Thongs_and_Perfect_Snogging "Angus, Thongs and Perfect Snogging")_ and _[Good Burger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Burger "Good Burger")_).

Nickelodeon also advertises hour-long episodes of its original series as movies; though the "TV movie" versions of Nickelodeon's original series differ from traditional television films in that they have shorter running times (approximately 45 minutes, as opposed to 75–100 minute run times that most television movies have), and use a traditional [multi-camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple-camera_setup "Multiple-camera setup") setup for regular episodes (unless the program is originally shot in the [single-camera setup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-camera_setup "Single-camera setup") common of films) with some on-location filming.

In 2002, Nickelodeon entered a long-standing broadcast partnership with [Mattel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattel "Mattel") to air films and specials based on the latter's [Barbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbie "Barbie") (and later [Monster High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_High "Monster High")) dolls. The first [Barbie film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Barbie_films "List of Barbie films") to air on Nickelodeon was _[Barbie as Rapunzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbie_as_Rapunzel "Barbie as Rapunzel")_ on November 24, 2002.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-23) The Barbie and Monster High films are usually aired under a [brokered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokered_programming "Brokered programming") format in which Mattel purchases the time in order to promote the release of their films on DVD within a few days of the Nickelodeon premiere, an arrangement possible as Nickelodeon does not have to meet the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission "Federal Communications Commission") rules which disallow that arrangement for broadcast channels due to regulations banning [paid programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infomercial "Infomercial") to children. This ended with _[Barbie: Video Game Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbie:_Video_Game_Hero "Barbie: Video Game Hero")_ in 2017, after which the Barbie film series moved to [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") with a reduced 1-hour runtime.

### Programming blocks

#### Current

- _**[Nick Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Jr. "Nick Jr.")**_ – Nickelodeon currently broadcasts shows targeted at preschool-aged children on Monday through Fridays from 7 a.m. to 2 p.m. Eastern and Pacific Time (7:00 to 10:00a.m. during the summer months, other designated school break periods, and on national holidays). The block primarily targets audiences of preschool age as Nickelodeon's usual audience of school-aged children are in school during the block's designated time period. Programs currently seen in this block include _[Paw Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paw_Patrol "Paw Patrol")_, _[Peppa Pig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peppa_Pig "Peppa Pig")_, _[Blaze and the Monster Machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaze_and_the_Monster_Machines "Blaze and the Monster Machines")_, _[Ryan's Mystery Playdate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan's_Mystery_Playdate "Ryan's Mystery Playdate")_, _[Blue's Clues & You!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue's_Clues_&_You! "Blue's Clues & You!")_, _[Santiago of the Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_of_the_Seas "Santiago of the Seas")_, and _[Baby Shark's Big Show!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Shark's_Big_Show! "Baby Shark's Big Show!")_.
- **[Nick at Nite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_at_Nite "Nick at Nite")** – Nickelodeon's nighttime programming service,[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200471%E2%80%9372-24) which premiered on July 1, 1985, and broadcasts from prime time to early morning (the block's air time varies each night). Originally featured classic sitcoms from the 1950s and 1960s such as _[The Donna Reed Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Donna_Reed_Show "The Donna Reed Show")_,[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200471%E2%80%9372-24) _[Mr. Ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Ed "Mr. Ed")_ and _[Lassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassie_(1954_TV_series) "Lassie (1954 TV series)")_, programming eventually shifted towards repeats of popular [sitcoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitcom "Sitcom") from the 1980s to the 2000s such as _[Home Improvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Improvement_(TV_series) "Home Improvement (TV series)")_, _[The Cosby Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cosby_Show "The Cosby Show")_ and _[Roseanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roseanne "Roseanne")_.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-:1-25) In 1996, a pay television channel, **[TV Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Land "TV Land")** (formerly **Nick at Nite's TV Land**, until 1997) based on the block, launched with a similar format of programs.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-26) Nick at Nite has also occasionally incorporated original scripted and competition series, with some in recent years produced through its parent network's Nickelodeon Productions unit. As of 2021, programming on Nick at Nite consists entirely of acquired shows such as _[Full House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_House "Full House")_, _[Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends "Friends")_, [_Mom_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mom_(TV_series) "Mom (TV series)") and _[Young Sheldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Sheldon "Young Sheldon")_. Since 2004, [Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research "Nielsen Media Research") has broken out the [television ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_ratings "Nielsen ratings") of Nick at Nite and Nickelodeon as two separate networks.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-:1-25)

#### Former

- **[SNICK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNICK "SNICK")** – "SNICK" (short for "Saturday Night Nickelodeon") was the network's first dedicated Saturday primetime block that aired from 8:00 to 10:00p.m. Eastern and Pacific Time featuring shows for preteens and teenagers, it premiered on August 15, 1992, with the initial lineup featuring two established series that originally aired on Sundays, _[Clarissa Explains It All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarissa_Explains_It_All "Clarissa Explains It All")_ and _[The Ren & Stimpy Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ren_&_Stimpy_Show "The Ren & Stimpy Show")_, and two new series, _[Roundhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundhouse_(TV_series) "Roundhouse (TV series)")_ and _[Are You Afraid of the Dark?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are_You_Afraid_of_the_Dark%3F "Are You Afraid of the Dark?")._ The block mainly featured live-action series (primarily comedies), although it periodically featured animated series. SNICK was discontinued on January 29, 2005, and was replaced the following week by a Saturday night edition of the TEENick block on February 5, 2005.
- **[Nick in the Afternoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_in_the_Afternoon "Nick in the Afternoon")** – "Nick in the Afternoon" was a daytime block that ran on weekday afternoons during the summer months from 1995 to 1997, and aired in an extended format until December for its final year in 1998. It was hosted by Stick Stickly, a [Mr. Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Bill "Mr. Bill")-like popsicle stick character (puppeteered by Rick Lyon and voiced by actor [Paul Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Christie_(voice_actor) "Paul Christie (voice actor)"), who would later voice the Noggin mascot [Moose A. Moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose_and_Zee "Moose and Zee")). The block was replaced for Summer 1999 by "Henry and June's Summer" (hosted by the animated hosts of the [anthology series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series "Anthology series") _[KaBlam!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KaBlam! "KaBlam!")_). From 2011 to 2012, Stick Stickly returned to television for TeenNick's "The '90s Are All That" to host "U-Pick with Stick" on Friday nights as a concept of user-chosen programming.
- **[U-Pick Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-Pick_Live "U-Pick Live")** – "U-Pick Live" (originally branded as "U-Pick Friday" from 1999 to late 2000, and originally hosted by the Henry and June characters from _KaBlam!_) was a block that aired weekday afternoons from 5:00 to 7:00p.m. Eastern and Pacific Time from October 14, 2002, to May 27, 2005, which was broadcast from studios in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City")'s [Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square "Times Square") district, where Nickelodeon is headquartered. Using a similar concept that originated in 1994, with the Nick in the Afternoon block, "U-Pick Live" allowed viewer interaction in selecting the programs (usually cartoons) that would air on the block via voting on the network's website.
- **[TEENick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TEENick "TEENick")** – "TEENick" was a teenage-oriented block that ran from March 4, 2001, to February 1, 2009, which ran on Sundays from 6:00 to 9:00p.m. Eastern and Pacific Time; a secondary block on Saturdays launched in 2005, replacing the 8:00 to 10:00p.m. Eastern/Pacific timeslot long held by SNICK. It was originally hosted by [Nick Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Cannon "Nick Cannon"), and then by Jason "J. Boogie" Everhart. ## Related networks and services

### Current sister channels

#### Nick Jr. Channel

Main article: [Nick Jr. Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Jr._Channel "Nick Jr. Channel")

**Nick Jr. Channel** (sometimes shortened to **Nick Jr.**) is a pay television network aimed mainly at children between 2 and 6 years of age. It features a mix of current and former preschool-oriented programs from Nickelodeon, as well as some shows that are exclusive to the channel. The Nick Jr. Channel launched on September 28, 2009, as a spin-off of Nickelodeon's preschool [programming block of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Jr. "Nick Jr."), which had aired since January 4, 1988.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-:2-29) The channel replaced [Noggin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noggin_(brand)#TV_channel "Noggin (brand)"), which was relaunched as a [streaming service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_service "Streaming service") in 2015, and acts as a separate sister brand. [Noggin's programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_programs_broadcast_by_Noggin "List of programs broadcast by Noggin") is distinct from the Nick Jr. channel's; it mainly carried preteen-oriented programs at its launch,[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-30) and its 2015 streaming service features a variety of exclusive series. On October 1, 2012, the Nick Jr. Channel introduced [NickMom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NickMom "NickMom"), a four-hour nighttime block aimed at parents,[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-31) which ran until September 28, 2015.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-32)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-33) While traditional advertising appeared on the channel during the NickMom block, the network otherwise only runs programming promotions and [underwriter-style sponsorships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwriting "Underwriting") in lieu of regular commercials.

#### Nicktoons

Main article: [Nicktoons (American TV channel)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicktoons_(American_TV_channel) "Nicktoons (American TV channel)")

**Nicktoons** is a [pay television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_television "Pay television") network that launched on May 1, 2002,[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-:2-29) as **Nicktoons TV**; it was renamed Nicktoons in April 2003, and rebranded as **Nicktoons Network** in September 2005, before reverting to its previous name in September 2009. The network airs a mix of newer live-action and animated shows from Nickelodeon such as _[Henry Danger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Danger "Henry Danger")_, _[The Fairly OddParents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fairly_OddParents "The Fairly OddParents")_, _[The Loud House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Loud_House "The Loud House")_, _[SpongeBob SquarePants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpongeBob_SquarePants "SpongeBob SquarePants")_, and _[Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles_(2012_TV_series) "Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles (2012 TV series)")_ alongside original series airing exclusively on Nicktoons.

#### TeenNick

Main article: [TeenNick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeenNick "TeenNick")

**TeenNick** is a pay television network that is aimed at [adolescents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescent "Adolescent") and young adults, named after the [TEENick block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TEENick "TEENick") that aired on Nickelodeon from March 2001[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-34) to February 2009. The channel merged programming from the TEENick block with [The N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_N "The N"), a former block on Noggin. Although TeenNick has more relaxed program standards than the other Nickelodeon channels (save for Nick at Nite and the former NickMom block on Nick Jr.), allowing for moderate profanity, suggestive dialogue and some violent content, the network has shifted its lineup almost exclusively towards current and former Nickelodeon series (including some that are [burned off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_off "Burning off") due to low ratings on the flagship channel) that have stricter content standards. It also airs some acquired sitcoms and drama series.

NickMusic

**NickMusic** is a pay television network in the United States featuring music videos from artists appealing to Nickelodeon's target audience. It launched on the channel space formerly held by MTV Hits on September 9, 2016.

Like its sibling [music video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_video "Music video")-only networks [BET Jams, BET Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#Sister_networks "BET"), and [CMT Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMT_(American_TV_channel)#CMT_Music "CMT (American TV channel)"), NickMusic is based on an [automated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_automation "Broadcast automation") "wheel" schedule that was introduced during the early years of [MTV2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV2 "MTV2"). A new loop starts at 6 a.m. [Eastern Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Time_Zone "Eastern Time Zone"), and is then repeated at 2 p.m. and 10 p.m. [Lyric videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyric_video "Lyric video") are sometimes substituted due to content concerns with the artist's actual music video.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/b/b7/MTV_Hits_Logo_2012.png/250px-MTV_Hits_Logo_2012.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV_Hits_Logo_2012.png) Final logo for MTV Hits from March 26, 2012, until September 9, 2016

The network launched on May 1, 2002, as _MTV Hits_, with its programming composed entirely of music videos. As with [MTV Jams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Jams "BET Jams"), the network was named for a daily program on MTV; in this case, _MTV Hits_, which was that network's main [pop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music") video program. The network composed of current hit music videos, along with a few older videos from earlier in the year, as well as a few from the late 1990s. As both MTV Hits and NickMusic, the network has maintained a commercial-free format, other than internal promotions for Nickelodeon or MTV and MTV-branded properties.

The network has no individual or original programs; _TeenNick Top 10_, a program shared with [TeenNick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeenNick "TeenNick"), was cancelled in mid-2018. In electronic program listings, the titles of each "block" merely delineate an hour in those listings and outside those titles denoting video theming, have no on-air mention. The network's specific theming to younger pop artists has also been underplayed as of 2024, due to various cuts at Paramount Global and the network's complete disassociation from further developing "triple threat" stars due to personnel and industry changes.

### Former sister channels

- **[Nickelodeon Games and Sports for Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Games_and_Sports_for_Kids "Nickelodeon Games and Sports for Kids")** (commonly branded as **Nickelodeon GAS** or **Nick GAS**), was a pay television network that launched on March 1, 1999, as part of the suite of high-tier channels launched by MTV Networks. It ran a mix of [game shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_show "Game show") and other competition programs from Nickelodeon (essentially formatted as a children's version of, and Viacom's answer to, the [Game Show Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Show_Network "Game Show Network")). The channel closed on December 31, 2007, and it was replaced by a short-lived 24-hour version of Noggin's teen-oriented block The N. However, an automated loop of Nick GAS continued to be carried on [Dish Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dish_Network "Dish Network") due to unknown factors until April 23, 2009.
- **[NickMom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NickMom "NickMom")** (stylized as **nickmom**) was a programming block launched on October 1, 2012, airing in the late night hours on the Nick Jr. Channel. The block aired its own original programming aimed at parents until 2014, then began to carry acquired films and sitcoms. Due to Viacom's 2015 cutbacks involving acquired programming and low ratings, the NickMom block and associated website were discontinued in the early morning hours of September 28, 2015.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-35)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Nick_2_%282009%29.svg/250px-Nick_2_%282009%29.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nick_2_(2009).svg)

- **Nick 2** was the off-air brand for a secondary [timeshift channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeshift_channel "Timeshift channel") of Nickelodeon formerly available on the high-tier packages exclusively on cable providers as a complement to the main Nickelodeon feed, repackaging Nickelodeon's Eastern and Pacific Time Zone feeds for the appropriate time zone (the Pacific feed was distributed to the Eastern and Central Time Zones, and the Eastern feed was distributed to the Pacific and Mountain Time Zones) resulting in the difference in local airtimes for a particular program between two geographic locations being three hours at most, allowing viewers a second chance to watch a program after its initial airing on the Eastern Time Zone feed or to watch the show ahead of its airing on the Pacific Time Zone feed of the main channel (for example, the Nick at Nite block would respectively start at 9:00p.m (Sundays-Fridays) and at 10:30 p.m (Saturdays) Eastern on Nick 2 Pacific or 12:00p.m. (weekdays) 10:00 a.m (weekends) Pacific weeknights on Nick 2 Eastern). Nick 2 would never broadcast in high definition, but the exception is through Xfinity's IPTV services. The service existed from around 2000 until November 2018, launching as **Nick TOO**. The timeshift channel was originally offered as part of the MTV Networks Digital Suite, a slate of channels exclusive to high-tier cable packages (many of the networks also received satellite carriage over time), and was the only American example of two feeds of a non- [premium service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_television "Pay television") being provided to cable and [IPTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPTV "IPTV") providers. A **Nick TOO** logo was used on the channel until 2004, when MTV Networks discontinued using a customized branding on the feed (a logo for Nick 2 was only used for identification purposes on [electronic program guides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_program_guide "Electronic program guide") as a placeholder image); most television listings thus showed the additional channel under the brandings "Nick Pacific (NICKP)/Nick West (NICKW)," or "Nick East (NICKE)." [DirecTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DirecTV "DirecTV") and [Dish Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dish_Network "Dish Network") also offer both Nickelodeon feeds, though they carry both time zone feeds of most of the children's networks that the providers offer by default. [Viacom Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_Media_Networks "Viacom Media Networks") discontinued the Nick 2 digital cable service on November 22, 2018, likely due to [video on demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_on_demand "Video on demand") options making timeshift channels for the most part superfluous. Both time zone feeds continue to be offered on [Xfinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xfinity "Xfinity") as well as satellite providers, unbranded.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-36)
- **[NickRewind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NickRewind "NickRewind") (TeenNick block)** On July 25, 2011, TeenNick began airing _[The '90s Are All That](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_'90s_Are_All_That "The '90s Are All That")_, renamed _The Splat_ in October 2015, a block of Nickelodeon's most popular 1990s programming, targeting the network's target demographic from that era.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-37) After several name changes, the block's final name was "NickRewind" and focused on programming from the 1980s, 1990s, and 2000s (mainly the latter two), and aired nightly. On January 31, 2022, the block was discontinued, with TeenNick's overnight programming mainly consisting of regular reruns.

### Production studios

#### Nickelodeon Animation Studio

Main article: [Nickelodeon Animation Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Animation_Studio "Nickelodeon Animation Studio")

**Nickelodeon Animation Studio** (formerly **Games Animation, Inc.**) is a production firm with two main locations (one in [Burbank, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California "Burbank, California"), and the other in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City")).[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-44) They serve as the animation facilities for many of the network's Nicktoons and Nick Jr. series.

#### Nickelodeon Productions

**Nickelodeon Productions** was a production studio in New York, that provides original sitcoms, animated shows and game-related programs for Nickelodeon. Despite this, the studio's logo is also seen at the end of animated television shows. It was founded as Games Productions in 1987, after [MTV Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Networks "MTV Networks") was purchased by [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932005) "Viacom (1952–2005)").[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-45) The studio was folded into [Paramount Television Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Television_Studios "Paramount Television Studios") in October 2025.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-46)

#### Nickelodeon on Sunset

Main article: [Nickelodeon on Sunset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_on_Sunset "Nickelodeon on Sunset")

**Nickelodeon on Sunset** was a studio complex in [Hollywood, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_California "Hollywood, California") which served as the primary production facility for Nickelodeon's series from 1997 until 2017; the studio is designated by the [National Register for Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_for_Historic_Places "National Register for Historic Places") as a historical landmark as a result of its prior existence as the Earl Carroll Theater, a prominent [dinner theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinner_theater "Dinner theater"). It served as the production facilities for several Nickelodeon series.

### Nickelodeon Games

**Nickelodeon Games** (formerly **Nick Games** from 2002 to 2009, from 1997 to 2002, **Nickelodeon Software**, and from 1993 to 1997, **Nickelodeon Interactive**) is the video gaming division of Nickelodeon. It was originally a part of Viacom Consumer Products, with early games being published by [Viacom New Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932005) "Viacom (1952–2005)").[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-47) They started a long-standing relationship with game publisher [THQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/THQ "THQ"). THQ's relationship with the network started off when THQ published their _[Ren & Stimpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ren_&_Stimpy_Show "The Ren & Stimpy Show")_ game for [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo "Nintendo") consoles in 1992,[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-48) followed by a full-fledged console deal in 1998 with several _[Rugrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugrats "Rugrats")_ titles,[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-49) and expanded in 2001, when THQ acquired some of the assets from [Mattel Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattel_Interactive "Mattel Interactive"), namely the computer publishing rights, and all video game rights to _[The Wild Thornberrys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wild_Thornberrys "The Wild Thornberrys")_.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-50) Nickelodeon also worked, alongside THQ on an original game concept, _[Tak and the Power of Juju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tak_and_the_Power_of_Juju "Tak and the Power of Juju")_.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-51)

### Nick.com

Main article: [Nick.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick.com "Nick.com")

**Nick.com** is Nickelodeon's main [website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web"), which launched in October 1995 as a component of [America Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOL "AOL")'s Kids Only channel before eventually moving to the full World Wide Web.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-52) It provides content, as well as video clips and full episodes of Nickelodeon series available for streaming. The website's popularity grew to the point where in March 1999, Nick.com became the highest rated website among children aged 6–14 years old. Nickelodeon used the website in conjunction with television programs which increased traffic.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-53) In 2001, Nickelodeon partnered with Networks Inc. to provide [broadband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband "Broadband") [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game") for rent from Nick.com; the move was a further step in the multimedia direction that the developers wanted to take the website. Skagerlind indicated that over 50% of Nick.com's audience were using a [high speed connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_speed_internet "High speed internet"), which allowed them to expand the gaming and video streaming options on the website.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-54)

### Nickelodeon Movies

Main article: [Nickelodeon Movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Movies "Nickelodeon Movies")

**Nickelodeon Movies** is a [motion picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film "Film") production unit that was founded in 1995, as a family entertainment arm of [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") (owned by Nickelodeon's corporate parent, Paramount Global).[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200436-57) The first film released from the studio was the 1996 mystery/comedy _[Harriet the Spy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_the_Spy_(film) "Harriet the Spy (film)")_.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200439-58) Nickelodeon Movies has produced films based on Nickelodeon animated programs including _[The Rugrats Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rugrats_Movie "The Rugrats Movie")_ and _[The SpongeBob SquarePants Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_SpongeBob_SquarePants_Movie "The SpongeBob SquarePants Movie")_, as well as other adaptations and original live-action and animated projects.

### Nickelodeon Magazine

Main article: [Nickelodeon Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Magazine "Nickelodeon Magazine")

**Nickelodeon Magazine** was a print magazine that was launched in 1993; the channel had previously published a short-lived magazine effort in 1990. _Nickelodeon Magazine_ incorporated informative non-fiction pieces, humor (including pranks and parodical pieces), interviews, recipes (such as green slime cake), and a comic book section in the center of each issue featuring original comics by leading underground cartoonists as well as strips about popular Nicktoons.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendershot200455%E2%80%9356-59) It ended in December 2009, after 16 years, citing a sluggish magazine industry.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-60) A new version of the magazine was published by [Papercutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papercutz_(publisher) "Papercutz (publisher)") from June 2015[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-61) to mid-2016.

### Nick Radio

**Nick Radio** was a [radio network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_network "Radio network") that launched on September 30, 2013, in a partnership between both the network and [IHeartMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHeartMedia "IHeartMedia") (then called Clear Channel Communications), which distributed the network mainly via its [iHeartRadio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHeartRadio "IHeartRadio") web platform and [mobile app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_app "Mobile app"). The station was also streamed via the Nick.com website and [WHTZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHTZ "WHTZ")'s second [HD Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD_Radio "HD Radio") subchannel in the New York area. Nick Radio focused on [Top 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_40 "Top 40") and [pop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music") aimed at the network's target audience of children (with [radio edits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_edit "Radio edit") of some songs incorporated due to inappropriate content), along with celebrity interview features. In addition to regular on-air [DJs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_jockey "Disc jockey"), Nick Radio also occasionally featured guest DJ stints by popular artists as well as stars from Nickelodeon's original series.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-62)[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-63)[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-64)

Nick Radio shut down without warning on July 31, 2019, and was replaced by Hit Nation Junior, likely due to the network's general failure to establish any sustained "triple threat" artists/actors throughout the 2010s, along with the general failure of the children's-only radio format in the streaming age.

## Themed experiences and hotels

### Nickelodeon Universe

Main article: [Nickelodeon Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Universe "Nickelodeon Universe")

**Nickelodeon Universe** at the [Mall of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_of_America "Mall of America") is the first indoor Nickelodeon theme park in the United States. Before being re-themed to Nickelodeon in 2007, the park was themed as "Camp Snoopy" and "The Park at MoA." The theme park contains a variety of Nickelodeon-themed rides, including: [SpongeBob SquarePants: Rock Bottom Plunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpongeBob_SquarePants_Rock_Bottom_Plunge "SpongeBob SquarePants Rock Bottom Plunge"), [Fairly Odd Coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairly_Odd_Coaster "Fairly Odd Coaster"), and [Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Shell Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles_Shell_Shock "Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles Shell Shock").

Nickelodeon and [Triple Five Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Five_Group "Triple Five Group") opened a second Nickelodeon Universe theme park in the [American Dream Meadowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dream_Meadowlands "American Dream Meadowlands") complex on October 25, 2019.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-65) Upon opening, it became the largest indoor theme park in the western hemisphere, defeating the Mall of America's Nickelodeon Universe which had the title from 2008 to 2019.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-66)

On August 18, 2009, Nickelodeon and Southern Star Amusements announced that it would build a Nickelodeon Universe in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans"), Louisiana, on the site of the former [Six Flags New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_New_Orleans "Six Flags New Orleans") by the end of 2010,[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-67) which was set to be the first outdoor Nickelodeon Universe theme park. On November 9, 2009, Nickelodeon announced that it had ended the licensing agreement with Southern Star Amusements.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-68)[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon#cite_note-69)

- **[Nickelodeon Splat City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Splat_City "Nickelodeon Splat City")** was an area inside California's Great America (from 1995 to 2002), [Kings Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Island "Kings Island") (from 1995 to 2000) and Kings Dominion (from 1995 to 1999), that featured slime and water-themed attractions. ## International

Further information: [Paramount International Networks §Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_International_Networks#Nickelodeon "Paramount International Networks")

Between 1993 and 1995, Nickelodeon opened international channels in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_(British_and_Irish_TV_channel) "Nickelodeon (British and Irish TV channel)"), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_(Australia) "Nickelodeon (Australia)"), and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_(Germany) "Nickelodeon (Germany)"); by the later year, the network had provided its programming to broadcasters in 70 countries. Since the mid-1990s and early 2000s, Nickelodeon as a brand has expanded into include language- or culture-specific channels for various other territories in different parts of the world including Europe, Asia, [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania"), and Canada, and has licensed some of its cartoons and other content, in English and local languages, to free-to-air networks and subscription channels such as [KI.KA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KI.KA "KI.KA") and [Super RTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_RTL "Super RTL") in Germany, [RTÉ Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Two "RTÉ Two") (English language) and [TG4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TG4 "TG4") (Irish language) in Ireland, [YTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YTV_(Canadian_TV_channel) "YTV (Canadian TV channel)") (in English) and [Vrak.TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrak.TV "Vrak.TV") (in French, defunct) in Canada, [Canal J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_J "Canal J"), [TF1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TF1 "TF1"), [Teletoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teletoon+ "Teletoon+") and later, [Gulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulli "Gulli") in France, Alpha Kids in Greece, [CNBC-e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNBC-e "CNBC-e") in Turkey and [Network 10's localised version of Nickelodeon in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_(Australian_TV_channel) "Nickelodeon (Australian TV channel)").
