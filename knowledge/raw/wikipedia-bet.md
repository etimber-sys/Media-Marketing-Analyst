Black Entertainment Television

**Black Entertainment Television** ( **BET**) is an American [basic cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_cable "Basic cable") [network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_channel "Television channel") targeting [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans") audiences. It is the flagship channel of the [BET Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Media_Group "BET Media Group"), a subsidiary of [Paramount Skydance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Skydance "Paramount Skydance")'s [Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks "Paramount Media Networks") division. Launched as a [USA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Network "USA Network") [programming block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_programming "Block programming") on January 25, 1980, BET evolved into a full-fledged cable channel in September 1982.

As of November 2023[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit), BET is available to approximately 67,000,000 pay television households in the United States-down from its 2011 peak of 92,000,000 households.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-2)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Early years

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Early years")\]

After stepping down as a lobbyist for the cable industry, [Freeport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeport,_Illinois "Freeport, Illinois"), [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois "Illinois") native [Robert L. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_L._Johnson "Robert L. Johnson") decided to launch his own [cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television "Cable television") network. Johnson acquired a loan for $15,000 (equivalent to $55,648 in 2023) and a $500,000 (equivalent to $1,854,921 in 2023) investment from media executive [John Malone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Malone "John Malone") to start the network.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-Market_Nobody_Wanted-3) The network, which was named Black Entertainment Television (BET), launched on January 25, 1980.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-Corporate_Fact_Sheet-4) [Cheryl D. Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheryl_D._Miller "Cheryl D. Miller") designed the logo that would represent the network, which featured a star to symbolize "Black Star Power".[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-6)

Initially broadcasting for two hours a week as a block of programming on the Madison Square Garden Sports Network (which would change their name to [USA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Network "USA Network") three months after BET launched),[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-7) the network's lineup was composed of [music videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_videos "Music videos") and reruns of popular [black sitcoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_sitcom "Black sitcom").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-Market_Nobody_Wanted-3)

### Becoming a full-time channel

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Becoming a full-time channel")\]

In September 1982,[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-8) BET became a full-fledged entity, independent of any other channel or programming block, though continuing to share channel space with other cable networks on local cable systems due to lack of channel room for their 24-hour schedule until the time of [digital cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_cable "Digital cable") allowed for larger channel capacity.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-9) In some markets, the network would not arrive at all until as late as the early 2010s and Viacom considered it compulsory in [retransmission consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retransmission_consent "Retransmission consent") negotiations to carry the BET-branded networks with [Viacom Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_Media_Networks "Viacom Media Networks"), due to some providers claiming that there was an overall lack of demand for the channel, or excused their lack of interest in BET due to an alleged low to non-existent Black American population within their service area.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-10)

BET launched a news program, _BET News_, in 1986, with Paul Berry as its first anchor. Berry was also a local anchor at WJLA-TV in Washington, DC at that time. Ed Gordon became anchor in 1988. Gordon later hosted other programs and specials on BET, such as _For Black Men Only: The Aftermath_, related to the [1992 Los Angeles riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Los_Angeles_riots "1992 Los Angeles riots"), and a recurring interview show, _Conversations with Ed Gordon_.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-11) In 1996, the talk show _BET Tonight_ started with [Tavis Smiley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavis_Smiley "Tavis Smiley") as host; in 2001, Ed Gordon replaced Smiley as host of the program.

In 1991, the network became the first black-controlled television company to be listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange "New York Stock Exchange").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-Market_Nobody_Wanted-3) Starting the late 1990s, the network expanded with the launch of [digital cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_cable "Digital cable") networks: what is now the general interest channel [BET Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Her "BET Her") was initially launched as "BET on Jazz" (later known as "BET Jazz", "BET J", and "Centric"), created initially to showcase jazz music-related programming, especially that of black American jazz musicians. In 1997, BET entered into a [joint venture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_venture "Joint venture") with [Starz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starz_(TV_channel) "Starz (TV channel)") (then-owned by John Malone's [Liberty Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Media "Liberty Media"), but later acquired by Lionsgate years later) to launch a multiplex service of the [premium channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_television "Pay television") featuring black American-oriented films called "BET Movies: Starz! 3" (later renamed "Black Starz" after BET dropped out of the venture following its purchase by Viacom, then-owner of Starz rival [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_(TV_channel) "Showtime (TV channel)"), and now known as "Starz InBlack").

### Sale to Viacom/Paramount

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Sale to Viacom/Paramount")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/BET_Logo.svg/250px-BET_Logo.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BET_Logo.svg)

The network's logo from 2005 to 2021; shown here is the 2010 revision. Earlier on, the star was outlined.

In 2000, media conglomerate [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(original) "Viacom (original)") (later to become part of [Paramount Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Skydance "Paramount Skydance")) purchased BET for $2.3 billion.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-12) In 2005, Johnson retired from the network, turning over his titles of president and chief executive officer to former BET vice president [Debra L. Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debra_L._Lee "Debra L. Lee").

In 2002, the network had launched two more music-oriented networks: BET Hip-Hop and [BET Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#BET_Gospel). BET also launched a series of original programming by this time, including reality shows _[Baldwin Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_Hills_(TV_series) "Baldwin Hills (TV series)")_ and _[Hell Date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_Date "Hell Date")_, competition show _[Sunday Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_Best_(American_TV_series) "Sunday Best (American TV series)")_, and town hall-style discussion show _Hip Hop vs. America_.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-confidence-13) BET's president of entertainment [Reginald Hudlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Hudlin "Reginald Hudlin") resigned from the network on September 11, 2008. He was then replaced by Stephen Hill, who is also executive vice president of music programming and talent.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-14) BET announced in March 2010 that Ed Gordon would return to the network to host "a variety of news programs and specials".[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-15)

In March 2017, president of programming Stephen Hill and executive vice president of original programming Zola Mashariki both stepped down. Connie Orlando, senior vice president of Specials, Music Programming, and News was named the interim president of programming.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-16)

In July 2017, Viacom signed new film and television development deals with Academy Award winner [Tyler Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Perry "Tyler Perry") following the expiration of his existing pact with [Discovery Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc. "Discovery, Inc.") in 2019. As part of this deal, Perry would produce _[The Oval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oval_(TV_series) "The Oval (TV series)")_ and _[Sistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistas_(TV_series) "Sistas (TV series)")_ for BET and co-own the network's newly launched streaming service, [BET+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET%2B "BET+").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-17)

## Criticism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Criticism")\]

A wide range of people have protested elements of BET's programming and actions, including [Public Enemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Enemy_(band) "Public Enemy (band)") rapper [Chuck D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_D "Chuck D"),[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-18) journalist [George Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_E._Curry "George E. Curry"),[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-19) writer [Keith Boykin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Boykin "Keith Boykin"),[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-20) comic book creator [Christopher Priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Priest_(comic_book_writer) "Christopher Priest (comic book writer)"),[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-21) filmmaker [Spike Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_Lee "Spike Lee"),[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-22) [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University "Syracuse University") professor of finance [Dr. Boyce Watkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyce_Watkins "Boyce Watkins"),[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-23) former NFL player [Burgess Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgess_Owens "Burgess Owens"),[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-24) and cartoonist [Aaron McGruder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_McGruder "Aaron McGruder") (who, in addition to numerous critical references throughout his series _[The Boondocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boondocks_(TV_series) "The Boondocks (TV series)")_, made two particular episodes, " [The Hunger Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Strike "The Hunger Strike")" and " [The Uncle Ruckus Reality Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Uncle_Ruckus_Reality_Show "The Uncle Ruckus Reality Show")", criticizing the channel). As a result, BET heavily censors suggestive content from the videos that it airs, often with entire verses and scenes removed from certain rap videos.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-Protesting-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-Take_On-26)

Many scholars within the black American community maintain that BET perpetuates and justifies racism by affecting the stereotypes held about black Americans, and also by affecting the psyche of its young viewers through its bombardment of negative images of black Americans.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-27)

Following the death of civil rights leader [Coretta Scott King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coretta_Scott_King "Coretta Scott King") in 2006, BET broadcast its regularly scheduled music video programming, rather than covering King's funeral live, as was done by [TV One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_One_(US_TV_network) "TV One (US TV network)") and [Black Family Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Family_Channel "Black Family Channel"), and by cable news channels such as [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN"), [Fox News Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News_Channel "Fox News Channel"), and [MSNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC "MSNBC"). The network's website streamed the funeral live, while it periodically broadcast taped, 60-second reports from the funeral by senior news correspondent Andre Showell. Michael Lewellen, BET's senior vice president for corporate communications, defended the decision: "We weighed a number of different options. In the end, we chose to offer a different kind of experience for BET viewers." Lewellen also explained that BET received around "two dozen" phone calls and "a handful" of emails criticizing BET for not showing the King funeral live.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-CSK-28) On the evening of the funeral, February 7, 2006, BET broadcast the tribute special _Coretta Scott King: Married to the Mission_, and repeated it the following Sunday, February 12.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-29) Showell hosted the program featuring highlights of the funeral, _Coretta Scott King: Celebrating Her Spirit_, that broadcast that same day.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-30) In its 2007 convention, the [National Association of Black Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Black_Journalists "National Association of Black Journalists") gave BET its "Thumbs Down Award" for not broadcasting King's funeral live.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-thumbs_down-31)

_[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ reported that the Reverend [Delman L. Coates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delman_Coates "Delman Coates") and his organization Enough is Enough led protests every weekend outside the residences of BET executives against what they claim are negative [stereotypes of black people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotypes_of_African_Americans "Stereotypes of African Americans") perpetuated by BET music videos.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-Protesting-25) Enough is Enough backed an April 2008 report titled _The Rap on Rap_ by the [Parents Television Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parents_Television_Council "Parents Television Council") that criticized BET's rap programming, suggesting that the gratuitous sexual, violent and profane content was targeting children and teens.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-32)

In a 2010 interview, BET co-founder [Sheila Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Johnson "Sheila Johnson") explained that she herself is "ashamed" of what the network has become. "I don't watch it. I suggest to my kids that they don't watch it," she said. "When we started BET, it was going to be the _[Ebony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebony_(magazine) "Ebony (magazine)")_ magazine on television. We had public affairs programming. We had news... I had a show called _[Teen Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teen_Summit "Teen Summit")_, we had a large variety of programming, but the problem is that then the video revolution started up... And then something started happening, and I didn't like it at all. And I remember during those days we would sit up and watch these videos and decide which ones were going on and which ones were not. We got a lot of backlash from recording artists...and we had to start showing them. I didn't like the way women were being portrayed in these videos."[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-33)

### Spin-off channels

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Spin-off channels")\]

BET has launched several spin-off cable networks over the years, including [BET Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Her "BET Her") (formerly known as "BET on Jazz", then "BET J" and later "Centric"), BET Hip-Hop, and BET Gospel. Over time, spin-offs from sibling channels would be realigned under the BET branding; such as BET Jams (formerly known as " [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV") Jams"), [BET Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Soul "BET Soul") (formerly known as "VH1 Soul"), SHO×BET, a premium [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network) "Showtime (TV network)") multiplex network, and [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1") (an older-skewing spin-off of MTV that drifted into reality shows and, later, Black American-centric programming).

In May 2019, a BET-branded channel was launched on [Pluto TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto_TV "Pluto TV") (which was acquired by its parent company two months earlier).[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-34) In June 2019, the launch of [BET+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET%2B "BET+") was announced, a premium [streaming service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_service "Streaming service") targeting Black Americans. The service launched in the United States in Fall 2019 with _[First Wives Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Wives_Club_(TV_series) "First Wives Club (TV series)")_ (which was originally planned to launch on [Paramount Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Network "Paramount Network") before being shifted to BET) announced as one of the service's original series.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-35)

BET Jams

**BET Jams** is an American pay television network airing [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop_music "Hip hop music") and [urban contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_contemporary "Urban contemporary") [music videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_video "Music video") on a thrice-daily automated wheel schedule of eight hours outside of temporary "roadblock" closures during Paramount Global's awards events, with all of its programming currently denoted in hour blocks as _BET Jams – Music Videos_ within [electronic program guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_program_guide "Electronic program guide") listings.

The channel launched on May 1, 2002, as [MTV Jams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Jams "MTV Jams"), and carried that name until October 5, 2015, and was placed under BET's purview as MTV drifted away from music programming along with _106 & Park_. The network space itself launched on August 1, 1998, as [MTVX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTVX "MTVX"), carrying modern rock videos, and was re-focused around hip-hop music on that date, to some controversy from MTVX's former viewers.

#### BET International

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: BET International")\]

Main article: [BET International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_International "BET International")

BET UK first transmitted on Videotron (now known as Virgin Media) and several other subscription providers from 1993 until 1996.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-36)

In May 2007 by [Ofcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofcom "Ofcom"), BET International Inc. was given a license to rebroadcast in the United Kingdom. [BET International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_International "BET International") is the first international version of the channel and is available in Europe, Africa and the Middle East through satellite providers. BET launched on February 27, 2008, on Sky channel 191 and began to be carried by [Freesat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freesat "Freesat") channel 140 on August 8, 2008. BET+1 is also available on Sky channel 198 and Freesat channel 141, and is free-to-air. [BET International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_International "BET International") shows with a mix of content from the main BET channel and locally produced shows. An exclusive, but temporary, HD version of the channel was made to show the 2009 BET Awards on Freesat EPG 142.

BET is additionally an associate member of the Caribbean Cable Cooperative.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-37)

BET launched an app called BET Play allowing international access to BET content in over 100 countries in June 2016.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-38)

The channel was shut down on April 8, 2021, with its content moved to [My5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My5 "My5") and [Pluto TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto_TV "Pluto TV").

The channel was shut down on January 1, 2026 in [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa"), [Sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa") and [MENA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_and_North_Africa "Middle East and North Africa") following its restructuring policy by [Paramount Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Skydance "Paramount Skydance") due to low ratings.

#### Canada

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Canada")\]

BET became available through most Canadian pay television providers on October 17, 1997, though with select programming substituted and [blacked out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackout_(broadcasting) "Blackout (broadcasting)") due to domestic broadcast groups owning the rights the shows being broadcast and carrying them on another of their networks.

In 2022, Paramount began to phase out BET in Canada due to expiring rights, a number of major providers began dropping the channel, beginning with [Rogers Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Communications "Rogers Communications") on April 1, 2022, followed by Hay Communications on September 30, 2022, and [Shaw Cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Communications "Shaw Communications") and [Shaw Direct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Direct "Shaw Direct") on August 31, 2023.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-39) The Canadian feed was discontinued on all other providers nationwide on January 1, 2025.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-40)\[ _[new archival link needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Archiving_a_source "Help:Archiving a source")_\]

#### France

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=BET&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: France")\]

Main article: [BET (France)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_(France) "BET (France)")

Introduced on November 17, 2015. BET France launched across a linear television channel alongside non-linear services including Bouygues Telecom, Canalsat, Numericable/SFR, and Free.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET#cite_note-41)
