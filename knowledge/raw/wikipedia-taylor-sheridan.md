**Sheridan Taylor Gibler Jr.** (born May 21, 1970),[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-TV_Insider_1970-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-3) known professionally as **Taylor Sheridan**, is an American writer, producer, director, and actor. He is best known as the co-creator of the television series _[Yellowstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_(TV_series) "Yellowstone (TV series)")_ and creator of its prequels _[1883](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1883_(TV_series) "1883 (TV series)")_ (2021) and _[1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_(TV_series) "1923 (TV series)")_ (2022).

Sheridan has written several films, including the screenplay for _[Sicario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicario_(2015_film) "Sicario (2015 film)")_ (2015), for which he was nominated for the [Writers Guild of America Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America_Award "Writers Guild of America Award") for [Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Writers Guild of America Award for Best Original Screenplay"). He was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay") for _[Hell or High Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_or_High_Water_(2016_film) "Hell or High Water (2016 film)")_ (2016), which was nominated for three other [Oscars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"). Sheridan also wrote and directed the 2017 [neo-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_(genre)#Contemporary_Western "Western (genre)") crime film _[Wind River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_River_(film) "Wind River (film)")_ and wrote [the 2018 sequel to _Sicario_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicario:_Day_of_the_Soldado "Sicario: Day of the Soldado").

Sheridan got his start portraying Danny Boyd in _[Veronica Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_Mars "Veronica Mars")_ (2005–2007) as well as portraying [David Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sons_of_Anarchy_and_Mayans_M.C._characters#Deputy_David_Hale "List of Sons of Anarchy and Mayans M.C. characters") in the [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)") television series _[Sons of Anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Anarchy "Sons of Anarchy")_. He has since created several series for [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount%2B "Paramount+"), including the crime thriller _[Mayor of Kingstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Kingstown "Mayor of Kingstown")_, the crime drama _[Tulsa King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_King "Tulsa King")_ (which he co-writes with [Terence Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_Winter "Terence Winter")),[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-4) the espionage thriller _[Lioness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lioness_(American_TV_series) "Lioness (American TV series)")_ and the drama _[Landman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landman_(TV_series) "Landman (TV series)")_.

In 2021, Sheridan was inducted into the [Texas Cowboy Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Cowboy_Hall_of_Fame "Texas Cowboy Hall of Fame").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-5) In 2024, Sheridan was inducted into the Texas Business Hall of Fame.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-6) He is an honorary graduate at [Texas Christian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Christian_University "Texas Christian University").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-7) He was also awarded an honorary doctorate by [Texas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_University "Texas State University").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-8)

## Early life and education

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Taylor_Sheridan&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")\]

Sheridan was born in [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina "Chapel Hill, North Carolina").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-Kendall-9) Several news articles have reported that he grew up on a ranch in [Cranfills Gap, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranfills_Gap,_Texas "Cranfills Gap, Texas"), but he was raised in [Fort Worth, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas "Fort Worth, Texas"), the son of a cardiologist.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-Kendall-9) His cowboy identity comes from his mother, who was originally from [Waco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waco,_Texas "Waco, Texas") and loved visiting her grandparents' ranch in that area.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-Corcoran-10) When Sheridan was eight years old, his mother insisted on buying a ranch in Cranfills Gap so that her children would "learn firsthand about the peaceful feeling of freedom in nature".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-Corcoran-10) Sheridan learned how to be a cowboy during his family's frequent visits to the Cranfills Gap ranch when he was growing up in the late 1980s.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-Kendall-9) Meanwhile, he attended and graduated from [R. L. Paschal High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._L._Paschal_High_School "R. L. Paschal High School"), where he was "the rare weekend wrangler who was also a [theater kid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_kid "Theater kid")".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-Corcoran-10)

Sheridan attended [Texas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_University "Texas State University") as a [Theater Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_Arts "Theater Arts") major but dropped out after his junior year. He later moved to [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"), where he supported himself by mowing lawns and painting houses. While looking for jobs in a shopping mall, Sheridan met a talent scout, who offered him the chance to go to [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") and pursue an acting career. He later lived in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") and [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") during his time as an actor.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-12)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Taylor_Sheridan&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

Sheridan began his career in acting, appearing in small films and in recurring roles in television series like _[Veronica Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_Mars "Veronica Mars")_, _[Walker, Texas Ranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker,_Texas_Ranger "Walker, Texas Ranger")_ and most notably, as [David Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sons_of_Anarchy_and_Mayans_M.C._characters#Deputy_David_Hale "List of Sons of Anarchy and Mayans M.C. characters") in _[Sons of Anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Anarchy "Sons of Anarchy")_. He made the transition into screenwriting after he turned 40.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-13) His first film as a screenwriter was _[Sicario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicario_(2015_film) "Sicario (2015 film)")_, directed by [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve "Denis Villeneuve").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-uproxx-14) It revolves around Kate Macer ( [Emily Blunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Blunt "Emily Blunt")), an FBI agent who is enlisted to a government task force to bring down the leader of a powerful and brutal [Mexican drug cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_drug_cartel "Mexican drug cartel").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-variety-incontention-16) It also starred [Josh Brolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Brolin "Josh Brolin") and [Benicio del Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benicio_del_Toro "Benicio del Toro"). The film received critical acclaim, with a 92% approval rating on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"),[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-17) and received a [number of nominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_Sicario_(2015_film) "List of accolades received by Sicario (2015 film)"), including a [Writers Guild of America Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America_Award "Writers Guild of America Award") nomination for [Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Writers Guild of America Award for Best Original Screenplay") for Sheridan.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-variety-wgasicario-19)

Sheridan wrote _Comancheria_ after _Sicario_. _Comancheria_ sold first but was [stuck in development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_hell "Development hell") for quite a few years, appearing on the [Black List survey of most liked screenplays not yet produced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_List_(survey) "Black List (survey)") in 2012. It was later retitled [_Hell or High Water_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_or_High_Water_(film) "Hell or High Water (film)") and was released in August 2016, starring [Jeff Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bridges "Jeff Bridges"), [Chris Pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Pine "Chris Pine") and [Ben Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Foster_(actor) "Ben Foster (actor)"), and again received critical acclaim.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-21) For his screenplay, Sheridan received a large amount of awards attention, earning [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA "BAFTA"), [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award"), and [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") nominations.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-23)

The low-budget horror film _[Vile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vile_(film) "Vile (film)")_ is credited as Sheridan's first film, but he does not consider the film his directorial debut, stating in a 2017 [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") interview:

> I would say this \[ _Wind River_\] is my feature debut. A friend of mine raised – I don't know what he raised – 20 grand or something, and cast his buddies, and wrote this bad horror movie, that I told him not to direct. He was going to direct it and produce it, and he started and freaked out, and called and said, "Can you help me?" I said, "Yeah, I'll try."
> I kind of kept the ship pointed straight, and they went off and edited, and did what they did. I think it's generous to call me the director. I think he was try \[ _[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_\] to say thank you, in some way. It was an excellent opportunity to point a camera and learn some lessons that actually benefited me on _Wind River_.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-24)

His second feature as director, and third as screenwriter, _[Wind River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_River_(film) "Wind River (film)")_, starring [Jeremy Renner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Renner "Jeremy Renner") and [Elizabeth Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Olsen "Elizabeth Olsen"), premiered at the [Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival "Sundance Film Festival") in January 2017. The film follows an FBI agent (Olsen) and a veteran game tracker (Renner) investigating a murder that occurred on a Native American reservation. [The Weinstein Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weinstein_Company "The Weinstein Company") had acquired the distribution rights during the 2016 [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival "Cannes Film Festival"),[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-25) but dropped the film prior to the Sundance premiere.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-26) However, the company later finalized its deal to distribute it.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-27) _Wind River_ was widely released in the United States on August 18, 2017, following a brief limited release.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-28) Following _Sicario_ and _Hell or High Water_, _Wind River_ is the third installment of Sheridan's trilogy of "the modern-day American frontier".[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-29)

On September 15, 2016, _[Deadline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood")_ reported that Sheridan had been set by [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures") and [Escape Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_Artists "Escape Artists") to script the American remake of the [Matthias Schoenaerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Schoenaerts "Matthias Schoenaerts") drama-thriller film _[Disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disorder_(2015_film) "Disorder (2015 film)")_, a 2015 French film directed by [Alice Winocour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Winocour "Alice Winocour"). Escape Artists' Todd Black, Jason Blumenthal, [Steve Tisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Tisch "Steve Tisch") and Tony Shaw were scheduled to produce the remake and David Beaubaire to oversee it for the studio.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-30) [James Mangold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mangold "James Mangold") was going to direct.

In 2017, Sheridan created the television series _[Yellowstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_(TV_series) "Yellowstone (TV series)")_, starring [Kevin Costner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Costner "Kevin Costner"), which aired on [Paramount Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Network "Paramount Network") for five seasons running beginning June 20, 2018.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-31) Spinoffs include _[1883](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1883_(TV_series) "1883 (TV series)")_ and _[1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_(TV_series) "1923 (TV series)") on Paramount+._

Sheridan wrote the sequel to _Sicario_, titled _[Sicario: Day of the Soldado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicario:_Day_of_the_Soldado "Sicario: Day of the Soldado")_, which was directed by [Stefano Sollima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_Sollima "Stefano Sollima") and released in 2018.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-32) More recently, his overall deal with ViacomCBS was renewed.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-33)

In May 2019, it was announced [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures") and [New Line Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Line_Cinema "New Line Cinema") acquired distribution rights to the film _[Those Who Wish Me Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Those_Who_Wish_Me_Dead "Those Who Wish Me Dead")_ with Sheridan as director.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-34) The film had a theatrical debut internationally in South Korea on May 5, 2021.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-koreajoongang-35) In the United States, it was released on May 14, 2021.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-36)

Owing partly to the success of the [_Yellowstone_ franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_(franchise) "Yellowstone (franchise)"), Sheridan expanded with the subsequent TV productions _[Tulsa King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_King "Tulsa King")_ starring [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone "Sylvester Stallone"), which has been renewed through season 4, and _[Landman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landman_(TV_series) "Landman (TV series)")_ starring [Billy Bob Thornton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bob_Thornton "Billy Bob Thornton").[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-37)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-38)

On June 23, 2025, Paulynne, Inc., which owns and controls all of famed broadcaster [Paul Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Harvey "Paul Harvey")'s intellectual property, sued Paramount Global in New York federal court. The company sued Paramount for using a 90 second audio clip from _[The Rest of the Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rest_of_the_Story "The Rest of the Story")_ in _Landman_ without permission. Paramount used a segment about rising gas prices from Harvey's 2009 "Gas Crisis" episode in the opening of the Season 1 finale. The lawsuit accused Paramount of failing to obtain permission to use the clip as well as editing the clip to change Harvey's viewpoint with regard to government fossil-fuel policies and his interest in alternative fuels.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-39)

On June 5, 2025, it was announced that _NOLA King_, a spin-off series of _Tulsa King_, was in the works at Paramount+. The series will be set in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans") and will star [Samuel L. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_L._Jackson "Samuel L. Jackson") in a similar role as the one Stallone plays in _Tulsa King_. Jackson appeared in the third season of _Tulsa King_.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-40)

On February 24th. 2026, it was reported by [The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter") that Paramount+ had announced that the spinoff was moving away from New Orleans in favor of Texas and the name of the show changed to _Frisco King_ instead of _NOLA King_. In addition, it was announced that Sheridan had been tapped to write all eight episodes of the first season. [\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-41)

On October 28, 2025, it was reported that Sheridan had signed a five-year overall deal with [NBCUniversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCUniversal "NBCUniversal"), which will take effect after his current deals with Paramount expire in March 2026 for film, and 2028 for television. The deal was described as being a "coup" for the company, coming shortly after [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media "Skydance Media") completed its acquisition of Paramount; CEO [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison "David Ellison") had previously praised Sheridan as being a "singular genius", and had hoped that he would "call Paramount his home for as long as he wants to be telling stories."[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-42)[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-43)

## Style

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Taylor_Sheridan&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Style")\]

As an actor, Sheridan explained that the amount of expositional dialogue he read for television caused him to form an "allergy to [exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposition_(narrative) "Exposition (narrative)")" in his writing. He has also said that he looks for "absurdly simple" plots to focus solely on character.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-44) He has cited the [Coen brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coen_brothers "Coen brothers"), [Cormac McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cormac_McCarthy "Cormac McCarthy"), and [Larry McMurtry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_McMurtry "Larry McMurtry") as influential to his writing.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-45)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Taylor_Sheridan&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Sheridan has been married to actress and model [Nicole Muirbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Muirbrook "Nicole Muirbrook") since 2013. The pair have a son named Gus.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-46)[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-47) They currently reside in [Weatherford, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weatherford,_Texas "Weatherford, Texas").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-Kendall-9)

In May 2021, a buyer group represented by Sheridan purchased the 350,000-acre [6666 Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6666_Ranch "6666 Ranch") in west Texas.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-48)[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-49) In 2025, Sheridan bought [Cattlemen's Steak House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattlemen%27s_Steak_House "Cattlemen's Steak House") in [Fort Worth, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas "Fort Worth, Texas"), with two business partners.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan#cite_note-50)
