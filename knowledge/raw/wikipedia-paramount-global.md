Paramount Global

**Paramount Global**, also known by its [trade name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_name "Trade name") as simply **Paramount** and formerly **ViacomCBS**, was an American multinational [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media "Mass media") and [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment "Entertainment") [conglomerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_(company) "Conglomerate (company)") controlled by [National Amusements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Amusements "National Amusements") and [headquartered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headquarters "Headquarters") at [One Astor Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Astor_Plaza "One Astor Plaza") in [Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square "Times Square"), Midtown Manhattan that was in operation from December 4, 2019 to August 7, 2025.

Established through the [merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_merger_of_CBS_and_Viacom "2019 merger of CBS and Viacom") of the second incarnations of [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(2005%E2%80%932019) "Viacom (2005–2019)") and [CBS Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation "CBS Corporation"),[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-mergerdate-4) which were [split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_of_CBS_Corporation_and_Viacom "Split of CBS Corporation and Viacom") from the [original Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932005) "Viacom (1952–2005)") on December 31, 2005, it took its latest name on February 16, 2022.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-5) Paramount's main properties include the namesake [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") Corporation, the [CBS Entertainment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Entertainment_Group "CBS Entertainment Group") (consisting of the [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") television network and [television stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_and_Stations "CBS News and Stations") as well as [The CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW "The CW") and other CBS-branded assets), the [BET Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Media_Group "BET Media Group") (which oversees [BET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET "BET") and its sister channels), [Paramount Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks "Paramount Media Networks") (consisting of locally-based cable television networks including [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV"), [Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon "Nickelodeon"), [Comedy Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_Central "Comedy Central"), [CMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMT_(American_TV_channel) "CMT (American TV channel)"), [Paramount Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Network "Paramount Network") and [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network) "Showtime (TV network)")) and [Paramount Streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Streaming "Paramount Streaming") (including [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+ "Paramount+") and [Pluto TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto_TV "Pluto TV")). It also has an [international division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_International_Networks "Paramount International Networks") that manages international versions of its cable networks, as well as region-specific assets including Argentina's [Telefe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telefe "Telefe"), Chile's [Chilevisión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilevisi%C3%B3n "Chilevisión"), the United Kingdom's [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_(British_TV_channel) "5 (British TV channel)") and Australia's [Network 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_10 "Network 10"). From 2011 to 2023, the division also owned a 30% stake in [Rainbow S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_(company) "Rainbow (company)") of Italy.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-6)As of 2019,[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Paramount_Global&action=edit) the company operates over 170 networks and reaches approximately 700 million subscribers in 180 countries.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-7)

In 2024, National Amusements held talks for a potential merger or acquisition of Paramount Global, with [Warner Bros. Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery "Warner Bros. Discovery"), [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures"), [Apollo Global Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Global_Management "Apollo Global Management"), [Edgar Bronfman Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Bronfman_Jr. "Edgar Bronfman Jr."), [Allen Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Media_Group "Allen Media Group"), and [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media "Skydance Media") all considering acquiring the company. By June 3, Paramount reportedly agreed to merger terms with Skydance. However, by June 11, merger talks between Paramount and Skydance had fallen apart, resulting in the proposed merger being called off. The companies would later re-negotiate the deal, and on July 2, 2024, Skydance reached [a preliminary agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Skydance_Media_and_Paramount_Global "Merger of Skydance Media and Paramount Global") to form " [Paramount Skydance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Skydance "Paramount Skydance")" through a three-way merger between it, National Amusements and Paramount.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-mergerdeal-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-9) On July 24, 2025 the merger was approved, over a year after a merger agreement.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-12)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-13) The merger was closed by August 7, 2025.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-16)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-17)

## Background

Main articles: [Gulf and Western Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_and_Western_Industries "Gulf and Western Industries"), [Viacom (1952–2005)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932005) "Viacom (1952–2005)"), and [Westinghouse Electric Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Electric_Corporation "Westinghouse Electric Corporation")

[Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures"), [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") and [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932006) "Viacom (1952–2006)") each had a history of being associated with one another through a series of various corporate mergers and splits.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-newsweek-18) Paramount Pictures was founded in 1912 as the [Famous Players Film Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famous_Players_Film_Company "Famous Players Film Company").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-19) CBS was founded in 1927, which Paramount Pictures held a 49% ownership stake in from 1929 to 1932.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-21) In 1952, CBS formed CBS Television Film Sales, a division which handled syndication rights for CBS's library of network-owned television series. This division was renamed CBS Films in 1958, again renamed CBS Enterprises in January 1968, and finally renamed [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932006) "Viacom (1952–2006)") (an acronym of Video and Audio Communications) in 1970. In 1971, this syndication division was [spun off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off "Corporate spin-off") amid new [FCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission "Federal Communications Commission") rules forbidding television networks from [owning syndication companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Interest_and_Syndication_Rules "Financial Interest and Syndication Rules") (these rules were eventually abolished completely in 1993).[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-22) In 1986, Viacom purchased [MTV Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks "Paramount Media Networks") and [Showtime/The Movie Channel Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_Networks "Showtime Networks") from [Warner Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia "WarnerMedia") and [American Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Express "American Express").[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-23) In 1987, Viacom was acquired by theater operator company [National Amusements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Amusements "National Amusements").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-24)

### Paramount Communications (1989–1994) and Viacom Inc. and CBS Corporation (1994–2005)

Main articles: [Paramount Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Communications "Paramount Communications") and [CBS Corporation (1997–2000)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation_(1997%E2%80%932000) "CBS Corporation (1997–2000)")

Meanwhile, Paramount Pictures was acquired by [Gulf and Western Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_and_Western_Industries "Gulf and Western Industries") in 1966, which then re-branded itself as [Paramount Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Communications "Paramount Communications") in 1989.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-25) Viacom then purchased Paramount Communications in 1994. In 1999, Viacom made its biggest acquisition to date by announcing plans to merge with its former parent CBS Corporation (the renamed [Westinghouse Electric Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Electric_Corporation "Westinghouse Electric Corporation"), which had merged with CBS in 1995). The merger was completed in 2000, resulting in CBS reuniting with its former syndication division.

### Viacom Inc. and CBS Corporation (2005–2019)

Further information: [Split of CBS Corporation and Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_of_CBS_Corporation_and_Viacom "Split of CBS Corporation and Viacom")

Main articles: [Viacom (2005–2019)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(2005%E2%80%932019) "Viacom (2005–2019)") and [CBS Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation "CBS Corporation")

On December 31, 2005, Viacom was split into two companies: [the second incarnation of CBS Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation "CBS Corporation"), the former's corporate successor, and [the second incarnation of Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(2005%E2%80%932019) "Viacom (2005–2019)"), which was formed as a spin-off.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-26)

Evolution of Paramount Skydance

### Formation

On September 29, 2016, [National Amusements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Amusements "National Amusements") wrote to its in-control subsidiaries, Viacom and CBS Corporation, encouraging a merger.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-27) On December 12, the deal was called off.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-28)

On January 12, 2018, [CNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNBC "CNBC") reported that Viacom had re-entered talks to merge back into CBS Corporation, after the merger of [AT&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T "AT&T") and [Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia "WarnerMedia") and [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company")'s [then-proposed acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_21st_Century_Fox_by_Disney "Acquisition of 21st Century Fox by Disney") of most of [21st Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Fox "21st Century Fox")'s assets were announced. Viacom and CBS Corporation also faced heavy competition from companies such as [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") and [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_(company) "Amazon (company)").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-29) Shortly afterward, it was reported that the combined company could be a suitor for acquiring the media company Lionsgate ( _now_ [Starz Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starz_Entertainment "Starz Entertainment")) .[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-30) Viacom and Lionsgate were both interested in acquiring [The Weinstein Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weinstein_Company "The Weinstein Company") (TWC).[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-thewrap.com-31) Following the [Weinstein effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weinstein_effect "Weinstein effect"), Viacom was listed as one of 22 potential buyers that were interested in acquiring TWC.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-thewrap.com-31) They lost the bid, and on March 1, 2018, it was announced that [Maria Contreras-Sweet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Contreras-Sweet "Maria Contreras-Sweet") would acquire all of TWC's assets for $500million.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-latimes20180301-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-deadline1202307564-33) Lantern Capital would later acquire the studio.

On March 30, 2018, CBS Corporation made an all-stock offer slightly below Viacom's market value, insisting that its existing leadership, including long-time chairman and CEO [Les Moonves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Moonves "Les Moonves"), oversee the re-combined company. Viacom rejected the offer as too low, requesting a $2.8 billion increase and that [Bob Bakish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bakish "Robert Bakish") be maintained as president and COO under Moonves. These conflicts had resulted from [Shari Redstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shari_Redstone "Shari Redstone") seeking more control over CBS Corporation and its leadership.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-34)[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-35)

Eventually, on May 14, 2018, CBS Corporation sued its and Viacom's parent company National Amusements and accused Redstone of abusing her voting power in the company and [forcing a merger that was not supported by it or Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostile_takeover "Hostile takeover").[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-36)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-37) CBS Corporation also accused Redstone of discouraging [Verizon Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verizon_Communications "Verizon Communications") from acquiring it, which could have been beneficial to its shareholders.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-38)

On May 23, 2018, Les Moonves explained that he considered the Viacom channels to be an " [albatross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albatross_(metaphor) "Albatross (metaphor)")," and while he favored more content for [CBS All Access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_All_Access "CBS All Access") (now Paramount+), he believed that there were better deals for CBS Corporation than the Viacom deal, such as [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer "Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer") (MGM), Lionsgate, or [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures"). Moonves also considered Bakish a threat because he did not want an ally of Shari Redstone as a board member of the combined company.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-39)

On September 9, 2018, Les Moonves exited CBS Corporation following multiple accusations of sexual assault. National Amusements agreed to not propose a CBS Corporation-Viacom merger for at least two years after the date of the settlement.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-40)

On May 30, 2019, CNBC reported that CBS Corporation and Viacom would explore merger discussions in mid-June 2019. CBS Corporation's board of directors was revamped with people who were open to merging; the re-merger was made possible with the resignation of Moonves, who had opposed all merger attempts. The talks had started following rumors of CBS Corporation acquiring [Starz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starz_Inc. "Starz Inc.") from Lionsgate.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-41) Reports said that CBS Corporation and Viacom reportedly set August 8 as an informal deadline for reaching an agreement to recombine the two media companies.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-42)[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-43) CBS Corporation announced its plans to acquire Viacom as part of the re-merger deal for up to $15.4billion.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-44)

On August 2, 2019, it was reported that CBS Corporation and Viacom agreed to merge back into one entity, with both companies agreeing on the management team for the merger. Bob Bakish would serve as CEO of the combined company with the president and acting CEO of CBS Corporation, [Joseph Ianniello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ianniello "Joseph Ianniello"), overseeing CBS Corporation-branded assets.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-45) On August 7, 2019, CBS and Viacom separately reported their quarterly earnings as the talks about the re-merger continued.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-46)[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-47)

### Initial operations

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/ViacomCBS.svg/500px-ViacomCBS.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ViacomCBS.svg) ViacomCBS' logo used from 2019 to 2022; the logo's colors were initially inverted from 2019 to 2020

On August 13, 2019, CBS and Viacom officially announced their merger; the combined company was to be named ViacomCBS, with Shari Redstone serving as chair.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-48)[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-49)[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-ViacomCBSOfficialPDF-50) Upon the merger agreement, Viacom and CBS jointly announced that the transaction is expected to close by the end of 2019, pending regulatory and shareholder approvals.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-ViacomCBSOfficialPDF-50) The merger required approval by the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission "Federal Trade Commission") (FTC).[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-ViacomCBSOfficialPDF-50)

On October 28, 2019, the merger was approved by National Amusements, which then announced the deal would close in early December; the recombined company trades its shares on [Nasdaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq "Nasdaq") under the symbols "VIAC" and "VIACA" after CBS Corporation delisted its shares on the [New York Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange "New York Stock Exchange") (NYSE).[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-51)[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-52)

On November 25, 2019, Viacom and CBS announced the merger would close on December 4 and begin trading on NASDAQ on the next day.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-53)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-mergerdate-4) On December 4, 2019, Bakish confirmed that the ViacomCBS merger had closed.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-54)

On December 10, 2019, days after the merger, Bakish announced that ViacomCBS would look to divest [Black Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Building "CBS Building"), the building that held CBS's headquarters since 1964. He stated, "Black Rock is not an asset we need to own and we believe that money would be put to better use elsewhere."[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-55) On December 20, 2019, ViacomCBS agreed to acquire a 49% minority stake in the film studio [Miramax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miramax "Miramax") from [beIN Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeIN_Media_Group "BeIN Media Group") for $379million. As part of the purchase, [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") reached a long-term deal for exclusive distribution rights to its library, and [first-look](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-look_deal "First-look deal") agreements to co-develop new film and television projects based on Miramax-owned properties.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-56)

On March 2, 2020, executive vice president Dana McClintock announced that he would depart the company after 27 years in CBS Communications.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-57) On March 4, the company announced plans to potentially sell its [Simon & Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_&_Schuster "Simon & Schuster") publishing unit, with Bakish arguing that it lacked a "significant connection for our broader business".[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-58)

On June 19, 2020, Jaime Ondarza, formerly the senior vice president of [Turner Broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Broadcasting "Turner Broadcasting") South Europe and Africa, became the new head of ViacomCBS Networks International for France, Spain, Portugal, Italy, the Middle East, Greece, and Turkey.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-59) On August 4, 2020, ViacomCBS announced that the company's connected video advertising platform, EyeQ, is set to launch in fall 2020.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-60)

On September 14, 2020, ViacomCBS announced an agreement to sell the CBSi-owned [CNET Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNET "CNET") to [Red Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ventures "Red Ventures") for $500million. The deal included the eponymous CNET tech site, as well as [ZDNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDNet "ZDNet"), [GameSpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameSpot "GameSpot"), the _[TV Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Guide "TV Guide")_ digital assets, [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), and [Chowhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowhound "Chowhound").[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-61)[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-62) The deal closed on October 30, 2020.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-63)

On November 17, 2020, various news outlets reported that companies such as [Vivendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivendi "Vivendi"), [Bertelsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertelsmann "Bertelsmann")'s [Penguin Random House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_Random_House "Penguin Random House") and [News Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corp "News Corp")'s [HarperCollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HarperCollins "HarperCollins") had considered acquiring [Simon & Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_&_Schuster "Simon & Schuster") for as much as $1.7billion. ViacomCBS had expected the bids to be placed before November 26.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-64) On November 25, 2020, Penguin Random House agreed to purchase Simon & Schuster for $2.175billion;[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-65) however, the deal was blocked two years later by U.S. federal judge [Florence Y. Pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Y._Pan "Florence Y. Pan").[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-66) On August 16, 2021, ViacomCBS announced that it had agreed to sell the [CBS Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Building "CBS Building") to the real estate investment and management firm Harbor Group International for $760million, leasing the space back under a short-term lease.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-67) On September 28, 2021, ViacomCBS announced that it had agreed to partner with software and data firm [VideoAmp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VideoAmp "VideoAmp").[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-68) On October 28, 2021, ViacomCBS announced that it had agreed to acquire a majority stake in the Spanish-language content producer [TeleColombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeleColombia "TeleColombia") & Estudios TeleMexico.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-69) On November 30, 2021, ViacomCBS announced that it had agreed to sell the [CBS Studio Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Studio_Center "CBS Studio Center") to Hackman Capital Partners and Square Mile Capital Management for $1.85billion.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-70)

On January 5, 2022, _[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_ reported that ViacomCBS and WarnerMedia (whose then-owner AT&T was selling it to [Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc. "Discovery, Inc.") to form [Warner Bros. Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery "Warner Bros. Discovery")) were exploring a possible sale of either a majority stake or all of [The CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW "The CW"), and that [Nexstar Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nexstar_Media_Group "Nexstar Media Group") was considered a leading bidder.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-71) Reports indicated that ViacomCBS and WarnerMedia could include a contractual commitment that would require any new owner to buy new programming from those companies, allowing them to reap some continual revenue through the network.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-72) Then-network president/CEO [Mark Pedowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Pedowitz "Mark Pedowitz") confirmed talks of a potential sale in a memo to CW staffers, but added that "It's too early to speculate what might happen."[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-73)[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-74)

### Rebranding to Paramount

On February 15, 2022, during a presentation to investors, ViacomCBS announced that it would change its name to Paramount Global beginning the following day; in a memo to staff announcing the change, it was stated that the rebranding was intended to leverage the "iconic global name", and would "reflect who we are, what we aspire to be, and all that we stand for". The company primarily does business as simply "Paramount".[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-75) Following the rename, Paramount Pictures' website URL changed from paramount.com to paramountpictures.com, while Paramount took the paramount.com URL.

Nexstar announced on August 15 that it would acquire a 75% majority share in The CW; the remaining 25% would be shared equally by Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-DeadlineConferenceCall-76)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-77) As the deal did not require any regulatory approvals (unlike the ["Big Four"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_(American_television) "Big Three (American television)") networks, which includes CBS, The CW had previously never owned or controlled its own stations outside of its [network service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW_Plus "The CW Plus") for smaller markets), Nexstar immediately took operational control of the network on the same day. The deal was closed on October 3, with CEO Mark Pedowitz stepping down and Dennis Miller taking Pedowitz's role as president of The CW.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-78)[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-79) Paramount's CBS News and Stations unit announced on May 5, 2023, that its eight CW stations would become independent on September 1, per the Nexstar buyout deal.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-80) On August 7, Paramount announced that it had agreed to sell Simon & Schuster to private equity firm [KKR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohlberg_Kravis_Roberts "Kohlberg Kravis Roberts") for $1.6billion in cash.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-81) The sale was completed on October 30.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-82)

### Merger with Skydance Media

Main article: [Merger of Skydance Media and Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Skydance_Media_and_Paramount_Global "Merger of Skydance Media and Paramount Global")

On December 20, 2023, it was reported by [Axios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axios_(website) "Axios (website)") and _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ that [David Zaslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Zaslav "David Zaslav"), CEO of Warner Bros. Discovery, had met with Bob Bakish and had discussed a possible merger. Spokespeople for the two companies stated that the talks were preliminary and may not result in a deal,[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-83)[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-84) while [Fox Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Business "Fox Business") reported via internal sources that Zaslav was "not in deal mode".[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-85)

On January 10, 2024, National Amusements was reported to be considering a deal or merger regarding Paramount Global, with [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media "Skydance Media") considering an all-cash bid of $2.5 billion for the earlier company.[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-86)[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-thrr-87) During this time, Paramount announced it would be laying off 800 employees.[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-88) On February 27, 2024, CNBC reported that Warner Bros. Discovery halted the merger talks with Paramount.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-89)

On April 2, 2024, Paramount and National Amusements approached Skydance for an exclusive acquisition window agreement. Shari Redstone and [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison "David Ellison") sought a three-way transaction between the companies.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-90) On April 18, it was reported that Sony Pictures was interested in acquiring Paramount Global through a joint buyout with Apollo.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-91)[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-92)[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-93)

On April 29, 2024, Bob Bakish stepped down from his role as president and CEO. He was replaced by an office of the CEO, led by [Brian Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Robbins "Brian Robbins"), George Cheeks and [Chris McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_McCarthy_(executive) "Chris McCarthy (executive)").[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-94) _[The Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Los_Angeles_Times "The Los Angeles Times")_ characterized this as an ouster by Redstone due to Bakish's reported opposition of the Skydance deal.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-LAAQQ-95) McCarthy was legally designated the company's "interim principal executive officer" in order to comply with [SEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission "U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission") regulations stipulating that one person must conduct "the normal course of business".[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-96)

On May 2, Sony and Apollo submitted a non-binding offer to Paramount for a $26 billion all-cash offer, with terms unclear at that point.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-97)
Skydance's exclusive negotiation window ended on May 3, 2024 and was not renewed, although the company was still interested in buying Paramount. The following day, Paramount's board members met, considering a "go-shop" approach for other such offers; it was ultimately decided that they would begin negotiations regarding Sony and Apollo's offer while still holding non-exclusive talks with Skydance.[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-98) That same day, Berkshire Hathaway's [Warren Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett "Warren Buffett") stated in an annual meeting that he had sold all of his shares in Paramount at a substantial loss.[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-99) By May 17, Sony and Apollo signed [non-disclosure agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-disclosure_agreements "Non-disclosure agreements") allowing them to investigate Paramount's private financial information, further progressing their bid. However, at that time, the companies were reportedly backing away from their all-cash offer and were re-thinking their approach to a deal for the company's assets.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-100)[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-101)

In late May, Skydance would revise its offer to acquire National Amusements, paying $2.25 billion and stipulating that Paramount's shareholders would receive $4.5 billion in cash, with the company taking $1.5 billion in debt reduction funds, which _The Wall Street Journal_ reported an independent committee representing Paramount had ultimately recommended.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-million-102)[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-johnpaul-103)[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-thrr-87) By June 3, Paramount and Skydance had agreed to terms of a merger. A final deal was expected to be announced in the coming days.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-104) However, at that point, Redstone's National Amusements had not formally approved the deal.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-105) Redstone was reportedly displeased with the revised terms, as she would now received less money for her shares and Skydance wanted Redstone to assume legal liabilities in the case of shareholders lawsuits unhappy with the deal.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-106) She considered a sale of her company to another bidder, with such names as writer and producer [Steven Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Paul "Steven Paul"), businessman [Edgar Bronfman Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Bronfman_Jr. "Edgar Bronfman Jr."), [Bain Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bain_Capital "Bain Capital"), [Patrón Tequila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patr%C3%B3n_Tequila "Patrón Tequila") founder [John Paul DeJoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_DeJoria "John Paul DeJoria") and businessman [Barry Diller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Diller "Barry Diller") in the running.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-107)[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-johnpaul-103) On June 11, National Amusements announced it had failed to reach an agreement with Skydance to acquire Paramount.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-108)

By July 2, 2024, Skydance renegotiated the deal and reached a preliminary agreement to acquire National Amusements and merge with Paramount.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-mergerdeal-8) The deal was referred by National Amusements to Paramount's special committee.[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-109) Also, Paramount reportedly entered talks for a sale of the [BET Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Media_Group "BET Media Group") to buyers led by BET CEO [Scott Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Mills_(businessman) "Scott Mills (businessman)") for $1.6–$1.7 billion.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-110)

On July 7, 2024, Paramount's board approved the deal to merge with Skydance.[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-111) Under the final deal, which has an enterprise value of $28 billion, Ellison would be appointed as chairman and CEO of what is currently being called "New Paramount" and former NBCUniversal CEO [Jeff Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Shell "Jeff Shell") would become company president. Before finalization, Paramount Global would retain a 45-day window to shop for matching or superior offers from other bidders. Redstone will receive $2.4 billion for her share in National Amusements.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-112) If Paramount were to find a better offer, Skydance would be entitled to a $400 million breakup fee payout from the company.[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-113) Skydance's executive team supported the potential sale of several Paramount assets which were deemed "not strategic" to its plans, including BET and others.[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-114)

In August 2024, Paramount announced it would lay off 15% of its U.S. workforce, amounting to about 2,000 employees. The cuts came as a result of a $6 billion write-down on its cable television networks.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-115) This included the shutdown of [Paramount Television Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Television_Studios "Paramount Television Studios").[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-116)[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-117) The following month, Paramount sold Vidcon to UK firm [Informa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informa "Informa").[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-118)

In November 2024, it was reported that Shari Redstone would leave Paramount Global after the company completed its planned merger with Skydance Media.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-redstone-119) As previously announced, the merged entity will see David Ellison, founder of Skydance Media, assume the roles of chairman and CEO, while Jeff Shell, former CEO of [NBCUniversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCUniversal "NBCUniversal"), will become president.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-redstone-119) In December 2024, before merger, Paramount consolidated its television and streaming units into one.[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-120)

In July 2025, the company agreed to pay a $16 million payment to settle a lawsuit filed by President Donald Trump alleging deceptive editing of its October 2024 _[60 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Minutes "60 Minutes")_ interview with his [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") rival, Kamala Harris.[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-121)[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-122)

A few weeks later, on July 15, the _[Late Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_(franchise) "The Late Show (franchise)")_ host, [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert "Stephen Colbert"), called the $16 million payment "a big fat bribe" to Trump. Three days later, Paramount announced the cancellation of _[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")_, although later reporting indicated that Colbert's talent manager had been informed of the cancellation on June 27.[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-123) The Writers Guild of America East and West has called for NY attorney general [Letitia James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letitia_James "Letitia James") to look into wrongdoing at Paramount as a result, specifically investigating whether "The Late Show’s cancelation is a bribe, sacrificing free speech to curry favor with the Trump Administration as the company looks for merger approval".[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-124)

## Company units

For a more comprehensive list, see [list of assets owned by Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_assets_owned_by_Paramount_Global "List of assets owned by Paramount Global") and [list of libraries owned by Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_libraries_owned_by_Paramount_Global "List of libraries owned by Paramount Global").

Paramount Global comprised seven major units split into three business segments:

- **Filmed Entertainment**consisted of the company's film studios as well as the Nickelodeon-branded television and film studios.

  - **Paramount Motion Picture Group** consists of [Paramount Pictures Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures"), the company's namesake division which focuses on theatrical [film production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_company "Production company") and distribution, including film releases under the [Paramount Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Animation "Paramount Animation") and [Paramount Players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Players "Paramount Players") labels in addition to the flagship Paramount Pictures label. Other assets owned by Paramount include [Republic Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Pictures "Republic Pictures")[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-125) and a 49% stake in [Miramax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miramax "Miramax").[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-126) The company also consists of Paramount Studio Group (physical studio and post-production), production facilities and lot, and archives for restoration/preservation for [Paramount Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Home_Entertainment "Paramount Home Entertainment") and [Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Music "Paramount Music").
  - **Nickelodeon Studios** consisted of Nickelodeon's live-action television content production unit as well as [Nickelodeon Movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Movies "Nickelodeon Movies"). The unit is also responsible for [Nickelodeon Animation Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Animation_Studio "Nickelodeon Animation Studio") (including [Avatar Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_Studios "Avatar Studios")) and [AwesomenessTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awesomeness_(company) "Awesomeness (company)").[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-127)
- **[Direct-to-Consumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Streaming "Paramount Streaming")** focuses on the global over-the-top streaming services that encompasses [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+ "Paramount+"), [Pluto TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto_TV "Pluto TV"), [SkyShowtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyShowtime "SkyShowtime") (50% with [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast "Comcast") through [Sky Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Group "Sky Group")), [CBS News 24/7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_24/7 "CBS News 24/7"), [CBS Sports HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports_HQ "CBS Sports HQ") and [BET+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET+ "BET+").
- **TV Media**consisted of the company's linear television networks as well as television content production, outside of Nickelodeon.

  - **[CBS Entertainment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Entertainment_Group "CBS Entertainment Group")** consists of CBS-branded assets including the [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") television network, [CBS News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News "CBS News") and [Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_and_Stations "CBS News and Stations"), [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports "CBS Sports"), [CBS Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Studios "CBS Studios"), [CBS Media Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Media_Ventures "CBS Media Ventures") and [Big Ticket Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ticket_Television "Big Ticket Television"). It also has a 12.5% ownership stake in [The CW Television Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW "The CW"). In addition, the group consists of the [BET Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Media_Group "BET Media Group"), which contains [BET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET "BET"), [BET Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Her "BET Her") and other BET-branded cable television channels.
  - **[Paramount Global Content Distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global_Content_Distribution "Paramount Global Content Distribution")** encompasses as the global international distribution arm that handles global distribution and licensing of productions produced by Paramount's television production companies for international networks and streaming services worldwide.
  - **[Paramount Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks "Paramount Media Networks")** encompasses the pay television channels owned by Paramount in the United States such as [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV"), [Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon "Nickelodeon") (including [Nick Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Jr._Channel "Nick Jr. Channel")), [Paramount+ with Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network) "Showtime (TV network)"), [Comedy Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_Central "Comedy Central"), [TV Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Land "TV Land"), [Paramount Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Network "Paramount Network"), [Logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo_TV "Logo TV"), [CMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMT_(American_TV_channel) "CMT (American TV channel)"), [Pop TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_(American_TV_channel) "Pop (American TV channel)"), [Smithsonian Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Channel "Smithsonian Channel"), [The Movie Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Movie_Channel "The Movie Channel"), [Flix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flix_(TV_network) "Flix (TV network)") and [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1"), as well as controlling its production facilities for the aforementioned channels, including [Nickelodeon Animation Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Animation_Studio "Nickelodeon Animation Studio") and [Showtime/MTV Entertainment Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Entertainment_Studios "MTV Entertainment Studios").
  - **[Paramount International Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_International_Networks "Paramount International Networks")** encompasses international versions of its television channels split into three regional hubs: [United Kingdom and Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Networks_UK_&_Australia "Paramount Networks UK & Australia"), [Europe, Middle East, Africa and Asia (EMEAA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Networks_EMEAA "Paramount Networks EMEAA"), and [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Networks_Americas "Paramount Networks Americas"), as well as region-specific networks (such as [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_(British_TV_channel) "5 (British TV channel)") (formerly Channel 5) in the United Kingdom, [Network 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_10 "Network 10") in Australia, [Telefe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telefe "Telefe") in Argentina and [Chilevisión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilevisi%C3%B3n "Chilevisión") in Chile. The division owned a 30% stake in the [Rainbow S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_S.p.A. "Rainbow S.p.A.") studio in Italy from 2011 to 2023[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-128) and 50% of [Viacom18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom18 "Viacom18") from 2007 to 2024.[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-129) It also co-owns all CBS-branded channels across Europe with [AMC Networks International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Networks_International "AMC Networks International").
  - **Paramount Digital Studios** consists of digital online and video internet properties.[\[130\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-130)
- **Paramount Experiences** focuses on the retailing and licensing of merchandising for Paramount-owned brands. It consists of [Paramount Consumer Products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Consumer_Products "Paramount Consumer Products") and also consists some theme parks.[\[131\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global#cite_note-131)
