MTV

**MTV** (originally an [initialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initialism "Initialism") of **Music Television**) is an American [cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television "Cable television") [channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_channel "Television channel") and the [flagship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship_(broadcasting) "Flagship (broadcasting)") namesake property of the [MTV Entertainment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks#MTV_Entertainment_Group "Paramount Media Networks"), a sub-division of the [Paramount Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks "Paramount Media Networks") division of [Paramount Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Skydance "Paramount Skydance"). Launched on August 1, 1981, the channel originally aired [music videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_video "Music video") and related music entertainment programming guided by television personalities known as [video jockeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_jockey "Video jockey") (VJs).[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-2) MTV soon began establishing its presence overseas, eventually gaining an unprecedented [cult following](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_following "Cult following") and becoming one of the major factors in [cable programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_show "Television show")'s rise to fame, leading American corporations to dominate the television economy in the 1990s.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-:02-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-:1-4)

In the years since its inception, the channel significantly shifted its focus away from music in favor of original [reality programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_television "Reality television") for teenagers and young adults. As of November 2023[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit), MTV is available to approximately 67 million pay television households in the United States, down from its 2011 peak of 99 million households.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-5)

[Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon "Nickelodeon") and MTV into a new public corporation called MTV Networks ( _now_ [Paramount Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks "Paramount Media ## Programming

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Programming")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/MTV_First_Promo.png/250px-MTV_First_Promo.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV_First_Promo.png) The first images shown on MTV were a montage of the [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11 "Apollo 11") [moon landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_landing "Moon landing").

See also: [List of programs broadcast by MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_programs_broadcast_by_MTV "List of programs broadcast by MTV")

As MTV expanded, music videos and VJ-guided programming were no longer the centerpiece of its programming (except MTV Germany). The channel's programming has covered a wide variety of genres and formats aimed at [adolescents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescence "Adolescence") and [young adults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_adult_(psychology) "Young adult (psychology)"). In addition to its original programming, MTV has also aired original and syndicated programs from Paramount-owned siblings and third-party networks.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-mtvweekly-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-21)

MTV is also a producer of films aimed at young adults through its production label, MTV Films, and has aired both its own theatrically released films and original made-for-television movies from MTV Studios in addition to acquired films.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-23)

In 2010, a study by the [Gay and Lesbian Alliance Against Defamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLAAD "GLAAD") found that of 207.5 hours of prime time programming on MTV, 42% included content reflecting the lives of gay, bisexual, and transgender people. This was the highest in the industry and the highest percentage ever.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-24)

In 2018, MTV launched a new production unit under the MTV Studios name focused on producing new versions of MTV's library shows.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-25) It was later consolidated into [MTV Entertainment Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Entertainment_Studios "MTV Entertainment Studios")\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Video Music Awards

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Video Music Awards")\]

Main article: [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards "MTV Video Music Awards")

In 1984, the channel produced its first [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award "MTV Video Music Award") show, or VMAs. The first award show, in 1984, was punctuated by a live performance by Madonna of " [Like a Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Virgin_(song) "Like a Virgin (song)")". The statuettes that are handed out at the Video Music Awards are of the MTV moon-man, the channel's original image from its first broadcast in 1981. As of 2012[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit), the Video Music Awards were MTV's most watched annual event.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-26)

### Special, annual events

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Special, annual events")\]

Further information: [List of MTV special events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_programs_broadcast_by_MTV#Special_events "List of programs broadcast by MTV")

MTV began its annual _[Spring Break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Break "Spring Break")_ coverage in 1986, setting up temporary operations in [Daytona Beach, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_Beach,_Florida "Daytona Beach, Florida"), for a week in March, broadcasting live 8 hours per day. "Spring break is a youth culture event", MTV's vice president [Doug Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Herzog "Doug Herzog") said at the time. "We wanted to be part of it for that reason. It makes good sense for us to come down and go live from the center of it, because obviously the people there are the kinds of people who watch MTV."[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-27)

The channel later expanded its beach-themed events to the summer, dedicating most of each summer season to broadcasting live from a beach house at different locations away from New York City, eventually leading to channel-wide branding throughout the summer in the 1990s and early 2000s such as _Motel California_, _Summer Share_, _Isle of MTV_, _SoCal Summer_, _Summer in the Keys_, and _Shore Thing_. MTV VJs would host blocks of music videos, interview artists and bands, and introduce live performances and other programs from the beach house location each summer.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-28)

MTV also held week-long music events that took over the presentation of the channel. Examples from the 1990s and 2000s include _All Access Week_, a week in the summer dedicated to live concerts and festivals; _Spankin' New Music Week_, a week in the fall dedicated to brand new music videos; and week-long specials that culminated in a particular live event, such as _Wanna be a VJ_ and the _Video Music Awards_.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-29)

At the end of each year, MTV takes advantage of its home location in New York City to broadcast live coverage on New Year's Eve in [Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square "Times Square"). Several live music performances are featured alongside interviews with artists and bands that were influential throughout the year. For many years from the 1980s to the 2000s, the channel upheld a tradition of having a band perform a [cover song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_song "Cover song") at midnight immediately following the beginning of the new year.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-30)

### Live concert broadcasts

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Live concert broadcasts")\]

Throughout its history, MTV has covered global benefit concert series live. For most of July 13, 1985, MTV showed the [Live Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Aid "Live Aid") concerts, held in London and Philadelphia and organized by [Bob Geldof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Geldof "Bob Geldof") and [Midge Ure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midge_Ure "Midge Ure") to raise funds for famine relief in Ethiopia. While the [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") network showed only selected highlights during prime-time, MTV broadcast 16 hours of coverage.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-31)

Along with [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1"), MTV broadcast the [Live 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_8 "Live 8") concerts, a series of concerts set in the [G8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G8 "G8") states and South Africa, on July 2, 2005.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-32) Live 8 preceded the [31st G8 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_G8_summit "31st G8 summit") and the 20th anniversary of Live Aid. MTV drew heavy criticism for its coverage of Live 8. The network cut to commercials, VJ commentary, or other performances during performances. Complaints surfaced on the Internet over MTV interrupting the reunion of [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd").[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Stung-33) In response, MTV president Van Toffler stated that he wanted to broadcast highlights from every venue of Live 8 on MTV and VH1, and clarified that network hosts talked over performances only in transition to commercials, informative segments or other musical performances.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Defends_coverage-34) Toffler acknowledged that "MTV should not have placed such a high priority on showing so many acts, at the expense of airing complete sets by key artists."[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Stung-33) He also blamed the Pink Floyd interruption on a mandatory cable affiliate break.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Defends_coverage-34) MTV averaged 1.4 million viewers for its original July 2 broadcast of Live 8.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Stung-33) Consequently, MTV and VH1 aired five hours of uninterrupted Live 8 coverage on July 9, with each channel airing other blocks of artists.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-35)

## Logo and branding

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Logo and branding")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/MTV_1981_logo_%28all_outlined_except_%22TV%22%29.svg/250px-MTV_1981_logo_%28all_outlined_except_%22TV%22%29.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV_1981_logo_(all_outlined_except_%22TV%22).svg) MTV's first logo, used from August 1, 1981 to May 31, 1994[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/MTV_Logo_1994.svg/250px-MTV_Logo_1994.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV_Logo_1994.svg) MTV's second logo, used from June 1, 1994 to February 7, 2010. It was still used outside United States until July 1, 2011.[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/1/10/Mtvstationid.gif/250px-Mtvstationid.gif)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mtvstationid.gif) One of many MTV [station IDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_ID "Station ID") used during the 1980s, this one was designed by [Henry Selick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Selick "Henry Selick").

MTV's logo was designed in 1981 by [Manhattan Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Design "Manhattan Design") (a collective formed by Frank Olinsky,[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-36) Pat Gorman and Patty Rogoff) under the guidance of original creative director [Fred Seibert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Seibert "Fred Seibert"). The [block letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_letters "Block letters") "M" was sketched by Rogoff, with the scribbled word "TV" spraypainted by Olinksky.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-37) The primary variant of MTV's logo at the time had the "M" in yellow and the "TV" in red. However, unlike most television networks' logos at the time, the logo was constantly branded with different colors, patterns, and images on a variety of station IDs. Examples include 1988's ID "Adam And Eve", where the "M" is an apple and the snake is the "TV". And for 1984's ID "Art History", the logo is shown in different art styles. The only constant aspects of MTV's logo at the time were its general shape and proportions, with everything else being dynamic.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-awn-38)

MTV launched on August 1, 1981, with an extended [network ID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_identification "Station identification") featuring the first landing on the Moon (with still images acquired directly from [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA")), which was a concept of Seibert's executed by [Buzz Potamkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Potamkin "Buzz Potamkin") and [Perpetual Motion Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzzco_Associates "Buzzco Associates").[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-bare_url-39) The ID then cut to the American flag planted on the Moon's surface changed to show the MTV logo on it, which rapidly changed into different colors and patterns several times per second as the network's original guitar-driven jingle was played for the first time. After MTV's launch, the "Moon landing" ID was edited to show only its ending, and was shown at the top of every hour until early 1986, when the ID was scrapped in light of the [Space Shuttle _Challenger_ disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster"). (since then the space theme and the Moonman became a fixture of MTV’s branding most notably in its award show statue) The ID ran "more than 75,000 times each year (48 times each day), at the top and bottom of every hour every day" according to Seibert.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-bare_url-39)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/MTV_Logo_Refresh.png/250px-MTV_Logo_Refresh.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV_Logo_Refresh.png) Comparison of MTV's original 1980s branding and its 2009 branding

From the late 1990s to the early 2000s, MTV updated its on-air appearance at the beginning of every year and each summer, creating a consistent brand across all of its music-related shows. This style of channel-wide branding came to an end as MTV drastically reduced its number of music-related shows in the early to mid-2000s. Around this time, MTV introduced a static and single color [digital on-screen graphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_on-screen_graphic "Digital on-screen graphic") mainly grey during on-air and some color to be shown during all of its programming.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/MTV_Logo_2010.svg/250px-MTV_Logo_2010.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV_Logo_2010.svg) MTV's former logo used on-air from February 8, 2010 to September 8, 2021. It was still used on some MTV programs and YouTube channels as the logo thumbnail on some videos.

Starting with the premiere of the short-lived program _[FNMTV: Friday Night MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FNMTV "FNMTV")_ in 2008, MTV started using an updated and cropped version of its original logo for 30 years during most of its on-air programming. It became MTV's official logo on February 8, 2010, and officially debuted on its website.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-omis-40) The channel's full text "MUSIC TELEVISION" was eliminated,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Music_Television_dropped-1)[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-flavorwire-41) with the revised and chopped down on the logo largely the same as the original logo, but without the initialism, the bottom of the "M" being cropped and the "V" in "TV" no longer branching off.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-flavorwire-41) This change was most likely made to reflect MTV's more prominent focus on reality and comedy programming and less on music-related programming. However, much like the original logo, the new logo was designed to be filled in with a seemingly unlimited variety of images. It is used worldwide, but not everywhere is it used existentially. The new logo was first used on MTV Films logo with the 2010 film _[Jackass 3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackass_3D "Jackass 3D")_. MTV's rebranding was overseen by Popkern.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-42)

On June 25, 2015,[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-43) MTV International rebranded its on-air look with a new [vaporwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaporwave "Vaporwave") and [seapunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seapunk "Seapunk")-inspired graphics package. It included a series of new station IDs featuring 3D renderings of objects and people, much akin to vaporwave and seapunk "aesthetics".[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-45) Many\[ _[like whom?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words "Wikipedia:Avoid weasel words")_\] have derided MTV's choice of rebranding, insisting that the artistic style was centered on denouncing [corporate capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_capitalism "Corporate capitalism") (many aesthetic pieces heavily incorporate corporate logos of the 1970s, 80s and 90s, which coincidentally include MTV's original logo) rather than being embraced by major corporations like MTV. Many have also suggested that MTV made an attempt to be relevant in the modern entertainment world with the rebrand. In addition to this, the rebrand was made on exactly the same day that the social media site [Tumblr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumblr "Tumblr") introduced Tumblr TV, an animated [GIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIF "GIF") viewer which featured branding inspired by MTV's original 1980s on-air look.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-46) Tumblr has been cited as a prominent location of aesthetic art,[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-47) and thus many have suggested MTV and Tumblr "switched identities". The rebrand also incorporated a modified version of MTV's classic "I Want My MTV!" slogan, changed to read "I Am My MTV." _[Vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_(magazine) "Vice (magazine)")_ has suggested that the slogan change represents "the current generation's movement towards self-examination, identity politics and apparent narcissism."[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-48) MTV also introduced MTV Bump, a website that allows [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") and [Vine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine_(service) "Vine (service)") users to submit videos to be aired during commercial breaks, as well as MTV Canvas, an online program where users submit custom IDs to also be aired during commercial breaks.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-49)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/MTV_2021_%28brand_version%29.svg/250px-MTV_2021_%28brand_version%29.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV_2021_(brand_version).svg) Logo since September 9, 2021[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/MTV-2021.svg/250px-MTV-2021.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV-2021.svg) MTV's single color version, used for specific pieces of content

On February 5, 2021, MTV began to use a revised logo in tandem with the 2010 version, doing away with the 3D effect inherited from its predecessors (much akin to the current [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards "MTV Video Music Awards") variant).[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-50) That logo is revealed to be an alternate variant of the current logo designed by the design agency Loyalkaspar, which pays homage to MTV of the past with the red-yellow-blue color combination and the 3D effect mainly inherited from its predecessor logo. The new logo's rollout was completed in time for the 2021 MTV Video Music Awards.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-51)

### "I Want My MTV!"

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: \"I Want My MTV!\"")\]

The channel's iconic "I Want My MTV!" advertising campaign was launched in 1982. It was first developed by [George Lois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lois "George Lois") and was based on a cereal commercial from the 1950s with the slogan " [I Want My Maypo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Want_My_Maypo "I Want My Maypo")!" that Lois adapted unsuccessfully from the original created by animator [John Hubley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hubley "John Hubley").[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Peter_Vidani-52)

Lois's first pitch to the network was roundly rejected when Lois insisted that rock stars like Mick Jagger should be crying when they said the tag line, not unlike his failed 'Maypo' revamp. His associate, and Seibert mentor Dale Pon,[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-53) took over the campaign, both strategically and creatively. Pon was able to get the campaign greenlit when he laughed the tears out of the spots. From then on–with the exception of the closely logos on the first round of commercials–Pon was the primary creative force.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-54)

All the commercials were produced by [Buzz Potamkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Potamkin "Buzz Potamkin") and his new company Buzzco Productions, directed first by [Thomas Schlamme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Schlamme "Thomas Schlamme") and [Alan Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Goodman "Alan Goodman") and eventually by Candy Kugel.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Peter_Vidani-52)

The campaign featured popular artists and celebrities, including [Pete Townshend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Townshend "Pete Townshend"), [Pat Lauper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyndi_Lauper "Cyndi Lauper"), [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna"), [Lionel Richie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Richie "Lionel Richie"), [Ric Ocasek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ric_Ocasek "Ric Ocasek"), [John Mellencamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mellencamp "John Mellencamp"), [Peter ## Influence and controversies

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Influence and controversies")\]

The channel has been a target of criticism by different groups about programming choices, social issues, [political correctness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_correctness "Political correctness"), sensitivity, censorship, and a perceived negative [social influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_influence "Social influence") on young people.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-56) Portions of the content of MTV's programs and productions have come under controversy in the general news media and among [social groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_groups "Social groups") that have taken offense. Some within the music industry criticized what they saw as MTV's homogenization of rock 'n' roll, including the punk band the [Dead Kennedys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Kennedys "Dead Kennedys"), whose song "M.T.V. – Get Off the Air" was released on their 1985 album _[Frankenchrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankenchrist "Frankenchrist")_, just as MTV's influence over the music industry was being solidified.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-57) MTV was also the major influence on the growth of music videos during the 1980s.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-58)

### Breaking the "color barrier"

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Breaking the \"color barrier\"")\]

During MTV's first few years, very few black artists were featured. The select few in MTV's rotation between 1981 and 1984 were [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson"), [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician) "Prince (musician)"), [Eddy Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddy_Grant "Eddy Grant"), [Tina Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Turner "Tina Turner"), [Donna Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Summer "Donna Summer"), [Joan Armatrading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Armatrading "Joan Armatrading"), [Musical Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_Youth "Musical Youth"), [The Specials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Specials "The Specials"), [The Selecter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Selecter "The Selecter"), [Grace Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Jones "Grace Jones"), [John Butcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Butcher "Jon Butcher") and [Herbie Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock "Herbie Hancock"). [Mikey Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikey_Craig "Mikey Craig") of [Culture Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_Club "Culture Club"), [Joe Leeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Leeway "Joe Leeway") of [Thompson Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_Twins "Thompson Twins") and [Tracy Wormworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Wormworth "Tracy Wormworth") of [The Waitresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waitresses "The Waitresses") were also black. The Specials, which included black and white vocalists and musicians, were also the first act with people of color to perform on MTV; their song "Rat Race" was the 58th video on the station's first broadcast day.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-59)

MTV refused other black artists' videos, such as [Rick James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_James "Rick James")' " [Super Freak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Freak "Super Freak")", because they did not fit the channel's carefully selected [album-oriented rock format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Album-oriented_rock "Album-oriented rock") at the time. The exclusion enraged James, who publicly advocated the addition of more black artists to the channel. [David Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie") also questioned MTV's lack of black artists during an on-air interview with VJ [Mark Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Goodman "Mark Goodman") in 1983.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-FINDART1-60) MTV's original head of talent and acquisition, Carolyn B. Baker, who was black, questioned why the definition of music had to be so narrow, as did a few others outside the network. Years later, Baker said, "The party line at MTV was that we weren't playing black music because of the research – but the research was based on ignorance… We were young, we were cutting-edge. We didn't have to be on the cutting edge of racism." Nevertheless, it was Baker who rejected Rick James' "Super Freak" video "because there were half-naked women in it, and it was a piece of crap. As a black woman, I did not want that representing my people as the first black video on MTV."[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-61)

The network's director of music programming, Buzz Brindle, told an interviewer in 2006: "MTV was originally designed to be a rock music channel. It was difficult for MTV to find African American artists whose music fit the channel's format that leaned toward rock at the outset." Writers Craig Marks and Rob Tannenbaum noted that the channel "aired videos by plenty of white artists who didn't play rock." Andrew Goodwin later wrote: "\[MTV\] denied racism, on the grounds that it merely followed the rules of the rock business."[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-62) MTV senior executive vice president [Les Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Garland "Les Garland") complained decades later, "The worst thing was that 'racism' bullshit... there were hardly any videos being made by black artists. Record companies weren't funding them. _They_ never got charged with racism." However, critics of that defence pointed out that record companies were not funding videos for black artists because they knew they would have difficulty persuading MTV to play them.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-63) The book _The Vault: The Definitive Guide to the Musical World of Prince_, which was co-written by the artist, states that while Bob Pittman had defined the channel’s focus as “strictly rock and roll”, the network nevertheless picked up the video to Prince’s “ [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_(Prince_song) "1999 (Prince song)")” on December 16, 1982.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-64)

In celebrating the 40th anniversary of the network's launch in 2021, current MTV Entertainment Group president Chris McCarthy acknowledged that "(o)ne of the bigger mistakes in the early years was not playing enough diverse music... but the nice thing that I've always learned at MTV is we have no problem owning our mistakes, quickly correcting them and trying to do the right thing and always follow where the audience is going."[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-40th-65)

Before 1983, Michael Jackson also struggled for MTV airtime.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-blender-66) To resolve the struggle and finally "break the color barrier", the president of [CBS Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records"), [Walter Yetnikoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Yetnikoff "Walter Yetnikoff"), denounced MTV in a strong, profane statement, threatening to take away its right to play any of the label's music.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-blender-66)[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-67) However, Les Garland, then acquisitions head, said he decided to air Jackson's " [Billie Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Jean "Billie Jean")" video without pressure from CBS,[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-FINDART1-60) a statement later contradicted by CBS head of Business Affairs David Benjamin in [_Vanity Fair_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)").[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-VanityFair-68)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Michael_Jackson_1984.jpg/250px-Michael_Jackson_1984.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Michael_Jackson_1984.jpg) [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson"), whose discography included music videos such as " [Beat It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_It "Beat It")", " [Billie Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Jean "Billie Jean")", and " [Thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson%27s_Thriller_(music_video) "Michael Jackson's Thriller (music video)")"

According to _[The Austin Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Austin_Chronicle "The Austin Chronicle")_, Jackson's video for the song "Billie Jean" was "the video that broke the color barrier, even though the channel itself was responsible for erecting that barrier in the first place."[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-69) But change was not immediate. "Billie Jean" was not added to MTV's "medium rotation" playlist (two to three airings per day) until it reached No. 1 on the [_Billboard_ Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") chart. In the final week of March, it was in "heavy rotation", one week before the MTV debut of Jackson's " [Beat It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_It "Beat It")" video. Prince's " [Little Red Corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Red_Corvette "Little Red Corvette")" joined both videos in heavy rotation at the end of April. At the beginning of June, " [Electric Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_Avenue "Electric Avenue")" by Eddy Grant joined "Billie Jean," which was still in heavy rotation until mid-June. At the end of August, " [She Works Hard for the Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_Works_Hard_for_the_Money "She Works Hard for the Money")" by Donna Summer was in heavy rotation on the channel. Herbie Hancock's " [Rockit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockit_(song) "Rockit (song)")" and [Lionel Richie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Richie "Lionel Richie")'s " [All Night Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Night_Long_(All_Night) "All Night Long (All Night)")" were placed in heavy rotation at the end of October and the beginning of November respectively. In the final week of November, Donna Summer's " [Unconditional Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconditional_Love_(Donna_Summer_song) "Unconditional Love (Donna Summer song)")" was in heavy rotation. When Jackson's [elaborate video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson%27s_Thriller_(music_video) "Michael Jackson's Thriller (music video)") for " [Thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_(song) "Thriller (song)")" was released late that year, raising the bar for what a video could be, the network's support for it was total; subsequently, more pop and R&B videos were played on MTV.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-70)

Following Jackson's and Prince's breakthroughs on MTV, Rick James did several interviews where he brushed off the accomplishment as [tokenism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokenism "Tokenism"), saying in a 1983 interview, in an episode of _[Mike Judge Presents: Tales from the Tour Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Judge_Presents:_Tales_from_the_Tour_Bus "Mike Judge Presents: Tales from the Tour Bus")_ on James, that "any black artist that \[had\] their video played on MTV should pull their \[videos\] off MTV."[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-71)

### Subsequent concepts

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Subsequent concepts")\]

[HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") also had a 30-minute program of music videos called _[Video Jukebox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Jukebox_(TV_series) "Video Jukebox (TV series)")_, that first aired around the time of MTV's launch and lasted until late 1986. Also around this time, HBO, as well as other premium channels such as [Cinemax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinemax "Cinemax"), [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network) "Showtime (TV network)") and [The Movie Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Movie_Channel "The Movie Channel"), occasionally played one or a few music videos between movies.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-72)

[SuperStation WTBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_(American_TV_channel) "TBS (American TV channel)") launched _[Night Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Tracks "Night Tracks")_ on June 3, 1983, with up to 14 hours of music video airplay each late night weekend by 1985. Its most noticeable difference was that black artists that MTV initially ignored received airplay. The program ran until the end of May 1992.

[Playboy TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy_TV "Playboy TV") launched their own music video program called "Playboy's Hot Rocks" that premiered on July 15, 1983, featuring uncensored versions of music videos that were shown in nightclubs by artists from Duran Duran and Mötley Crüe to Nine Inch Nails and 2Pac. At times, they would do a certain theme like the all [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician) "Prince (musician)") theme on the channel back in the 90s.

A few markets also launched music-only channels, including Las Vegas' KRLR-TV (now [KSNV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSNV "KSNV")), which debuted in the summer of 1984 and was branded as "Vusic 21". The first video played on that channel was "Video Killed the Radio Star", following in the footsteps of MTV.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Shortly after TBS began _Night Tracks_, [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") launched a music video program called _[Friday Night Videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_Night_Videos "Friday Night Videos")_, which was considered network television's answer to MTV. Later renamed simply _Friday Night_, the program ran from 1983 to 2002. ABC's contribution to the music video program genre in 1984, _[ABC Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Rocks "ABC Rocks")_, was far less successful, lasting only a year.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-73)

TBS founder [Ted Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Turner "Ted Turner") started the [Cable Music Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_Music_Channel "Cable Music Channel") in 1984, designed to play a broader mix of music videos than MTV's rock format allowed. But after one month as a money-losing venture, Turner sold it to MTV, who redeveloped the channel into VH1.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-74)

The founders of [Financial News Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_News_Network "Financial News Network"), Glenn Taylor and Karen Tyler tried to capitalize on the concept by launching Discovery Music Network, which was set to be a cable network,[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-75) and has plans to set up the Discovery Broadcasting System, which consists of the aforementioned network, along with computer and business networks,[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-76) but it never got off the ground.[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-77)

Shortly after its launch, [Disney Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Channel "Disney Channel") aired a program called _[DTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-TV "D-TV")_, a play on the MTV acronym. The program used music cuts, both from past and upcoming artists. Instead of music videos, the program used clips of various vintage [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney "Disney") cartoons and animated films (from _[Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_White_and_the_Seven_Dwarfs_(1937_film) "Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937 film)")_ to _[The Fox and the Hound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fox_and_the_Hound "The Fox and the Hound")_) to go with the songs. The program aired in multiple formats, sometimes between shows, sometimes as its own program, and other times as one-off specials. The specials tended to air both on the Disney Channel and [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC"). The program aired at several times between 1984 and 1999. In 2009, Disney Channel revived the _DTV_ concept with a new series of short-form segments called _Re-Micks_.

[Hanna-Barbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna-Barbera "Hanna-Barbera") created _[HBTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBTV "HBTV")_, similar to _DTV_ in 1985 and in 1986.

### Censorship

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Censorship")\]

Main article: [Censorship on MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_on_MTV "Censorship on MTV")

MTV has edited a number of music videos to remove nudity, references to drugs,[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-smut_8-78) sex, violence, weapons, racism, [homophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia "Homophobia"), and/or advertising.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-79) Many music videos aired on the channel were either censored, moved to late-night rotation, or banned entirely from the channel.

In the 1980s, parent media watchdog groups such as the [Parents Music Resource Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parents_Music_Resource_Center "Parents Music Resource Center") (PMRC) criticized MTV over certain music videos that were claimed to have explicit imagery of [satanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satanism "Satanism"). As a result, MTV developed a strict policy on refusal to air videos that may depict Satanism or [anti-religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antireligion "Antireligion") themes.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-censorthis-80) This policy led MTV to ban music videos such as " [Jesus Christ Pose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ_Pose "Jesus Christ Pose")" by [Soundgarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundgarden "Soundgarden") in 1991[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-jcp-81) and " [Megalomaniac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalomaniac_(Incubus_song) "Megalomaniac (Incubus song)")" by [Incubus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incubus_(band) "Incubus (band)") in 2004;[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-attack_fcc-82) however, the controversial band [Marilyn Manson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_Manson_(band) "Marilyn Manson (band)") was among the most popular rock bands on MTV during the late 1990s and early 2000s.

On September 28, 2016, on an AfterBuzz TV live stream, [Scout Durwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_Durwood "Scout Durwood") said that MTV had a " [no appropriation policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_appropriation "Cultural appropriation")" that forbid her from wearing her hair in [cornrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornrows "Cornrows") in an episode of _[Mary + Jane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_%2B_Jane "Mary + Jane")_. She said, "I wanted to cornrow my hair, and they were like, 'That's racist.'"[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-83)

### Trademark suit

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Trademark suit")\]

[Magyar Televízió](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyar_Telev%C3%ADzi%C3%B3 "Magyar Televízió"), Hungary's public broadcaster who has a trademark on the initials **MTV**, registered with the Hungarian copyright office, sued the American MTV (Music Television) network for trademark infringement when the Hungarian version of the music channel was launched in 2007.

In 2008 according to the final verdict of the Hungarian Metropolitan Regional Court, although the national public broadcaster and the American entertainment station are identified by the same brand name in the same market, consumers couldn't confuse those TV channels. The two channels appear with the same label in public, but the services of the public broadcaster and the commercial channel cannot be compared either content-wise or stylistically.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-84)[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-85)

### Andrew Dice Clay

### Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show")\]

Main article: [Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVIII_halftime_show_controversy "Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show controversy")

After [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(1952%E2%80%932005) "Viacom (1952–2005)")'s purchase of [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS"), MTV was selected to produce the [Super Bowl XXXV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXV "Super Bowl XXXV") [halftime show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Super_Bowl_halftime_shows "List of Super Bowl halftime shows") in 2001, airing on [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_CBS "NFL on CBS") and featuring [Britney Spears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britney_Spears "Britney Spears"), [NSYNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSYNC "NSYNC"), and [Aerosmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosmith "Aerosmith").[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-92) Due to its success, MTV was invited back to produce another halftime show in 2004; this sparked a nationwide debate and controversy that drastically changed Super Bowl halftime shows, MTV's programming, and radio censorship.

When CBS aired [Super Bowl XXXVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVIII "Super Bowl XXXVIII") in 2004, MTV was again chosen to produce the halftime show, with performances by such artists as [Nelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelly "Nelly"), [P. Diddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Combs "Sean Combs"), [Janet Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Jackson "Janet Jackson"), and [Justin Timberlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Timberlake "Justin Timberlake"). The show became controversial, however, after Timberlake tore off part of Jackson's outfit while performing " [Rock Your Body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Your_Body "Rock Your Body")" with her, revealing her right breast. All involved parties apologized for the incident, and Timberlake referred to the incident as a " [wardrobe malfunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wardrobe_malfunction "Wardrobe malfunction")".[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-cnnfcc-93)

[Michael Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Powell_(attorney) "Michael Powell (attorney)"), then-chairman of the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission "Federal Communications Commission") (FCC), ordered an investigation the day after the broadcast.[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-cnnfcc-93) In the weeks following the halftime show, MTV censored much of its programming. Several music videos, including " [This Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Love_(Maroon_5_song) "This Love (Maroon 5 song)")" and " [I Miss You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Miss_You_(Blink-182_song) "I Miss You (Blink-182 song)")", were edited for sexual content.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-attack_fcc-82) In September 2004, the FCC ruled that the halftime show was indecent and fined CBS $550,000.[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-94) The FCC upheld it in 2006,[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-95) but federal judges reversed the fine in 2008.[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-96)

#### Nipplegate

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Nipplegate")\]

Timberlake and Jackson's controversial event gave way to a "wave of self-censorship on American television unrivaled since the McCarthy era".[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Feeney-97) After the sudden event, names surfaced such as [nipplegate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipplegate "Nipplegate"), Janet moment, and [boobgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boobgate "Boobgate"), and this spread politically, furthering the discussion into the 2004 presidential election surrounding " [moral values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality "Morality")" and "media decency".[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Feeney-97)

### Moral criticism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Moral criticism")\]

In 2005, the [Parents Television Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parents_Television_Council "Parents Television Council") (PTC) released a study titled "MTV Smut Peddlers", which sought to expose excessive sexual, profane, and violent content on the channel, based on MTV's spring break programming from 2004.[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-98) Jeanette Kedas, an MTV network executive, called the PTC report "unfair and inaccurate" and "underestimating young people's intellect and level of sophistication", while [L. Brent Bozell III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Brent_Bozell_III "L. Brent Bozell III"), then-president of the PTC, stated: "the incessant sleaze on MTV presents the most compelling case yet for consumer cable choice", referring to the practice of pay television companies to allow consumers to pay for channels _[à la carte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%80_la_carte "À la carte")_.[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-99)

In April 2008, PTC released _The Rap on Rap_, a study covering hip-hop and R&B music videos rotated on programs _[106 & Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/106_%26_Park "106 & Park")_ and _[Rap City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap_City "Rap City")_, both shown on [BET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET "BET"), and _[Sucker Free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucker_Free "Sucker Free")_ on MTV. PTC urged advertisers to withdraw sponsorship of those programs, whose videos PTC stated targeted children and teenagers containing adult content.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-100)[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-101)

### _Jersey Shore_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Jersey Shore")\]

Main article: [Controversies of Jersey Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversies_of_Jersey_Shore "Controversies of Jersey Shore")

MTV received significant criticism from Italian American organizations for _[Jersey Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_Shore_(TV_series) "Jersey Shore (TV series)")_, which premiered in 2009.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Fox_News-102) The controversy was due in large part to the manner in which MTV marketed the show, as it liberally used the word " [guido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_(slang) "Guido (slang)")" to describe the cast members. The word "guido" is generally regarded as an [ethnic slur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_slurs "List of ethnic slurs") when referring to Italians and Italian Americans. One promotion stated that the show was to follow, "eight of the hottest, tannest, craziest Guidos,"[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-103) while yet another advertisement stated, " _Jersey Shore_ exposes one of the tri-state area's most misunderstood species ... the GUIDO. Yes, they really do exist! Our Guidos and Guidettes will move into the ultimate beach house rental and indulge in everything the [Seaside Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaside_Heights,_New_Jersey "Seaside Heights, New Jersey"), New Jersey scene has to offer."[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-104)

Prior to the series debut, [Unico National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unico_National "Unico National") formally requested that MTV cancel the show.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-105) In a formal letter, the company called the show a "direct, deliberate and disgraceful attack on Italian Americans."[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-106) Unico National President Andre DiMino said, "MTV has festooned the 'bordello-like' house set with Italian flags and red, white and green maps of New Jersey while every other cutaway shot is of Italian signs and symbols. They are blatantly as well as subliminally bashing Italian Americans with every technique possible."[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-107) Around this time, other Italian organizations joined the fight, including the NIAF and the [Order Sons of Italy in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_Sons_of_Italy_in_America "Order Sons of Italy in America").[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-108)[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-109)[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-110)

MTV responded by issuing a press release which stated in part, "The Italian American cast takes pride in their ethnicity. We understand that this show is not intended for every audience and depicts just one aspect of youth culture."[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-Fox_News-102) Following the calls for the show's removal, several sponsors requested that their ads not be aired during the show. These sponsors included [Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell "Dell"), [Domino's Pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino%27s_Pizza "Domino's Pizza"), and [American Family Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Family_Insurance "American Family Insurance").[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-111) Despite the loss of certain advertisers, MTV did not cancel the show. Moreover, the show saw its audience increase from its premiere in 2009, and continued to place as MTV's top-rated programs during _Jersey Shore's_ six-season run, ending in 2012.

## Social activism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Social activism")\]

In addition to its regular programming, MTV has a long history of promoting social, political, and environmental activism in young people.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-112) The channel's vehicles for this activism have been _Choose or Lose_, encompassing political causes and encouraging viewers to vote in elections; _Fight For Your Rights_, encompassing anti-violence and anti-discrimination causes; _think MTV_; and _MTV Act_ and _Power of 12_, the newest umbrellas for MTV's social activism.

### _Choose or Lose_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Choose or Lose")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/a/a2/Chooselose.jpg/250px-Chooselose.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chooselose.jpg) MTV _Choose or Lose_ logo

In 1992, MTV started a pro-democracy campaign called _Choose or Lose_, to encourage over 20 million people to register to vote, and the channel hosted a town hall forum for then-candidate [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton").[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-113)

In recent years, other politically diverse programs on MTV have included _[True Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Life "True Life")_, which documents people's lives and problems, and [MTV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_News "MTV News") specials, which center on very current events in both the music industry and the world. One special show covered the [2004 US presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election "2004 United States presidential election"), airing programs focused on the issues and opinions of young people, including a program where viewers could ask questions of Senator [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry "John Kerry").[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-114) MTV worked with [P. Diddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Combs "Sean Combs")'s " [Citizen Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Change "Citizen Change")" campaign, designed to encourage young people to vote.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-115)

Additionally, MTV aired a documentary covering a trip by the musical group [Sum 41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_41 "Sum 41") to the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo"), documenting the conflict there. The group ended up being caught in the midst of an attack outside of the hotel and was subsequently flown out of the country.[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-116)

The channel also began showing presidential campaign commercials for the first time during the [2008 US presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election").[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-117) This has led to criticism, with [Jonah Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonah_Goldberg "Jonah Goldberg") opining that "MTV serves as the [Democrats'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") main youth outreach program."[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-118)

### _Rock the Vote_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Rock the Vote")\]

MTV is aligned with [Rock the Vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_the_Vote "Rock the Vote"), a campaign to motivate young adults to register and vote.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-119)

### _MTV Act_ and _Power of 12_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: MTV Act and Power of 12")\]

In 2012, MTV launched _MTV Act_ and _Power of 12_, its current social activism campaigns. _MTV Act_ focuses on a wide array of social issues,[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-120) while _Power of 12_ was a replacement for MTV's _Choose or Lose_ and focused on the [2012 US presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election "2012 United States presidential election").[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-121)

### _Elect This_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Elect This")\]

In 2016, MTV continued its pro-democracy campaign with _Elect This_, an issue-oriented look at the [2016 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election "2016 United States presidential election") targeting Millennials. Original content under the "Elect This" umbrella includes "Infographica," short animations summarizing MTV News polls; "Robo-Roundtable," a digital series hosted by animatronic robots; "The Racket," a multi-weekly digital series; and "The Stakes," a weekly political podcast.[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-122)

### _Vote Early Day_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Vote Early Day")\]

In 2020, MTV was the principal founder of [Vote Early Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_Early_Day "Vote Early Day"). Initially, the primary target audience was young voters. The MTV campaign launched with partners across media, consumer brands, and advocacy organizations, and its strength being that it isn't ‘owned’ by any one entity.[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-123)[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-124)[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-125)

## Beyond MTV

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Beyond MTV")\]

Since its launch in 1981, the brand "MTV" has expanded and includes many additional properties beyond the original MTV channel, including an array of sister channels in the US, dozens of affiliated channels all over the world, and an online presence through MTV.com and related websites.

### Sister channels in the United States

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Sister channels in the United States")\]

MTV operates a group of channels under MTV Networks – a name that continues to be used for the individual units of the [Paramount Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks "Paramount Media Networks"), a division of corporate parent [Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global "Paramount Global"). In 1985, MTV saw the introduction of its first regular sister channel, [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1"), which was originally an acronym for "Video Hits One" and was designed to play [adult contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_contemporary "Adult contemporary") music videos, but later switched to different genres such as rap among others and all of the videos currently removed as of 2016. VH1 is aimed at celebrity and pop-culture programming, which includes many reality shows and later shifted to ethnically-diverse viewing in 2013. Another sister channel, [CMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Television "Country Music Television"), targets the [Southern U.S. culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Southern_United_States "Culture of the Southern United States") market.

The advent of satellite television and [digital cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_cable "Digital cable") brought MTV greater channel variety, including its sister channels [MTV2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV2 "MTV2") (currently, all music videos do not show up on the channel) and Spanish-language MTV Tr3́s (now rebranding as [Tr3́s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr3s "Tr3s")), which initially played music videos exclusively but later focused on other programming. MTV also formerly broadcast [MTVU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTVU "MTVU") on campuses at various universities until 2018, when the MTV Networks on Campus division was sold, and the channel remained as a digital cable channel only as of currently. MTV formerly also had MTV Hits and [MTVX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTVX "MTVX") channels until these were converted into [NickMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NickMusic "NickMusic") (under the editorial control of PS' kid-aimed Nickelodeon) and MTV Jams, respectively. MTV Jams was later rebranded as [BET Jams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Jams "BET Jams") in 2015 and BET removed all of the music videos along _106 & Park_.

In January 2006, MTV launched MTV HD, a [1080i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1080i "1080i") [high-definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television "High-definition television") simulcast feed of MTV. Until Viacom's main [master control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_control "Master control") was upgraded in 2013, only the network's original series after 2010 (with some pre-2010 content) are broadcast in high definition, while music videos, despite being among the first television works to convert to high definition presentation in the mid-2000s, were presented in 4:3 [standard definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-definition_television "Standard-definition television"), forcing them into a [windowboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windowbox_(filmmaking) "Windowbox (filmmaking)") type of presentation; since that time, all music videos are presented in HD and are framed to their director's preference. _Jersey Shore_, despite being shot with widescreen HD cameras, was also presented with SD windowboxing (though the 2018 _[Family Vacation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_Shore:_Family_Vacation "Jersey Shore: Family Vacation")_ revival is in full HD). The vast majority of providers carry MTV HD.

MTV Networks also operates [MTV Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Live_(TV_channel) "MTV Live (TV channel)"), a [high-definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television "High-definition television") channel that features original HD music programming and HD versions of music-related programs from MTV, VH1, and CMT. The channel was launched in January 2006 as MHD (Music: High Definition). The channel was officially rebranded as MTV Live on February 1, 2016.[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-palladia-126)

In 2005 and 2006, MTV launched a list of channels for Asian Americans. The first channel was [MTV Desi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Desi "MTV Desi"), launched in July 2005, dedicated towards Indian Americans. Next was [MTV Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Chi "MTV Chi"), in December 2005, which catered to Chinese Americans. The third was [MTV K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_K "MTV K"), launched in June 2006 and targeted toward Korean Americans. Each of these channels featured music videos and shows from MTV's international affiliates as well as original US programming, promos, and packaging. All three of these channels ceased broadcasting on April 30, 2007.

On August 1, 2016, the 35th anniversary of the original MTV's launch, VH1 Classic was rebranded as [MTV Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Classic_(U.S._TV_network) "MTV Classic (U.S. TV network)"). The channel's programming focused on classic music videos and programming (including notable episodes of _MTV Unplugged_ and _[VH1 Storytellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1_Storytellers "VH1 Storytellers")_), but skews more towards the 1980s, 1990s, 2000s and early-mid 2010s. The network aired encores of former MTV series such as _Clone High_ (original) _Beavis and Butt-Head_ and _[Laguna Beach: The Real Orange County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Beach:_The_Real_Orange_County "Laguna Beach: The Real Orange County")_. The network's relaunch included a broadcast of MTV's first hour on the air, which was also simulcast on MTV and online via Facebook live streaming.[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-polygon-mtvclassic-127)[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-rollingstone-mtvclassic-128) MTV Classic only retained three original VH1 Classic programs, which were _[That Metal Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Metal_Show "That Metal Show")_, _[Metal Evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Evolution "Metal Evolution"),_ and _[Behind the Music Remastered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behind_the_Music "Behind the Music")_, although repeats of current and former VH1 programs such as _[Pop-Up Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-Up_Video "Pop-Up Video")_ and _[VH1 Storytellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1_Storytellers "VH1 Storytellers")_ remained on the schedule. However, the rebranded MTV Classic had few viewers and declined quickly to become the least-watched English-language subscription network rated by Nielsen at the end of 2016. At the start of 2017, it was reorganized into an all-video network.[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-129)[\[130\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-130)

### Internet

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Internet")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/96/Mtvcom.png/250px-Mtvcom.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mtvcom.png) MTV.com in 2008

In the late 1980s, before the [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet"), MTV VJ [Adam Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Curry "Adam Curry") began experimenting online. In 1993, he registered the then-unclaimed [domain name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_name "Domain name") " **MTV.com**" with the idea of being MTV's unofficial new voice on the Internet. Although this move was sanctioned by his supervisors at MTV Networks at the time, when Curry left to start his own web-portal design and hosting company, MTV subsequently sued him for the domain name, which led to an out-of-court settlement.[\[131\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-131)

The service hosted at the domain name was originally branded "MTV Online" during MTV's first few years of control over it in the mid-1990s. It served as a counterpart to the [America Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOL "AOL") portal for MTV content, which existed at AOL keyword MTV until approximately the end of the 1990s. After this time, the website became known as simply "MTV.com" and served as the Internet hub for all MTV and [MTV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_News "MTV News") content.

MTV.com experimented with entirely video-based layouts between 2005 and 2007. The experiment began in April 2005 as _MTV Overdrive_, a streaming video service that supplemented the regular MTV.com website.[\[132\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-132) Shortly after the [2006 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_MTV_Video_Music_Awards "2006 MTV Video Music Awards"), which were streamed on MTV.com and heavily used the _MTV Overdrive_ features, MTV introduced a massive change for MTV.com, transforming the entire site into a [Flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Flash "Adobe Flash") video-based entity.[\[133\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-133) Much of users' feedback about the Flash-based site was negative, demonstrating a dissatisfaction with videos that played automatically, commercials that could not be skipped or stopped, and the slower speed of the entire website. The experiment ended in February 2007 as MTV.com reverted to a traditional [HTML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML "HTML")-based website design with embedded video clips, in the style of YouTube and some other video-based websites.[\[134\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-134)

From 2006 to 2007, MTV operated an online channel, [MTV International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_International "MTV International"), targeted to the broad international market. The purpose of the online channel was to air commercial-free music videos as television channels began focusing on shows unrelated to music videos or music-related programming.

The channel responded to the rise of the Internet as the new central place to watch music videos in October 2008 by launching MTV Music (later called [MTV Hive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Hive "MTV Hive")), a website that featured thousands of music videos from MTV and [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1")'s video libraries, dating back to the earliest videos from 1981.

A newly created division of the company, MTV New Media, announced in 2008 that it would produce its own original web series, in an attempt to create a bridge between old and new media.[\[135\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-135) The programming is available to viewers via personal computers, cell phones, [iPods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod "IPod"), and other digital devices.[\[136\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-MTV,_Craig_Brewer_team_up_for_online_drama-136)

In the summer of 2012, MTV launched a music discovery website called the [MTV Artists Platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Artists_Platform "MTV Artists Platform") (also known as Artists.MTV). MTV explained, "While technology has made it way easier for artists to produce and distribute their own music on their own terms, it hasn't made it any simpler to find a way to cut through all the Internet noise and speak directly to all of their potential fans. The summer launch of the platform is an attempt to help music junkies and musicians close the gap by providing a one-stop place where fans can listen to and buy music and purchase concert tickets and merchandise."[\[137\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-artistsmtv-137)

MTV.com remains the official website of MTV, and it expands on the channel's broadcasts by bringing additional content to its viewers. In 2022, it was revised to mostly focus on directing consumers to content on [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount%2B "Paramount+") and [Pluto TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto_TV "Pluto TV"). The site featured an online version of [MTV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_News "MTV News") and podcasts. It has [TV Everywhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Everywhere "TV Everywhere") authenticated streaming. The news site is defunct but still can be accessed with prior movie features, profiles and interviews with recording artists and from MTV's television programs. A related MTV app was available on mobile platforms and connected TV devices.

## Termination of music programming

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=MTV&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Termination of music programming")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/MTV_Music_shutdown.png/250px-MTV_Music_shutdown.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MTV_Music_shutdown.png) A final frame of MTV Music UK before the broadcast ended

Parent company Paramount announced on October 10, 2025, that its United Kingdom and Ireland channels [MTV Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Music_(UK_%26_Ireland) "MTV Music (UK & Ireland)"), [MTV 80s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_80s "MTV 80s"), [MTV 90s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_90s "MTV 90s"), [Club MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_MTV_(UK_%26_Ireland) "Club MTV (UK & Ireland)"), and [MTV Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Live_(international_TV_channel) "MTV Live (international TV channel)") would all stop broadcasting after December 31, 2025. The broadcaster's "flagship" channel [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_(UK_%26_Ireland) "MTV (UK & Ireland)") would remain on air, showing [reality series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_series "Reality series") such as _[Dating Naked UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dating_Naked_UK "Dating Naked UK")_, _[Teen Mom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teen_Mom "Teen Mom")_, and _[Geordie Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geordie_Shore "Geordie Shore")_.[\[138\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-rs-138)[\[139\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-beeb-139) North American channels will remain unaffected but will also be evaluated later.[\[140\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-140)

After going off the air in the UK and Ireland, the aforementioned MTV channels would be switched off in France, Germany, Austria, Poland, Hungary, Australia, and Brazil,[\[141\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-141)[\[142\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-nw-142) in what [Euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews "Euronews") journalist David Mouriquand called "a sign of changing times".

The final song played on MTV in the UK was “Video Killed The Radio Star”, the first song played in 1981, completing the loop.[\[143\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV#cite_note-143)

As of February 2026, MTV only carries music videos in five regions: United States (through a 1 hour weekly block on the main network and several side channels on cable and Pluto TV), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Japan "MTV Japan"), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_(Israeli_TV_channel) "MTV (Israeli TV channel)"), [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Mandarin "MTV Mandarin"), and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_(India) "MTV (India)").
